## Part A — Multiple Choice

1. Under federal law, a broker is best described as someone who:
   a) Physically transports freight for compensation
   b) Arranges or offers to arrange transportation by an authorized motor carrier, for compensation, without being that carrier or its bona fide agent
   c) Owns operating authority for a fleet of trucks
   d) Works exclusively for one shipper

2. A bona fide agent is precluded, under 49 CFR § 371.2(b), from:
   a) Working for more than one carrier under any circumstances
   b) Exercising discretion in allocating traffic between the carrier and others
   c) Receiving compensation from a carrier
   d) Using a load board

3. "Allocating traffic" refers to:
   a) Routing a truck around highway congestion
   b) Any exercise of discretion in deciding which represented motor carrier receives a given load
   c) Scheduling driver rest breaks
   d) Filing a BOC-3 form

4. Representing more than one motor carrier:
   a) Automatically makes a dispatch service a broker
   b) Is legally capped at exactly three carriers
   c) Does not automatically create broker status — it depends on whether allocation of traffic occurs
   d) Is prohibited by 49 U.S.C. § 14916

5. Non-overlapping carrier operations (e.g., one reefer carrier, one flatbed carrier) tend to:
   a) Increase allocation-of-traffic risk
   b) Have no relevance to the broker/bona fide agent analysis
   c) Reduce or eliminate the opportunity for allocation-of-traffic discretion
   d) Automatically require broker authority

6. According to FMCSA guidance, handling freight funds between a shipper and carrier is:
   a) Legally irrelevant to broker status
   b) An absolute, standalone requirement for broker status
   c) A factor that strongly suggests the need for broker authority, but not an essential requirement standing alone
   d) Only relevant for freight forwarders

7. A written agreement titled "Bona Fide Agent Agreement":
   a) Legally guarantees bona fide agent status regardless of conduct
   b) Is irrelevant to the analysis
   c) Can support the relationship but does not control the outcome if actual conduct meets the broker definition
   d) Is required by 49 CFR § 371.2

8. Current federal broker financial-security requirements generally include:
   a) A $10,000 bond only
   b) $75,000 in financial security via a BMC-84 bond or qualifying BMC-85 trust
   c) No financial security requirement
   d) A personal credit check only

12. "Dispatch service" has its own separate federal legal definition, distinct from broker and bona fide agent.

## Part C — Scenario Classification

For each short scenario, classify it as: (A) Consistent with carrier-side agency, (B) Fact-specific / requires more information, or (C) Raises significant brokerage risk.

14. A dispatcher represents one carrier under a two-year ongoing agreement and sources loads exclusively for that carrier from a load board.

15. A dispatch service advertises to shippers: "We'll find the right carrier for any load, anywhere."

16. A dispatch service represents two carriers operating in entirely separate, non-overlapping states, and each carrier only ever receives loads originating in its own territory.

17. A dispatch service represents four overlapping dry-van carriers and personally decides, load by load, which carrier receives each one.
