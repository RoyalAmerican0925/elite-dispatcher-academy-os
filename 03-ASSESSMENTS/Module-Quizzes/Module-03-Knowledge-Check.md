## Part A — Multiple Choice

1. The federal 80,000 lb gross weight baseline on the Interstate System refers to:
   a) The maximum weight of cargo alone
   b) The combined weight of the entire loaded vehicle combination
   c) The maximum weight of the trailer alone
   d) A state-specific, non-federal figure

2. The federal tandem-axle weight limit on the Interstate System is:
   a) 20,000 lb
   b) 34,000 lb
   c) 40,000 lb
   d) 80,000 lb

3. A truck weighing 79,000 lb gross:
   a) Is always legal, since it's under 80,000 lb
   b) Can still have an illegal axle-weight distribution
   c) Cannot legally operate on the Interstate System
   d) Automatically satisfies the Bridge Formula

4. On the National Network, federal rules generally require states to permit commercial vehicle widths up to:
   a) 96 inches
   b) 100 inches
   c) 102 inches
   d) 108 inches

5. Federal vehicle height limits are:
   a) Set at a single nationwide maximum, like width
   b) Primarily state-controlled, with no comparable federal maximum
   c) Always 13'6"
   d) Not regulated at any level of government

   a) Cannot legally be used for multi-day loads
   b) Has no sleeper berth, but HOS regulates hours, not cab type
   c) Is legally required to return to base every night
   d) Is the same thing as a straight truck

7. Which statement best reflects the core equipment-fit rule in this module?
   a) If gross weight is under 80,000 lb, the load is confirmed legal
   b) Nominal trailer size is enough to confirm physical fit

   d) The dispatcher should physically correct axle distribution before movement

8. Power-only equipment means:
   a) The carrier supplies both tractor and trailer
   b) The carrier supplies only the tractor; the trailer comes from another party
   c) The carrier supplies only the trailer
   d) No equipment is required

   a) The same thing as an H endorsement
   b) A combined tank-vehicle-plus-hazardous-materials qualification, required when a driver operates a placarded-hazmat tank vehicle
