## Part A — Multiple Choice

1. A standard load-board posting is best understood as:
   a) A binding contract
   b) A sourcing lead requiring further verification
   c) Proof the load is currently available
   d) A legal offer to arrange transportation

2. Spot freight is best defined as:
   a) Always emergency freight
   b) Freight commonly priced/tendered outside a longer-term contracted arrangement
   c) Freight that never repeats
   d) Freight requiring special handling

3. Origin deadhead refers to:
   a) Empty movement after delivery
   b) Empty movement to the pickup location
   c) Loaded movement to the pickup location
   d) A rate-calculation formula

4. The correct search-radius approach is:
   a) Always 150 miles
   b) Set by federal regulation
   c) A function of carrier policy, market, driver availability, and repositioning plan

5. FCFS means:
   a) The facility is open 24 hours
   b) Arrival order determines service order, within the facility's actual hours
   c) An appointment has been scheduled
   d) The load requires a team driver

6. Posting age should be treated as:
   a) Proof the load is available
   b) Proof the load is unavailable
   c) A priority signal requiring separate availability verification
   d) Irrelevant to sourcing decisions

7. Two similar-looking postings under different company names should be:
   a) Automatically reported as double brokering
   b) Ignored entirely
   c) Flagged and verified before proceeding, escalating unresolved cases as appropriate
   d) Booked with whichever company responds first

8. A dispatcher sourcing shipper-direct freight for their represented carrier crosses into broker territory when they:
   a) Contact a shipper about a specific load for their carrier
   b) Solicit freight generally for an undefined network of carriers
   c) Ask about pickup availability
   d) Request a rate confirmation
