## Risk-Tier Key for Scenario Questions

- **A — Consistent with carrier-side agency:** facts fit the carrier-representation model taught by the academy.
- **B — More facts required / elevated concern:** facts are incomplete or mixed; do not label safe or brokerage from one fact alone.
- **C — Significant brokerage risk:** facts show arranging/allocating transportation in a manner that materially conflicts with the bona fide-agent boundary.

## Part C — Scenario Classification

For Items 11–22 choose **A, B, or C** using the risk-tier key above.

11. River Bend Transport signs a continuing agreement with a dispatcher. The dispatcher searches freight only for River Bend, follows River Bend's lane/equipment instructions, and does not offer shipper freight to other carriers.

12. A dispatch company represents two carriers, one operating only refrigerated freight in the Pacific Northwest and the other operating dry vans only in the Southeast. The company follows separate standing instructions and does not choose between them for the same freight opportunity.

13. A website advertises: "Send us your freight and we'll find the best truck from our carrier network." The business accepts shipper requests before deciding which carrier will haul them.

14. A dispatcher has agreements with four carriers. One shipper load could fit all four. The dispatcher decides which carrier gets it based on which one will produce the highest dispatch fee.

15. A single represented carrier asks its dispatcher to call brokers and locate freight matching that carrier's truck, driver location, and operating preferences.

16. A dispatcher receives freight directly from a shipper for Carrier A. Carrier A declines. The dispatcher then sends the same opportunity to Carrier B without returning the load to the shipper or obtaining a separately defined arrangement.

17. A dispatch company charges Carrier A 6% and Carrier B a flat weekly fee. No other facts are known about how loads are obtained or allocated.

18. A dispatcher works under an agreement titled "Bona Fide Agent Agreement," but routinely accepts shipper loads first and later selects among six carriers to determine who will move each load.

19. A dispatcher for one carrier helps communicate appointment changes on a load that the carrier already booked and does not arrange replacement transportation for the shipper.

20. **CRITICAL-FAILURE ITEM.** A shipper gives a dispatcher five loads. The dispatcher represents eight independent carriers with overlapping equipment and lanes and independently assigns each load to whichever carrier the dispatcher prefers. The dispatcher has no broker authority. Classification:
   a) A — Consistent with carrier-side agency
   b) B — More facts required only; no meaningful concern yet
   c) C — Significant brokerage risk

21. A dispatcher receives a broker's load offer for its represented carrier. The carrier rejects the offer, and the dispatcher tells the broker it cannot cover the load; the dispatcher does not redirect it elsewhere.

22. A company calls itself a dispatch service and receives payment only from carriers, but publicly solicits shipper freight and promises to locate a truck from whichever client carrier is available.
