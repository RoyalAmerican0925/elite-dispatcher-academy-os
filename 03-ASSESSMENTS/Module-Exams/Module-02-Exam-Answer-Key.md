## Answer Key

### Part A — Regulatory Framework

1. **b** — Arranging transportation for compensation between shippers and motor carriers is the core broker-function concept.
2. **b** — Bona fide-agent analysis centers on a continuing carrier relationship and the absence of discretion to allocate traffic among competing carriers.
3. **b** — Labels matter less than actual conduct when the two conflict.
4. **b** — Choosing among competing carriers for the same shipper freight is the classic allocation-of-traffic concern.
5. **b** — "Dispatch service" is not a separate federal category that overrides broker/bona fide-agent rules.
6. **c — $75,000.** Current-source checked before item writing.

### Part B — True / False

7. **False** — There is no federal numerical safe harbor such as "three carriers." Relationship and conduct control.
8. **False** — Percentage compensation is one fact, not a single-factor legal test.
9. **False** — Brokers arrange transportation; they ordinarily do not physically transport the freight themselves.
10. **True** — Tax/contractor classification does not independently determine broker status.

### Part C — Scenario Classification

11. **A** — One carrier, continuing relationship, carrier-specific sourcing, no allocation among others.
12. **A** — Non-overlapping carrier roles substantially reduce allocation discretion on the supplied facts.
13. **C** — General shipper solicitation plus later carrier selection is a significant brokerage-risk pattern.
14. **C** — Choosing among competing represented carriers for the same load based on the dispatcher's own preference/fee is allocation-of-traffic conduct.
15. **A** — Carrier-directed sourcing for one represented carrier fits the academy's carrier-side agency model.
16. **C** — Reassigning shipper freight from one represented carrier to another introduces the prohibited-allocation concern.
17. **B** — Fee structure alone cannot decide status; more facts about sourcing, authority, discretion, and allocation are required.
18. **C** — Actual conduct contradicts the "agent" label; freight is accepted first and allocated later among carriers.
19. **A** — Operational communication for a load already booked by the represented carrier does not itself show brokerage.
20. **c — C, Significant brokerage risk. CRITICAL.** This is a direct allocation-of-traffic pattern among overlapping independent carriers. **Any other answer fails the exam regardless of overall percentage.**
21. **A** — Returning/declining the opportunity to the source without reallocating it avoids the carrier-selection act at issue.
22. **C** — Carrier-paid compensation does not cure general shipper solicitation plus discretionary carrier selection.

### Part D — Sample Reasoning

23. **Expected:** Cancellation/return does not require the dispatcher to decide which other carrier receives the freight. Reassignment requires an allocation decision among carriers, which is the core regulatory concern under the bona fide-agent definition.

24. **Expected:** Contract wording is evidence of the intended relationship, but actual conduct can show the business is arranging transportation or allocating traffic in a manner inconsistent with bona fide agency. Substance controls over labels.

25. **Expected:** Different legitimate and non-legitimate structures can use percentage, flat, weekly, or per-load fees. Compensation must be evaluated together with who solicits freight, who chooses carriers, the relationship with carriers, discretion, and actual conduct.

26. **Expected:** The business must evaluate whether the expanded activity constitutes brokerage rather than carrier-side agency — especially general solicitation, arranging transportation, and discretion to allocate shipper freight among carriers. Appropriate authority/legal review may be required before proceeding.
