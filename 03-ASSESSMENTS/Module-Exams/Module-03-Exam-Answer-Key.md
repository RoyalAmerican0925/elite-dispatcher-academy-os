## Answer Key

### Part A — Federal Weight and Size Figures
1. **b** — 80,000 lb gross baseline.
2. **b** — 20,000 lb single-axle limit.
3. **c** — 34,000 lb tandem-axle limit.
4. **c** — 102-inch National Network width.
5. **c** — 48-foot federal floor for minimum semitrailer length.

### Part B — Equipment Types and Concepts
6. **b** — Day cab has no sleeper berth; HOS regulates hours, not cab type.
7. **b** — Power-only: carrier supplies tractor only, trailer from another party.
8. **b** — Conestoga retraction configurations vary; verify the specific trailer.
9. **b** — GVWR is a specific manufacturer rating concept, not a casual figure.

### Part C — Equipment-Fit Principle
10. **c** — Multiple factors (weight, dimensions, axle distribution, loading method, equipment configuration, legal/route factors) may all matter; no single fact confirms fit.
11. **b** — Reefer trailers generally have less usable interior space than similarly sized dry vans due to insulation.
12. **b** — Fitting on a flatbed deck alone says nothing about legal height, securement, or route feasibility.

### Part D — True / False
13. **True** — Gross weight under 80,000 lb does not guarantee legal axle distribution; an individual axle or tandem group can exceed its own federal limit independently of the gross total.
14. **False** — There is no single nationwide federal maximum height limit applied uniformly; height limits are primarily state-controlled, with variation across jurisdictions.
15. **False** — Federal Hours of Service regulates hours of driving/on-duty time, not a numerical mileage cutoff.

### Part E — Equipment-Fit Classification
16. **BAD FIT** — 81,500 lb gross exceeds the 80,000 lb federal Interstate baseline with no permit indicated; this is not legal as stated.
17. **NEEDS MORE INFORMATION** — Conestoga retraction configuration must be verified before this can be classified as good or bad fit for overhead crane loading.
18. **GOOD FIT (as to width only)** — 101 inches is within the 102-inch width framework on the supplied facts; this does not independently establish complete physical, route, weight, or operational fit.
19. **BAD FIT** — the driver lacks the required H or X endorsement for the stated placarded hazmat load; this is a hard qualification mismatch for the proposed driver.

### Item 20 — CRITICAL-FAILURE ITEM
20. **b** — BAD FIT. 35,100 lb on the drive tandem exceeds the federal 34,000 lb tandem-axle limit, independently of the 79,200 lb gross total being under the 80,000 lb baseline. **A student who selects (a) or (d) has failed this exam regardless of overall percentage score**, per the exam blueprint's critical-failure designation.

### Part F — Sample Reasoning
21. A combination's gross weight is the total of all axle weights combined, but each individual axle or axle group also has its own separate limit. A combination can be under the 80,000 lb gross baseline overall while still having one axle group carrying more than its applicable limit. Gross and axle-group distribution must both be evaluated; passing one does not guarantee the other.

22. This reasoning conflates equipment/manufacturer capability with legal and operational movement requirements. Equipment being physically rated for a weight does not establish legal axle distribution, route legality, dimensional fit, or other applicable requirements. Those factors must be verified separately.

### Part G — Endorsement & Overwidth Decision Control
23. **b** — H is Hazardous Materials, N is Tank Vehicle, and X combines Tank Vehicle and Hazardous Materials qualifications.
24. **c — SPECIALIZED / ESCALATE.** A documented 104-inch load should not be treated as ordinary standard-width freight. The dispatcher should stop and verify the actual equipment, jurisdictions, route, carrier capability, and applicable permit/specialized requirements before booking. Do not assume either that it is automatically illegal everywhere or that a permit will automatically be available.

Passing standard: **85% = 21/24**, **and Item 20 must be answered correctly regardless of the percentage score.** A student who reaches the numerical passing score but misses Item 20 does not pass and must complete axle-weight-distribution remediation before retesting.
