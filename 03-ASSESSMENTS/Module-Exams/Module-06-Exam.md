## Part C — Risk Classification Scenarios

For Items 15–24 choose the best status: **CLEAR**, **VERIFY / HOLD**, **DO NOT BOOK / DO NOT USE**, or **ESCALATE**. Use the least accusatory status justified by the facts.

15. A broker's current authority is active. The company name matches. The contact uses a previously verified corporate number. The rate confirmation details and payment path match established records.

16. A real broker's name and MC number check out, but the incoming email uses a newly registered look-alike domain with one extra letter.

17. A carrier's factor declines a broker because the factor has reached its internal exposure limit. Broker authority is active and no identity mismatch is found. Carrier policy requires factor approval.

18. A rate confirmation lists the correct broker name but a different MC number from the established relationship. No explanation has yet been verified.

19. A known broker contact sends a message from the normal corporate account, but the message suddenly asks for gift-card payment and a new wire destination. The request is inconsistent with every prior transaction.

20. Current FMCSA data shows required broker operating authority is inactive.

21. Two similar postings appear under different brokers. Both companies have active authority, but the relationship between the postings has not been confirmed.

22. A broker's BMC-85 shows a pending cancellation event, but the current effective status and reason have not yet been resolved.

23. A message pressures the dispatcher to skip callback verification because "the shipper will pull the load in five minutes."

24. Three minor inconsistencies appear together: a different phone extension, a changed document layout, and a contact who cannot answer a routine account-verification question. None alone proves fraud.
