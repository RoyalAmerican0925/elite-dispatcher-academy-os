## Part A — Core Mathematics

1. A load pays $1,800 for 600 loaded miles. Its loaded RPM is:
   a) $2.00
   b) $2.50
   c) $3.00
   d) $3.50

2. A truck has 50 miles of origin deadhead, 500 loaded miles, and 25 miles of relevant destination deadhead. Total trip miles equal:
   a) 500
   b) 525
   c) 550
   d) 575

3. A load pays $1,500 and requires 750 total trip miles. Effective RPM is:
   a) $1.50
   b) $2.00
   c) $2.50
   d) $3.00

4. A 700-mile trip in a truck averaging 7 MPG requires approximately:
   a) 70 gallons
   b) 90 gallons
   c) 100 gallons
   d) 120 gallons

5. If the 100 gallons in Item 4 are priced at an illustrative $4.00 per gallon, estimated fuel cost is:
   a) $300
   b) $350
   c) $400
   d) $450

6. A fictional dispatcher agreement states a 6% fee on $2,000 of applicable revenue. The dispatcher fee is:
   a) $60
   b) $100
   c) $120
   d) $160

7. A fictional factoring agreement charges 2.5% on a $1,600 invoice. The estimated fee is:
   a) $25
   b) $40
   c) $50
   d) $64

8. A load has $1,700 applicable revenue and $625 of specifically included variable trip costs. Its estimated contribution margin under those assumptions is:
   a) $625
   b) $975
   c) $1,075
   d) $1,325

## Part B — Concepts and Terminology

9. Which statement about loaded RPM is most accurate?
   a) It automatically includes all deadhead miles
   b) It is sufficient by itself to determine carrier profit
   c) It measures revenue against loaded miles only and can be misleading if deadhead is ignored
   d) It is the same as break-even cost per mile

10. Effective RPM differs from loaded RPM because effective RPM:
    a) Includes total trip miles in the denominator
    b) Excludes deadhead
    c) Automatically deducts fuel
    d) Equals accounting profit per mile

11. Estimated contribution margin, as taught in this module, should be described as:
    a) Net profit
    b) Revenue minus the specifically included variable trip costs
    c) Cash in the bank after all obligations
    d) Revenue before expenses

12. Which item is most appropriately treated as carrier-specific rather than a universal industry figure?
    a) The mathematical definition of loaded RPM
    b) The carrier's actual break-even cost per mile
    c) The fact that 100 cents equal one dollar
    d) The formula for gallons = miles ÷ MPG

13. A national operating-cost average published by an industry research organization should be used:
    a) As the exact break-even figure for every carrier
    b) As general context only, not a substitute for a specific carrier's own costs
    c) As a mandatory minimum rate
    d) As a replacement for carrier bookkeeping

14. Which statement best distinguishes cash flow from profit?
    a) They are always identical
    b) Cash flow concerns timing of money moving in and out; profit depends on recognized revenue and costs
    c) Profit measures only fuel cost
    d) Cash flow is the same as loaded RPM

## Part D — Accessorial and Agreement Discipline

19. A dispatcher sees a delay at a receiver but no detention term has yet been verified. The correct treatment during load evaluation is to:
    a) Add an assumed detention amount to expected revenue
    b) Treat detention as guaranteed after two hours
    c) Verify the applicable written terms and documentation before counting detention revenue

    a) A universal national rule
    b) The applicable rate confirmation/agreements and supporting receipt requirements
    c) The dispatcher's preferred percentage

    a) Only the advertised percentage headline
    b) The actual written terms, fees, timing, and transaction applicability

    a) A guaranteed dollar value

## Part E — Critical-Failure Items

24. **CRITICAL ITEM — Unsupported Revenue.** A fictional rate confirmation says detention may pay after a documented threshold, but the truck has not been detained and no detention amount has been earned. A dispatcher adds projected detention to the load's guaranteed revenue to make the load look better. This is:
    a) Correct because a written detention clause guarantees future detention revenue
    b) Incorrect because a possible accessorial should not be counted as earned revenue before the triggering event and documentation exist
    c) Correct whenever the load's loaded RPM is low
    d) Required for all load comparisons

25. A dispatcher calculates $900 after subtracting only fuel and a dispatcher fee from load revenue. The safest label is:
    a) Net profit
    b) Accounting profit
    c) Estimated contribution margin under the stated included-cost assumptions
    d) Guaranteed take-home pay

26. **CRITICAL ITEM — Profit Mislabeling.** A dispatcher tells a carrier, "This load makes you $1,100 profit," after subtracting only fuel and tolls and without considering driver pay, maintenance, insurance, equipment, taxes, or other costs. The statement is:
    a) Acceptable because fuel is the only meaningful cost
    b) Incorrect and misleading; the calculation must be labeled according to the costs actually included, not as net profit
    c) Correct if the loaded RPM is above $2.00
    d) Correct whenever the broker has active authority

27. A carrier tells the dispatcher its own documented all-in cost per mile is $1.90. A proposed load's effective RPM is $1.75. The best response is to:
    a) Declare bankruptcy is inevitable
    b) Present the comparison and relevant assumptions to the carrier for the final accept/decline decision
    c) Ignore the carrier's own cost figure and use a national average
    d) Call $1.75 profitable because it is above zero

## Part F — Applied Reasoning

28. In 150–250 words, explain the professional sequence a dispatcher should use to compare two competing loads. Your answer must address loaded RPM, relevant deadhead, effective RPM, fuel, known trip-specific costs/fees, accessorial verification, contribution-margin labeling, destination positioning, and the carrier's final decision authority.
