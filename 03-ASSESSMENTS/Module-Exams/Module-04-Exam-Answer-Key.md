5. **c — $750,000.** Current 49 CFR § 387.9 / FMCSA guidance verified before item writing.

### Part B — True / False

9. **True** — SAFER is supplemental and should not be treated as the sole source for every fact.
10. **False** — Cargo insurance requirements vary; it is not federally required at one universal level for every general-property carrier.
11. **True** — DQ/compliance responsibilities remain carrier-owned; the dispatch profile is not a substitute.
12. **False** — UCR is the carrier/entity's own registration responsibility; outsourcing dispatch does not automatically transfer it.

### Part C — Onboarding Scenarios

13. **READY / PROCEED** — No unresolved onboarding mismatch is supplied.

15. **NEEDS MORE INFORMATION / VERIFY** — The naming difference may be legitimate but must be resolved/documented.
16. **STOP / ESCALATE** — Conflicting payment directions from an untrusted channel require independent verification; do not redirect funds.
17. **NEEDS MORE INFORMATION / VERIFY** — Actual availability/status and reliable contact information are operationally necessary.

The exemption applies to covered CDL/CLP holders and motor carriers; it does not convert the dispatcher into the legal compliance authority.
20. **NEEDS MORE INFORMATION / VERIFY** — COI information does not replace a current authority/registration check.
21. **STOP / ESCALATE** — Payment redirection from an unverified channel must be callback-verified through a trusted contact before action.
22. **c — DO NOT DISPATCH. CRITICAL.** A driver lacking the required H/X endorsement cannot be dispatched on the stated placarded hazmat load. **Any other answer fails the exam regardless of overall score.**

### Part D — Sample Reasoning

23. **Expected:** The DQ file is the carrier's broader compliance/qualification record; the dispatch profile contains only the operational facts reasonably needed for dispatch. Some fields may overlap, but purpose, ownership, legal responsibility, and scope are different. The dispatcher should not assume carrier compliance duties merely because operational data is shared.

24. **Expected:** The requesting message itself may be compromised, spoofed, or unauthorized. Verification must use an independently established trusted channel so the questionable communication is not allowed to verify itself.

25. **Expected:** A COI is an insurance-information document. It does not prove the carrier's current operating authority, does not guarantee coverage has not changed, and is not a substitute for checking the current FMCSA status source.

26. **Expected:** The nationwide exemption is temporary and currently expires October 11, 2026. The procedure after that date may change; therefore a post-expiration exam cannot assume the temporary paper-MEC rule remains valid without a new current-source check.
