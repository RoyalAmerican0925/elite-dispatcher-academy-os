### Part D — Sample Reasoning

21. The 80,000 lb figure is a gross combination baseline, not a guarantee of legal axle distribution. A combination can be under 80,000 lb total and still have an individual axle or tandem group over its federal limit (20,000 lb single / 34,000 lb tandem), which is independently illegal regardless of the gross total.

22. Trailer length (53 feet) says nothing about that specific trailer's actual tare weight or the tractor's weight — available payload depends on the applicable gross/axle limits minus the actual operating weight of the specific equipment being used, not a number derived from trailer length alone.

23. Booking a load without complete information risks creating a load that can't legally or physically be completed — a failed pickup, a safety issue, or a legal violation. Recognizing missing information and pausing to get it is what a professional dispatcher does; guessing creates the failures this module is designed to prevent.

24. "Wider than 102 inches" only means the freight exceeds the standard National Network width figure — it does not by itself determine legality. Overwidth freight can potentially move with appropriate permits, routing, and equipment, but this depends entirely on the specific states and jurisdictions involved, and not every overwidth load can obtain a permit. A dispatcher must verify which states/routes are involved, whether those jurisdictions permit the specific overwidth condition, the permit process and lead time, and whether the carrier's equipment and driver are set up for oversize movement — before concluding the load either can or cannot move.

## Instructor Safety Notes

### Do Not Teach As Absolute

- Any fixed trailer payload figure tied to nominal length.
- 80,000 lb as a cargo-only figure rather than combined gross weight.
- Uniform dimensions across all 53-foot trailers.
- Equal reefer/dry-van usable space.
- “Fits on deck = can move” for open-deck equipment.
- A single nationwide height limit.
- Weight-only booking without dimensional confirmation.
- Power-only as carrier-supplies-trailer.
- Hotshot as a self-defining regulatory category.
- Universal dry-van dock-loading.
- Any numerical mileage cutoff for HOS legality.
- “Any hazmat = registration + H endorsement + HMSP.”
- “H and X mean the same thing” — X is the combined tank-vehicle-plus-hazmat qualification, a narrower and distinct requirement from H alone.
- “Anything wider than 102 inches is automatically illegal” — overwidth freight requires verification, not automatic rejection, and is also not automatically permittable.

### Fact-Specific Areas

- Hotshot federal-requirement applicability.
- Food/sanitary transportation requirements.
- Tank/bulk commodity compatibility.
- Livestock transportation requirements.
- Hazmat classification and endorsement/permit applicability (including the H vs. N vs. X distinction — Section 25).

### State-Specific / Configuration-Specific Areas Requiring Verification

- State-permitted semitrailer lengths above the 48-foot federal floor.
- State height limits.
- Conestoga/curtain-side loading configuration.
- Overwidth permit availability, process, and route control (state- and jurisdiction-specific — Scenario S-03-13).

### Legal/Regulatory Distinctions That Must Remain Visible

- Gross weight vs. cargo weight (FACT-M03-001).
- Gross weight legality vs. axle-group legality (FACT-M03-002, Bridge Formula).
- Federal floor vs. state-permitted size vs. common equipment size vs. manufacturer dimension.
- “Available gross-weight margin” vs. “legal payload” — the former does not by itself equal the latter.
- H endorsement vs. N endorsement vs. X endorsement (Section 25) — X requires both N and H together for a placarded-hazmat tank vehicle.

## Verification Status Note

Core federal weight and size figures in this chapter — 80,000 lb gross baseline, 20,000 lb single-axle and 34,000 lb tandem-axle limits under 23 CFR § 658.17; 102-inch National Network width and 48-foot minimum semitrailer length under 23 CFR Part 658 — were independently verified against primary regulatory sources during production. No unresolved factual conflicts were identified. State-specific figures are illustrative and should be reconfirmed if a future revision expands state-by-state detail. The H/N/X endorsement framework (49 CFR § 383.93) and the overwidth-permit framework were added during the 2026-08-27 C-011 QA repair pass and should be reconfirmed at the pre-publication gate along with everything else in this note.
