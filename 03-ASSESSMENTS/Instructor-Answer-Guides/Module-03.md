### Part D — Sample Reasoning

18. The 80,000 lb figure is a gross combination baseline, not a guarantee of legal axle distribution. A combination can be under 80,000 lb total and still have an individual axle or tandem group over its federal limit (20,000 lb single / 34,000 lb tandem), which is independently illegal regardless of the gross total.

19. Trailer length (53 feet) says nothing about that specific trailer's actual tare weight or the tractor's weight — available payload depends on the applicable gross/axle limits minus the actual operating weight of the specific equipment being used, not a number derived from trailer length alone.

20. Booking a load without complete information risks creating a load that can't legally or physically be completed — a failed pickup, a safety issue, or a legal violation. Recognizing missing information and pausing to get it is what a professional dispatcher does; guessing creates the failures this module is designed to prevent.

## Instructor Safety Notes

### Do Not Teach As Absolute

- Any fixed trailer payload figure tied to nominal length.
- 80,000 lb as a cargo-only figure rather than combined gross weight.
- Uniform dimensions across all 53-foot trailers.
- Equal reefer/dry-van usable space.
- “Fits on deck = can move” for open-deck equipment.
- A single nationwide height limit.
- Weight-only booking without dimensional confirmation.
- Power-only as carrier-supplies-trailer.
- Hotshot as a self-defining regulatory category.
- Universal dry-van dock-loading.
- Any numerical mileage cutoff for HOS legality.
- “Any hazmat = registration + H endorsement + HMSP.”

### Fact-Specific Areas

- Hotshot federal-requirement applicability.
- Food/sanitary transportation requirements.
- Tank/bulk commodity compatibility.
- Livestock transportation requirements.
- Hazmat classification and endorsement/permit applicability.

### State-Specific / Configuration-Specific Areas Requiring Verification

- State-permitted semitrailer lengths above the 48-foot federal floor.
- State height limits.
- Conestoga/curtain-side loading configuration.

### Legal/Regulatory Distinctions That Must Remain Visible

- Gross weight vs. cargo weight (FACT-M03-001).
- Gross weight legality vs. axle-group legality (FACT-M03-002, Bridge Formula).
- Federal floor vs. state-permitted size vs. common equipment size vs. manufacturer dimension.
- “Available gross-weight margin” vs. “legal payload” — the former does not by itself equal the latter.

Core federal weight and size figures in this chapter — 80,000 lb gross baseline, 20,000 lb single-axle and 34,000 lb tandem-axle limits under 23 CFR § 658.17; 102-inch National Network width and 48-foot minimum semitrailer length under 23 CFR Part 658 — were independently verified against primary regulatory sources during production.
