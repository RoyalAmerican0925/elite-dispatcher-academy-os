### Part D — Sample Reasoning

18. FOUND means a posting exists and looks potentially relevant; VERIFIED AVAILABLE means the posting party or platform has actually confirmed the freight is still open. The distinction matters because pursuing, pricing, or committing to a load that's already covered wastes time and can damage carrier/broker relationships — see Scenario S-05-12.

19. There is no universal age threshold that makes a posting “definitely gone” — platform refresh and removal behavior varies, and age is only a priority signal (Section 15), not proof of unavailability. The correct response is to verify directly, not assume from age alone.

20. A dispatcher sourcing for one specific represented carrier they have an ongoing relationship with is consistent with the carrier-side agency model. It could become a Module 02 problem if the dispatcher instead solicited freight generally for an undefined network, secured freight before identifying a carrier for it, or allocated shipper freight among multiple competing represented carriers — activity that starts to look like arranging transportation generally rather than sourcing for one carrier.

### Part E

21. **b** — Section 26. Do not teach the rate confirmation as always THE binding contract or as automatically controlling every dispute; teach careful operational review and STOP/VERIFY on material conflict.
22. **b** — Section 30. Entity verification and contact verification are separate questions.
23. **b** — Section 31/32. Payment-redirection requests are a signal to verify through a trusted channel, not to action immediately or to accuse without verification.
24. **b** — Section 28. Factoring approval is a private financial-risk decision; recourse/non-recourse and contract terms vary.
25. **b** — Section 29. Verify driver availability/HOS feasibility rather than concluding automatic violation from mileage and elapsed time alone, or ignoring timing entirely.

### Part F — Sample Reasoning

26. The description confirms the details match, but doesn't confirm the dispatcher checked broker identity, carrier identity, load/reference number, accessorial provisions, deductions/fees, and payment/remittance information where relevant — Section 26's full checklist, not just "does it look right." It also skips confirming the dispatcher actually has the authority to sign under their carrier's Dispatch Agreement/LPOA.

27. A red flag like a look-alike domain or an unexpected contact change has innocent explanations as well as concerning ones — by itself it doesn't establish which. The correct response is STOP → VERIFY through a trusted, previously established channel, and ESCALATE if verification doesn't resolve the concern — not an immediate accusation.

28. Commercial credit information is a private company's assessment of a broker's payment reliability/history. Federal financial-security status (Module 06's territory) is a separate, government-registration-linked fact. A good credit reputation doesn't establish federal compliance, and federal financial security doesn't guarantee a broker pays reliably — treating either as a substitute for the other risks missing what it doesn't actually cover.

## Instructor Safety / Publication Notes

### Current-Date Platform Material
Section 5 — DAT One, Truckstop, Trucker Path/TruckLoads, and 123Loadboard descriptions are current as of August 11, 2026 and were independently verified during production. Reverify before publication if there is meaningful delay; platform features and market position change.

### Platform-Specific Claims
Automated filters, load recommendations, broker credit displays, and booking functionality vary by platform and subscription tier; do not present them as universal. Do not teach that all boards calculate mileage identically, that paid boards universally carry less fraud risk, or that all boards prohibit credential sharing in the same way — platform terms and features vary; current official platform Terms of Service control platform-specific claims.

### Carrier-Policy Items — Not Law
- Search radius.
- Lane restrictions and home-time commitments.

### Legal / Contract-Specific Items
Any platform booking mechanic with its own terms. Rate confirmations: legal effect depends on parties, authority, agreement language, and surrounding facts — teach operational review (Section 26), not categorical contract-law conclusions.

### Module 02 Shipper-Direct Boundary
Section 19 must remain consistent with Module 02's bona-fide-agent/broker framework; do not let this module imply broader shipper-direct latitude than Module 02 establishes.

### Module 05's Own Introductory Verification/Fraud Layer (updated — was previously deferred entirely to Module 06)
As of the C-013 expansion, Module 05 itself teaches introductory entity-vs-contact verification (Section 30) and sourcing-stage fraud red flags (Section 31) — this is NOT the full depth Module 06 provides, but it is no longer accurate to tell students the topic is "entirely Module 06's territory." Sections 16 and 18's original scenarios still flag-and-route rather than resolve; Section 30–31 give students the vocabulary and first-pass judgment to do that flagging correctly. Full identity/authority verification procedure and deeper fraud investigation remain Module 06's territory.

### Module 05's Own Introductory Rate-Screening Layer (updated — was previously deferred entirely to Module 07)
Section 27 teaches introductory rate screening (posted rate, loaded miles, deadhead, "highest rate/RPM ≠ best load") strictly to support candidate screening. Do not expand this into Module 07's full contribution-margin/break-even methodology — that boundary is unchanged.

### Module 11 HOS Boundary
The Search Workflow and Section 29 route HOS-feasibility questions toward verification against the driver's actual dispatch-profile hours rather than answering full HOS mechanics here — that remains Module 11's territory.
