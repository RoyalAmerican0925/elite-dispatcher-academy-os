### Part D — Sample Reasoning

18. FMCSA guidance treats dispatch-service activity as falling under broker or bona fide agent rules depending on actual conduct and scope, not the label used. There is no separate legal category for “dispatch service” that exists outside those two frameworks.

19. Cancellation returns the opportunity to its original source without the dispatcher choosing among carriers. Reassignment requires the dispatcher to decide which of several independent represented carriers receives the freight — that decision-making is the allocation-of-traffic activity the bona fide agent definition prohibits.

20. Not transporting the freight is true of every broker by definition — brokers arrange transportation without physically moving it themselves. “I never touch the freight” describes normal broker behavior; it isn't evidence against broker status.

## Instructor Safety & Current-Date Notes

- Do not teach any numerical carrier limit as a federal safe harbor. The analysis turns on conduct, relationship, and allocation of traffic.
- Do not teach percentage compensation, written contract labels, 1099 status, or freight-money handling as a single-factor legal test.
- Keep statute, regulation, and FMCSA interpretive guidance visibly distinct.
- BMC-85 rules effective January 16, 2026: current FMCSA materials confirm updated trustee eligibility. An ineligible provider can trigger a 30-day replacement window; financial security below $75,000 can trigger a seven-business-day replenishment window after notice.
- Unauthorized brokerage penalty: current 2026 inflation-adjusted maximum is $13,647 per knowing violation under 49 CFR § 1022.4, after the May 5, 2026 revision. Recheck at publication.
