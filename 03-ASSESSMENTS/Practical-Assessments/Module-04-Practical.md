## Scenario — Lone Star Ridge Transport

You are onboarding Lone Star Ridge Transport for dispatch service. The file contains:

- FMCSA current-status check: required carrier authority is active.
- COI: shows non-hazardous for-hire property coverage at $750,000 for a vehicle class at/above 10,001 lb GVWR.
- W-9: lists the LLC owner's tax name while FMCSA registration lists the disregarded LLC trade/legal entity; relationship has not yet been documented.
- Driver A: correct CDL class for assigned equipment; current contact and availability recorded.
- Driver B: otherwise qualified for the truck, but does **not** have the H/X endorsement required for a proposed placarded hazmat load.
- Medical certification: Driver A has a paper MEC issued 35 days ago during the current April 11–October 11, 2026 NRII exemption period; no conflicting disqualification fact is supplied.
- Payment setup: existing Notice of Assignment sends payment to Factor One. A new text from an unverified number says to redirect all future payments to a new bank account immediately.
- Carrier owner asks you to maintain the entire DQ file and administer the company's drug/alcohol compliance program even though your dispatch agreement does not grant or define that role.

## Student Tasks

1. Build a short onboarding decision table with columns: **Item**, **Status (Verified / Needs Verification / Stop / Out of Scope)**, **Reason**, **Next Action**.
2. Classify the W-9/FMCSA name difference appropriately without calling it fraud.
3. Explain what the COI proves and what it does **not** prove.
4. Classify Driver A's current medical-certification evidence under the presently verified NRII exemption.
5. Classify Driver B for the proposed placarded hazmat load.
6. State what to do with the payment-redirection text.
7. Explain why the DQ file/compliance request exceeds the normal dispatch-profile scope unless separately and lawfully assigned.
8. Give a final onboarding recommendation: **Ready**, **Ready with documented qualifications**, **Hold pending verification**, or **Do not dispatch specific driver/load**. More than one status may apply to different parts of the file.

## Critical-Failure Criteria

Any of the following is a critical failure:

- approving Driver B for the placarded hazmat load despite the missing required endorsement;
- redirecting payment based only on the unverified message;
- treating the dispatch profile as automatic authority to take over the carrier's DQ/drug-alcohol compliance program.

## Passing Rule

At least 80% of rubric points **and** no critical failure.
