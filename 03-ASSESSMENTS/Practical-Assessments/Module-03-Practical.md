## Carrier Equipment Profile

Available tractor-trailer combination:

- 53-foot dry van
- 102-inch legal width configuration
- non-temperature-controlled
- current driver has standard dry-van experience
- no H/X hazardous-material endorsement

## Load Candidates

### Load A
Packaged home goods, 31,000 lb, 96 inches wide, standard dock loading, no temperature control, no specialty handling.

### Load B
Frozen food requiring continuous temperature control at -10°F.

### Load C
Rigid industrial machinery 104 inches wide. No oversize permit information is provided.

### Load D
Placarded hazardous material requiring an H or X endorsement. The available driver does not have the required endorsement.

### Load E
Total gross combination weight is projected at 79,400 lb, but the drive tandem is projected at 35,000 lb.

### Load F
A shipper says only: "53-foot van, 44,500 lb." No loading distribution, actual equipment tare weight, dimensions, commodity, or facility/loading details are supplied.

## Student Tasks

For each load:

1. Classify as **GOOD FIT**, **BAD FIT**, **NEEDS MORE INFORMATION**, or **SPECIALIZED / ESCALATE**.
2. Identify the controlling equipment, weight, dimension, endorsement, or information issue.
3. Identify any legal/safety check that remains independent of gross weight alone.
4. State the next operational action.

Then answer:

5. Explain why a load can be below an 80,000 lb gross baseline and still be illegal because of axle distribution.
6. Explain why the label "53-foot dry van" does not by itself prove payload, dimensional, or loading compatibility.

## Critical-Failure Criteria

Either of the following is a critical failure:

- approving **Load D** for dispatch despite the missing required hazardous-material endorsement;
- approving **Load E** solely because gross weight is below 80,000 lb while ignoring the 35,000 lb tandem condition.

## Passing Rule

At least 80% of rubric points **and** no critical failure.
