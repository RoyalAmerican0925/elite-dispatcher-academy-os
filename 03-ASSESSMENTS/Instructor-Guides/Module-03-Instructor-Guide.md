## 1. Employment Outcome

A successful learner can evaluate whether a proposed load is compatible with the actual carrier, driver, tractor/trailer, commodity, dimensions, temperature requirement, weight distribution, endorsement status, and facility/loading conditions supplied — and can distinguish:

- **GOOD FIT** — supplied facts support proceeding to the next booking/verification step;
- **BAD FIT** — a supplied fact creates a clear incompatibility;
- **NEEDS MORE INFORMATION** — a material fact is missing and guessing would be unsafe;
- **SPECIALIZED / ESCALATE** — the load requires specialized compliance, permit, qualification, route, securement, or subject-matter review beyond ordinary dispatcher fit screening.

The instructor must grade the learner's reasoning and next action, not merely the label selected.

## 2. Module Control Principle

The central operating principle is:

**DO NOT BOOK FROM WEIGHT ALONE.**

A load may appear acceptable by gross weight and still fail because of axle/tandem distribution, bridge-formula constraints, dimensions, trailer interior/door dimensions, loading method, temperature control, commodity compatibility, driver endorsement, facility constraints, route restrictions, or missing information.

Likewise, a nominal equipment label such as “53-foot dry van” does not prove usable dimensions, payload, door opening, loading access, or legal fit.

## 4. Source and Rule Taxonomy

Require learners to distinguish:

**FEDERAL BASELINE / STATE OR ROUTE RULE / PERMIT CONDITION / EQUIPMENT SPECIFICATION / SHIPPER REQUIREMENT / CARRIER POLICY / FACILITY REQUIREMENT / ACADEMY CONTROL MODEL**

Do not allow a learner to turn a federal baseline into a universal statement for every road, state, permit, vehicle, or load.

Current federal instructional anchors include:

- 102-inch vehicle-width standard on the National Network, subject to the governing federal framework and exceptions;
- Interstate-system federal weight framework of 80,000 lb gross, 20,000 lb single axle, 34,000 lb tandem axle, plus Federal Bridge Formula requirements and applicable exceptions/grandfather provisions;
- no general federal vehicle-height limit; state/route/permit requirements therefore matter;
- CDL endorsements include N for tank vehicle, H for hazardous materials, and X for the combined tank/hazardous-material authorization; a placarded hazardous-material scenario may require H or X as applicable.

These anchors are instructional starting points, not permission to skip route-, state-, vehicle-, commodity-, or permit-specific verification.

## 5. Lesson Delivery Sequence

### Lesson A — Equipment identification and actual capacity
Teach tractor versus trailer, day cab versus sleeper, straight/box/cargo-van/hotshot configurations, and common trailer families.

Instructor emphasis:
- nominal category is the beginning of fit analysis, not the conclusion;
- actual equipment configuration controls;
- usable dimensions can differ from nominal dimensions;
- door opening and loading access can control fit even when interior dimensions appear adequate.

Diagnostic prompt: “The posting says 53-foot van. What still do you not know?”

A strong answer includes actual usable dimensions, door opening, tare/available payload, loading method, commodity requirements, facility constraints, and any other material supplied/load-specific facts.

### Lesson B — Commodity and equipment fit
Teach dry freight, temperature-controlled freight, open-deck freight, tank/bulk and specialized freight as compatibility families rather than memorized trailer trivia.

Require the learner to answer:
1. What does the commodity require?
2. What can the assigned equipment actually provide?
3. What evidence confirms the match?
4. What remains unknown?

Do not allow “reefer = food” or “flatbed = machinery” to substitute for shipment-specific requirements.

### Lesson C — Temperature-control reasoning
Teach the distinction among shipper-required operating temperature, reefer set point, and product/pulp temperature.

Do not teach a universal pre-cooling rule. The learner must identify and follow the controlling shipment requirement and verify uncertainty.

### Lesson D — Dimensions and loading method
Teach width, height, length, usable interior dimensions, door opening, deck height, freight height, loading orientation, and facility access as separate checks.

Required misconception correction:
- physical fit does not automatically equal legal/route fit;
- legal-width baseline does not answer height, route, permit, or loading questions;
- a 104-inch rigid load cannot be treated as ordinary 102-inch-width freight merely because it fits a particular deck or trailer conceptually.

### Lesson E — Gross, axle, tandem, and bridge-formula reasoning
Teach gross weight and axle-group distribution as separate controls.

Use this sequence:
1. identify actual/estimated tractor and trailer operating weight;
2. identify cargo weight;
3. determine whether the supplied information supports an estimated gross;
4. inspect supplied axle/tandem distribution;
5. identify whether Bridge Formula or another route/jurisdiction control must be checked;
6. hold/verify when distribution or actual weights are missing.

Never teach 80,000 lb as a cargo limit or universal “legal load” test.

Worked instructor contrast:
- 79,400 lb gross with a 35,000 lb tandem is not made acceptable by the gross figure alone.
- a 44,500 lb cargo statement without actual equipment tare/distribution is insufficient to prove legality.

### Lesson F — Driver qualifications and endorsements
Teach recognition, not full CDL administration.

Learner must recognize that a supplied placarded-hazardous-material requirement paired with a driver lacking the required H/X authorization is a dispatch stop, not a paperwork detail to solve after booking.

Do not imply that H/X is the only possible hazardous-material compliance issue. Full hazardous-material compliance is broader than this module.

### Lesson G — Facility and loading constraints
Require learners to check whether the shipper/receiver can actually load/unload the assigned configuration.

Examples:
- dock versus ground loading;
- side/top/rear access;
- crane/forklift needs;
- appointment or site restrictions supplied in the exercise;
- washout/cleanliness or commodity-specific requirements where stated.

### Lesson H — Missing information as a professional outcome
“Needs More Information” is a valid classification when a material fact is genuinely absent.

The learner must identify:
- the exact missing fact;
- why it matters;
- who/source can verify it;
- what action is held pending verification.

Vague answers such as “ask broker for more info” do not meet premium standard.

## 6. Student Tool Facilitation

### Load Fit Verification Checklist
Use in model → guided → independent progression.

Observe whether the learner checks equipment, commodity, temperature, dimensions, weight/axles, driver qualification, loading/facility, and missing facts as an integrated decision rather than isolated boxes.

### STOP-VERIFY-ESCALATE Decision Tool
Teach:
- **STOP** when a supplied fact creates a critical incompatibility or material uncertainty;
- **VERIFY** when a material fact can be resolved through a reliable source;
- **ESCALATE** when specialized authority, permit, compliance, securement, route, or other expertise is required.

The tool is not a legal certification system.

### Weight & Dimension Verification Worksheet
Require actual units and source attribution. Students must not invent tare weight, axle distribution, interior dimensions, or permit status.

## 7. Scenario-Lab Facilitation

For every scenario require the learner to state:
1. proposed classification;
2. equipment fact that matters;
3. commodity/temperature fact that matters;
4. dimension/loading fact that matters;
5. weight/axle fact that matters;
6. driver/endorsement fact that matters;
7. facility/route/specialized fact that matters;
8. material unknowns;
9. verification source;
10. next operational action.

Then change one material fact and require a fresh decision. This prevents answer memorization and tests whether the learner understands controlling facts.

## 8. Professional Language Coaching

Prefer:
- “The supplied facts support a good equipment fit, subject to the remaining booking checks.”
- “Gross is below the federal Interstate baseline, but the supplied tandem figure creates a separate problem.”
- “Permit/route information is missing; hold and verify before treating this as an ordinary legal-width move.”
- “The driver's supplied endorsement profile does not support this placarded-hazmat assignment.”
- “Actual tare/distribution is missing, so legality cannot be established from cargo weight alone.”

Reject:
- “Under 80,000 means legal.”
- “A 53-foot van can haul 45,000.”
- “Flatbed means it fits.”
- “It is only two inches over, so it is fine.”
- “The broker said it fits, so book it.”

## 11. Practical Assessment — Equipment & Freight Fit Desk

The practical uses Loads A–F.

Expected treatment at the controlling-fact level:
- **A:** GOOD FIT on supplied facts, subject to ordinary remaining booking checks.
- **B:** BAD FIT for the supplied non-temperature-controlled equipment.
- **C:** SPECIALIZED / ESCALATE or NEEDS MORE INFORMATION with an explicit oversize/permit/route verification hold; do not approve as ordinary legal-width freight.
- **D:** BAD FIT / DO NOT DISPATCH with the supplied driver because the scenario expressly requires H or X and the driver lacks it.
- **E:** BAD FIT / STOP because the supplied 35,000 lb drive tandem exceeds the ordinary 34,000 lb federal Interstate tandem baseline even though gross is 79,400 lb; route/jurisdiction exceptions must not be invented into the scenario.
- **F:** NEEDS MORE INFORMATION. Cargo weight plus “53-foot van” does not establish actual gross, axle distribution, dimensional, commodity, or facility compatibility.

### Practical scoring
Use the active 18-point rubric:
1. equipment/freight classifications — 0–3;
2. weight and axle reasoning — 0–3 **CRITICAL**;
3. endorsement/specialized-load reasoning — 0–3 **CRITICAL**;
4. missing-information/verification discipline — 0–3;
5. next operational action — 0–3;
6. nominal-trailer-label explanation — 0–3.

**Pass: 15/18 and no critical failure.**

### Critical failures
The learner fails regardless of point total if the learner:
1. approves Load D despite the expressly missing required H/X endorsement; or
2. approves Load E solely because gross is below 80,000 lb while ignoring the supplied 35,000 lb tandem.

Do not invent additional hidden critical failures during grading.

## 12. Practical Debrief and Remediation

Debrief only after scoring.

For each failed competency record:
- failed dimension;
- reasoning error;
- manual/tool section assigned;
- remediation exercise;
- alternate fact pattern;
- corrected evidence;
- retest result.

Required remediation patterns:

| Deficiency | Remediation |
|---|---|
| Gross-weight-only reasoning | Alternate scenario where gross is below 80,000 but an axle/tandem/bridge constraint controls. |
| Axle/tandem confusion | Weight & Dimension Worksheet with supplied axle groups and explicit source check. |
| Equipment-label overconfidence | Compare two same-nominal-size trailers with different usable dimensions/tare/loading access. |
| Dimension/permit confusion | Alternate overwidth or height scenario requiring route/state/permit verification. |
| Endorsement failure | Alternate driver/load pairing requiring the learner to identify the required authorization before dispatch. |
| Missing-information guessing | Scenario with deliberately withheld tare, dimensions, commodity, or facility facts; learner must hold and name the verification source. |
| Specialized-load overconfidence | Route learner to STOP-VERIFY-ESCALATE and require a professional escalation statement. |

Retest with a materially different scenario. Do not reveal the answer and repeat the same scenario.
