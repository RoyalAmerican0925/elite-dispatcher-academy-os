## B. Employment Outcome and Competency Map

**Observable occupational outcome:** the student can evaluate whether a specific load can safely and legally move with specific equipment, using a multi-factor check (equipment, commodity, temperature, dimensions, weight/axles, driver qualifications) — never from a single fact alone.

**Competency IDs:**
- **classify** a load as GOOD FIT, BAD FIT, NEEDS MORE INFORMATION, or SPECIALIZED/ESCALATE
- **distinguish** nominal equipment size/type from actual usable dimensions and current tare weight
- **calculate** gross weight legality independently from axle-group legality
- **identify** required driver endorsements (H/N/X) and distinguish them from each other
- **recognize** overwidth/oversize freight as requiring verification, not an automatic conclusion in either direction
- **verify** rather than assume when a load file is incomplete

## D. Lesson-by-Lesson Teaching Notes

**Equipment fundamentals:**
- *Essential point:* nominal trailer size ("53-foot dry van") is a starting point, not a guarantee of exact usable dimensions or current tare weight.
- *Misconception to watch for:* students assume all 53-foot dry vans have identical payload. Correct this early — it undermines every later weight calculation if left uncorrected.

**Load A (straightforward fit):**
- *Purpose:* establish what a clean GOOD FIT actually looks like before introducing traps.
- *Demonstration:* walk the full multi-factor check even though every factor passes — the discipline matters more than the difficulty here.

**Load B (temperature mismatch):**
- *Essential point:* dry van does not equal reefer capability, full stop — not a workaround situation.
- *Misconception to watch for:* students propose "tricks" (running short distances, partial loads) to make a dry van handle frozen freight. Shut this down directly — it's not a judgment call, it's a hard equipment mismatch.

**Load C (overwidth, 104 inches):**
- *Essential point:* exceeding 102 inches is neither automatically illegal nor automatically permittable — both are wrong answers.
- *Misconception to watch for (the second-most dangerous misconception in this module):* students want a single bright-line answer. Resist giving one — the correct answer is "verify state/route/permit/equipment," and a student who skips straight to a confident yes/no has missed the lesson regardless of which direction they guessed.

**Load D (hazmat endorsement gap):**
- *Essential point:* a valid CDL does not substitute for a specific required H/N/X endorsement. This is a hard qualification mismatch, not a judgment call — treat it with the same seriousness as a critical failure (see Section H).

**Load E (the weight/axle trap — the module's signature lesson):**
- *Essential point:* gross weight under 80,000 lb does NOT cure an independently excessive axle-group condition.
- *Misconception to watch for:* students see "79,400 lb" and stop checking — the number under 80,000 feels like confirmation. This is the exact trap the load is built to expose. Do not let students skip the axle-distribution sheet.
- *Demonstration:* reveal gross weight first, ask for a preliminary classification, then reveal axle distribution and ask students to revise — the moment of revision is the actual teaching point, more valuable than getting it right on the first pass.

**Load F (incomplete file):**
- *Essential point:* "53' van, 44,500 lb" tells you almost nothing usable. Reinforces STOP → VERIFY → DO NOT ASSUME.

## E. Professional-Language Coaching

| Correct term | Common misuse | Consequence | Acceptable phrasing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross combination weight | "The weight" (ambiguous) | Conflates cargo weight with total legal weight, breaking every downstream calculation | "Gross combination weight is 79,400 lb — that's tractor, trailer, and cargo together." |
| Axle-group legality | "It's under 80,000, so it's fine" | The single most consequential misuse in this module — masks a real safety/legal violation | "Gross is legal, but I still need to check axle distribution separately." |
| H/N/X endorsement | "Hazmat endorsement" (used generically for all three) | Obscures which specific qualification a load actually requires | "This load requires X specifically — the driver holds H only, which doesn't cover the tank-vehicle requirement." |

**Language to correct on sight:** "104 inches is illegal" or "104 inches is fine with a permit" stated as settled fact — both overstate what the file actually establishes.

## F. Activity Facilitation

**Weight & Dimension Verification Worksheet:**
- *Purpose:* practice separating gross-weight and axle-group checks as genuinely independent calculations.
- *Predictable error:* students perform only the gross-weight check and treat the worksheet as complete.
- *Debrief question:* "What does a legal gross weight tell you, and what does it not tell you?"

**STOP-VERIFY-ESCALATE Decision Tool:**
- *Purpose:* structured response to Load C (overwidth) and Load F (incomplete) patterns.
- *Scored:* No — formative practice ahead of the Practical.

## H. Critical-Failure Controls

Two critical-failure patterns, matching both the Practical's own critical-failure criteria and SIM-003's two critical-escalation decisions exactly — do not manufacture a third:

1. **Dispatching Load D despite the missing required H/N/X qualification.** Critical because it's a direct safety/legal violation, not a difficult judgment call.
2. **Approving Load E because gross weight is under 80,000 lb, without checking axle distribution.** Critical because it represents the exact trap the module is built to teach students to avoid — a student who falls for it has missed the module's central lesson, not just one question.
