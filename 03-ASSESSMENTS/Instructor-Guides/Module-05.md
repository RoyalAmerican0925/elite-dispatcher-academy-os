## B. Employment Outcome and Competency Map

**Observable occupational outcome:** the student can run a disciplined live load search — building a search profile, screening candidates, distinguishing FOUND from VERIFIED AVAILABLE, catching red flags without unsupported accusation, reviewing rate confirmations for discrepancies, and correctly routing anything that exceeds ordinary sourcing judgment (including the Module 02 allocation-of-traffic boundary resurfacing in a live sourcing context).

**Competency IDs:**
- **construct** a search profile and appropriate search parameters from a carrier/driver/equipment file
- **distinguish** FOUND from VERIFIED AVAILABLE
- **screen** candidates against equipment/commodity/qualification requirements
- **generate** specific verification questions from identified missing information
- **verify** entity legitimacy separately from individual contact authenticity
- **respond** to red flags (duplicate postings, factor declines) without unsupported accusation
- **catch** material rate-confirmation discrepancies before signing
- **locate** actual signing authority rather than assuming it from document review
- **verify** HOS/timing feasibility rather than calculating it from distance alone
- **escalate** a shipper-first allocation request using the Module 02 framework
- **re-evaluate** an already-confirmed candidate when new material information arrives
- **identify** oneself truthfully even while verifying another party

## D. Lesson-by-Lesson Teaching Notes

**Search fundamentals (Part 07, §26–27):**
- *Essential point:* search parameters follow the specific carrier's actual stated preferences and conditions — there is no universal correct search radius.
- *Misconception to watch for:* students want a fixed number ("always 150 miles") rather than reasoning from the carrier's actual file.

**FOUND vs. VERIFIED AVAILABLE:**
- *Essential point:* a posting matching the search profile is FOUND; only an independent confirmation (typically a phone call) makes it VERIFIED AVAILABLE.
- *Misconception to watch for (the module's most common error):* students treat a matching, recent-looking posting as already available. Posting age is a priority signal, not proof either way.
- *Demonstration:* contrast a phone-confirmed candidate against a merely-posted one side by side.

**Missing-information discipline:**
- *Essential point:* an incomplete posting requires specific verification questions, not an assumption in either direction (not automatically rejected, not automatically fine).
- *Workplace application:* have students convert missing fields directly into the actual questions they'd ask a broker — not a restatement of what's missing.

**Entity vs. contact verification:**
- *Essential point:* a verified brokerage entity does NOT automatically authenticate a new, unfamiliar individual contact. These are two separate checks.
- *Misconception to watch for:* students conflate "the company is real" with "this specific person is who they say they are."

**Red flags and fraud-adjacent patterns:**
- *Essential point:* duplicate-looking postings and factor declines are verification triggers, not proof of anything. Do NOT teach an oversimplified "double-brokering rule" from a single ambiguous fact — this was explicitly corrected during this module's own regulatory QA pass.
- *Misconception to watch for:* students want to declare fraud confidently from one data point. Redirect every time toward "this needs verification," not "this proves X."

**Rate confirmation review and signing authority:**
- *Essential point:* two separate failure modes — signing past a real discrepancy, and assuming signing authority merely from reviewing the document. Treat both as equally serious (see Section H).

**Shipper-first allocation (direct Module 02 callback):**
- *Essential point:* a shipper asking the dispatcher to choose among represented carriers is the exact allocation-of-traffic pattern from Module 02, now appearing in a live sourcing context. Students who passed Module 02 should recognize this immediately — if they don't, that's a genuine regression worth flagging, not just a new-content miss.

**Truthful role identification:**
- *Essential point:* verifying someone else's identity does not suspend the dispatcher's own obligation to identify themselves accurately — including the carrier they represent.

## E. Professional-Language Coaching

| Correct term | Common misuse | Consequence | Acceptable phrasing |
|---|---|---|---|
| VERIFIED AVAILABLE | "Found it, so it's mine" | Pursuing freight that may already be covered, wasting time and credibility with the broker | "This is FOUND — I still need to confirm availability before treating it as booked." |
| Entity vs. contact verification | "The company's legit, so we're good" | Skips the actual authentication step for the specific person on the line | "The brokerage checks out, but I haven't verified this specific contact yet." |
| Red flag | "This is fraud" (stated as proven fact) | Unsupported accusation, real reputational/legal exposure | "This is a red flag — worth verifying before proceeding." |

**Language to correct on sight:** any confident fraud accusation built on a single ambiguous signal (duplicate posting, factor decline) — redirect to verification language every time.

## H. Critical-Failure Controls

Six critical-failure patterns, matching SIM-005's six critical-escalation decisions exactly:

1. **Treating a missing required endorsement as curable after booking.**
2. **Declaring duplicate postings proven fraud without verification.**
3. **Letting a verified brokerage entity authenticate an unverified new individual contact.**
4. **Signing past a material rate-confirmation discrepancy.**
5. **Assuming signing authority merely from reviewing the document.**
6. **Treating a shipper-first allocation request as ordinary sourcing** (the Module 02 boundary reappearing).

All six carry the same weight — do not treat any one as more or less serious than the others; this module deliberately does not single out one canonical failure.
