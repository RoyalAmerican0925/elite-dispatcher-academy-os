## 1. Employment Outcome and Competency Standard

The learner must be able to operate on the carrier side without relying on job titles, contract labels, payment method, carrier count, tax status, or assumptions to determine legal boundaries. The learner identifies the represented principal, traces who supplied the freight opportunity, identifies who controls carrier selection, recognizes discretionary allocation of traffic, distinguishes cancellation from reassignment, preserves material uncertainty, chooses an operational response, documents the decision, and escalates when facts or authority are insufficient.

The module is not successful if the learner merely memorizes that a dispatcher is “not a broker.” Actual conduct and relationship facts control the operational analysis.

By closeout, the learner must demonstrate the following competency chain:

1. identify represented principal and relationship;
2. trace freight source;
3. distinguish verified facts from unknowns;
4. analyze carrier-selection/allocation discretion;
5. classify from the complete fact pattern;
6. identify what changed fact could change the classification;
7. select a defensible operational action;
8. document a professional case note;
9. make a professional escalation handoff when required;
10. reject false categorical safe harbors.

## 2. Prerequisite Transfer

Reactivate Module 01 role and freight-flow knowledge. Require the learner to identify motor carrier, broker, shipper, driver, dispatcher, freight source, represented principal, and handoff point before analyzing a boundary problem.

Do not reteach Module 01. Require transfer of those concepts into a legal-boundary decision.

## 3. Core Operating Model

Teach every boundary problem through:

**STOP → IDENTIFY → TRACE → CHECK → CLASSIFY → ACT → DOCUMENT → ESCALATE**

### STOP
Pause before accepting, assigning, redirecting, promising coverage, or relying on a title.

### IDENTIFY
Determine the represented principal, continuing relationship, and actual authority/scope.

### TRACE
Follow the freight opportunity from its original source and determine whether a specific represented carrier was already tied to it. Distinguish cancellation/return from redirection/reassignment.

### CHECK
Separate verified facts from assumptions. Check carrier-selection discretion, overlapping carriers, compensation/fund flow, shipper solicitation, agreements versus actual conduct, and material unknowns.

### CLASSIFY
Use one of the instructional classifications:
- **Consistent with carrier-side agency**
- **Fact-specific / insufficient verified information**
- **Raises significant brokerage risk**

Require controlling facts. A checkbox without reasoning is incomplete.

### ACT
Require an operational next step:
- **PROCEED**
- **HOLD**
- **VERIFY**
- **ESCALATE**
- **DO NOT PROCEED AS PROPOSED**

“Proceed” is an operational decision supported by verified facts, not a legal certification.

### DOCUMENT
Require a case note containing situation, principal, freight source, verified facts, material unknowns, reasoning, action, verification/follow-up, and next owner.

### ESCALATE
Require a professional handoff that states known facts, unresolved question, held/risk activity, evidence provided, and the decision requiring qualified review.

This eight-stage chain is an **ACADEMY CONTROL MODEL**. Do not present it as statutory text, a federal legal test, or a legal safe harbor.

## 5. Lesson-by-Lesson Delivery Notes

### A. Titles do not decide status
Demonstrate two fictional businesses using the same title “dispatch service” but performing materially different functions. Ask first for the conduct facts, then the classification. Correct students who begin with the title.

**Instructor diagnostic:** If a learner says “they are safe because they call themselves a dispatcher,” require the learner to identify principal, freight source, selection discretion, and actual conduct before continuing.

### B. Principal and agency
A learner should identify who the dispatcher represents and what authority actually exists. Possession of a contract does not expand authority beyond its terms or override applicable law.

**Demonstration:** Present a carrier agreement plus a shipper-direct request that goes beyond the described carrier-side work. Ask which facts come from the agreement and which come from actual conduct.

### C. Broker and bona fide-agent concepts
Teach definitions and guidance as decision inputs rather than slogans. The student must distinguish a carrier-side agency relationship from activity involving broader arrangement of transportation or discretionary allocation among carriers.

**Coaching control:** Require phrases such as “on the supplied facts” or “the current facts raise...” instead of unsupported declarations that a person or company is definitely legal/illegal.

### D. TRACE — Freight-source discipline
This is now a required explicit skill. Give students an opportunity and ask them to draw:

**SOURCE → DISPATCHER/CHANNEL → REPRESENTED CARRIER → PROPOSED DISPOSITION**

If the original carrier cannot perform, require the student to state whether the opportunity returns to its source or is being redirected to another carrier.

### E. Allocation of traffic
Make the learner determine whether several represented carriers could reasonably haul the same freight and, if so, who exercises selection discretion.

**Do not accept:** “There are only two carriers, so it is fine.”

**Acceptable reasoning:** “Carrier count alone does not decide the issue. I need to determine whether the dispatcher is exercising discretion in allocating this opportunity among overlapping carriers.”

### F. Cancellation versus reassignment
A cancellation/return-to-source event and unilateral reassignment to another represented carrier are not operationally equivalent. Require a side-by-side trace and ask exactly where carrier-selection discretion enters.

### G. Compensation, tax, contract, and freight-money myths
Percentage fees, flat fees, subscription fees, invoice structure, tax classification, contract labels, and money/document handling can be relevant evidence but do not independently resolve the classification.

Run a **single-factor challenge**: give the learner one tempting fact, then ask what additional facts still must be checked.

### H. Truthful role representation
Students must describe their role accurately to carriers, brokers, and shippers. They may not use “agent,” “broker,” “carrier representative,” or another title to imply authority they do not possess.

### I. Known versus unknown facts
Give a scenario with one material fact removed. Require the learner to write **UNKNOWN**, identify who/what can verify it, and choose HOLD or VERIFY when appropriate.

“More facts required” is a valid professional result when the missing fact is material. Penalize unsupported guessing, not justified uncertainty.

### J. Classification and fact-change reasoning
After each classification, ask:

**“What fact mattered most?”**  
**“What fact, if different, could change your classification?”**

This prevents pattern memorization and tests whether the learner understands causation in the decision model.

### K. Operational action
Do not allow the exercise to end with “this is risky.” Require the student to choose what happens next: PROCEED, HOLD, VERIFY, ESCALATE, or DO NOT PROCEED AS PROPOSED.

Require the learner to identify the responsible person and deadline/trigger when follow-up is necessary.

### L. Documentation and escalation
Require a professional case note and, when needed, an escalation handoff. The note should let another dispatcher or supervisor understand the situation without reconstructing it from memory.

Do not reward legal-sounding language. Reward accurate facts, restrained classification language, operational action, ownership, and unresolved-question control.

### M. Multi-factor boundary drill
Give a fact pattern containing at least four distracting facts—for example a written dispatch agreement, percentage-based fee, two represented carriers, and no direct handling of shipper money. Require the learner to explain why none independently resolves the boundary question and identify the conduct facts that actually require analysis.

### N. Operational consequence drill
Require the learner to explain the consequence of a bad boundary decision in operational terms: unauthorized commitment, improper reassignment, misrepresented role, carrier relationship problem, or matter requiring regulatory/legal review. Do not use fear-based teaching or unsupported legal conclusions.

## 6. Student Decision Tool Facilitation

Use `Module-02-Decision-Tool-Worksheet.md` on fresh examples before the graded practical.

### Instructor observation checklist

Watch whether the learner:

- STOPs before acting when facts are incomplete;
- IDENTIFYs the represented carrier/principal;
- TRACEs the freight opportunity to its source;
- CHECKs evidence rather than assumptions;
- identifies overlapping carrier choices and selection discretion;
- uses insufficient information when justified;
- CLASSIFYs using controlling facts;
- identifies a fact that could change the decision;
- ACTs rather than merely naming a risk;
- DOCUMENTs a usable case note;
- ESCALATEs with a specific question and supporting facts.

A correct classification with unsupported reasoning is not full competency evidence.

### Guided-practice progression

1. Instructor models one complete worksheet aloud.
2. Instructor and learner complete one together, with instructor questioning rather than supplying conclusions.
3. Learner completes one independently.
4. Instructor debriefs the decision chain, not merely the final classification.
5. Learner proceeds to the practical only when the worksheet can be completed without instructor prompting on the core steps.

## 7. Scenario Lab Facilitation

The eight Student Manual Part 03 scenarios are demonstrations/practice, not the graded practical.

For each scenario, require students to answer:

1. Who is the principal?
2. Where did the freight come from?
3. What facts are verified?
4. What material fact, if any, remains unknown?
5. Is there meaningful carrier-selection discretion?
6. What classification is supported?
7. What fact could change that classification?
8. What operational action follows?
9. What would be documented?
10. Is escalation necessary?

Do not let students memorize “Scenario 4 = risky.” Change one material fact and ask them to reclassify.

## 8. Professional-Language Coaching

Preferred language:

- “The current facts support carrier-side coordination for the identified carrier.”
- “The relationship cannot be classified from the contract label alone.”
- “The dispatcher appears to have carrier-selection discretion; hold and escalate before proceeding.”
- “The freight should be returned or declined rather than reassigned without verified authority.”
- “More facts are required regarding who controls carrier selection.”
- “On the supplied facts, this raises significant brokerage risk.”
- “I verified ___; ___ remains unknown; the action is on hold pending verification.”

Avoid categorical statements such as:

- “dispatchers can never be brokers”;
- “three carriers is safe”;
- “a percentage fee makes you a broker”;
- “a contract makes this legal”;
- “not touching freight means you are not brokering”;
- “this worksheet proves the activity is legal.”

## 11. Practical Administration — Agency Boundary Decision Desk

The practical is a reasoning-and-work-product assessment, not a vocabulary exercise.

### Required student evidence
For Opportunities A–E require:

- classification;
- controlling facts;
- principal/relationship identification;
- freight-source tracing;
- allocation analysis;
- material unknowns where applicable;
- fact-change/verification reasoning;
- operational next action;
- professional case notes;
- false-safe-harbor explanation; and
- escalation handoff.

### Scoring
Use the controlled Module 02 rubric in `Modules-01-05-Instructor-Scoring-Guide.md`.

**Maximum:** 30 points  
**Pass:** 24/30 **AND no critical failure**.

Dimensions:

1. Classification accuracy — 0–3.
2. Principal/relationship identification — 0–3.
3. Freight-source tracing and cancellation/reassignment reasoning — 0–3.
4. Allocation-of-traffic analysis — 0–3 **CRITICAL**.
5. Known-versus-unknown fact discipline — 0–3.
6. Fact-change and verification reasoning — 0–3.
7. Operational next-action selection — 0–3 **CRITICAL**.
8. Professional case-note quality — 0–3.
9. False-safe-harbor rejection — 0–3.
10. Escalation handoff quality — 0–3.

### Critical failures
A learner fails the practical regardless of total score if the learner:

1. treats Opportunity B or D as ordinary safe carrier-side agency without recognizing the supplied discretionary allocation among competing carriers;
2. knowingly proceeds with a supplied high-risk allocation pattern after identifying the material boundary issue rather than holding/returning/declining/escalating appropriately; or
3. uses a false categorical safe harbor — carrier count, contract title, 1099 status, compensation method, “I never touch freight,” or equivalent — as the controlling reason to approve conduct despite contradictory facts.

Do not invent hidden critical failures during grading.

## 12. Practical Debrief Rules

Debrief after scoring, not while the learner is producing graded evidence.

For each deficient dimension:

1. identify the failed competency;
2. show the learner where the reasoning chain broke;
3. route the learner to the relevant student-manual section/tool;
4. assign an alternate scenario;
5. require the corrected reasoning and work product;
6. record remediation and retest status.

Do not convert remediation into answer disclosure. The learner must demonstrate the competency on changed facts.

## 13. Diagnostic Remediation Matrix

| Deficiency | Instructor response | Required new evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Principal/relationship confusion | Revisit agency/principal sections | Alternate principal-identification exercise |
| Freight-source/reassignment error | Revisit TRACE and cancellation vs reassignment | Alternate source-to-disposition trace |
| Allocation error | Mandatory allocation remediation | New overlapping-carrier scenario; correct selection-discretion analysis |
| Unknown-fact discipline | Withhold at least three material facts | Completed worksheet using UNKNOWN + HOLD/VERIFY rather than guessing |
| Classification without reasoning | Require controlling-fact analysis | New classification with facts and fact-change explanation |
| False safe harbor | Correct the specific myth | Explain why full conduct pattern overrides the isolated factor |
| Operational-action failure | Revisit ACT | Different scenario requiring PROCEED/HOLD/VERIFY/ESCALATE/DO NOT PROCEED reasoning |
| Documentation weakness | Model structure, not answer | Rewritten professional case note on alternate facts |
| Escalation weakness | Teach five-part handoff | New handoff with knowns, unknown, held action, evidence, review question |

Reassessment must use a materially different scenario while measuring the same competency.
