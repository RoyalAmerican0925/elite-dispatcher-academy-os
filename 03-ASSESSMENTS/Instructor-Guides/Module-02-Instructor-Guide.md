## 1. Employment Outcome
The learner must be able to operate on the carrier side without relying on job titles, contract labels, payment method, carrier count, tax status, or assumptions to determine legal boundaries. The learner identifies the represented principal, traces who supplied the freight opportunity, identifies who controls carrier selection, recognizes discretionary allocation of traffic, distinguishes cancellation from reassignment, and stops/escalates when facts or authority are insufficient.

The module is not successful if the learner merely memorizes that a dispatcher is “not a broker.” Actual conduct and relationship facts control the decision analysis.

## 2. Prerequisite Transfer
Reactivate Module 01 role and freight-flow knowledge. Require the learner to identify motor carrier, broker, shipper, driver, dispatcher, freight source, represented principal, and handoff point before analyzing a boundary problem.

Do not reteach Module 01. Require transfer of those concepts into a legal-boundary decision.

## 3. Core Operating Model
Teach every boundary problem through:

**STOP → IDENTIFY → TRACE → CHECK → CLASSIFY → ACT → DOCUMENT → ESCALATE**

The learner must answer:

1. Who is the represented principal?
2. Who supplied the freight opportunity?
3. Was the opportunity sourced for a specific represented carrier or supplied for later carrier selection?
4. Does the dispatcher have discretion to choose among competing carriers?
5. Is traffic being allocated among carriers?
6. Is the dispatcher merely communicating within an established carrier relationship, or arranging transportation more broadly?
7. What fact is missing?
8. What authority or scope document applies?
9. What is the safest defensible next action?
10. What must be documented or handed off?

Required decision language:

**PROCEED WITHIN SCOPE / MORE FACTS REQUIRED / STOP-VERIFY / RETURN OR DECLINE / ESCALATE FOR AUTHORITY OR LEGAL REVIEW**

## 5. Lesson Delivery
### A. Titles do not decide status
Demonstrate two fictional businesses using the same title “dispatch service” but performing materially different functions. Require students to classify the conduct, not the name.

### B. Principal and agency
A learner should be able to identify who the dispatcher represents and what authority actually exists. Possession of a contract does not expand authority beyond its terms or override applicable law.

### C. Broker and bona fide-agent concepts
Teach definitions and guidance as decision inputs rather than slogans. The student must distinguish a carrier-side agency relationship from activity involving broader arrangement of transportation or discretionary allocation among carriers.

### D. Allocation of traffic
This is a central occupational risk concept. Make the learner trace the freight opportunity from source to carrier. If several represented carriers overlap, require the learner to identify who decides which carrier receives the freight and on what basis.

### E. Cancellation versus reassignment
A cancellation/return-to-source event and a unilateral reassignment to another represented carrier are not operationally equivalent. Require the learner to draw the decision path and identify where carrier-selection discretion enters.

### F. Compensation and tax myths
Percentage fees, flat fees, subscription fees, invoice structure, or tax classification do not independently resolve broker/agency status. Require the learner to state what conduct facts still must be examined.

### G. Freight-money and document handling
Handling or not handling freight money or documents is evidence about workflow, not a universal legal switch. Students must avoid single-factor conclusions.

### H. Truthful role representation
Students must describe their role accurately to carriers, brokers, and shippers. They may not use “agent,” “broker,” “carrier representative,” or another title to imply authority they do not possess.

### I. Insufficient facts
A professional dispatcher does not guess. “More facts required” is a valid result when the missing fact changes the classification. The learner must name the missing fact and the verification source or person needed.

### J. Escalation
Teach escalation as a skilled operational act. A proper handoff states the known facts, unresolved issue, operational risk, documents reviewed, and exact decision requiring authorized review.

### K. Multi-factor boundary drill
Give the learner a fact pattern containing at least four potentially distracting facts—for example a written dispatch agreement, percentage-based fee, two represented carriers, and no direct handling of shipper money. Require the learner to explain why none of those facts independently resolves the boundary question and identify the conduct facts that actually require analysis.

### L. Operational consequence drill
Require the learner to explain the consequence of a bad dispatch-boundary decision in operational terms: an unauthorized commitment, an improper reassignment, a misrepresented role, a carrier relationship problem, or a matter requiring regulatory/legal review. Do not use fear-based teaching or unsupported legal conclusions.

## 6. Student Tool Facilitation
Use the Module 02 Decision Tool Worksheet on fresh examples before the practical. The instructor should watch whether the student:

- identifies the represented carrier/principal;
- traces the freight source;
- detects overlapping carrier choices;
- distinguishes known facts from assumptions;
- identifies allocation discretion;
- chooses an action rather than merely naming a risk;
- documents the reason for STOP/VERIFY or escalation.

Require complete reasoning. A correct classification with unsupported reasoning is not full competency evidence.

## 7. Professional-Language Coaching
Preferred language includes:

“The current facts support carrier-side coordination for the identified carrier.”

“The relationship cannot be classified from the contract label alone.”

“The dispatcher appears to have carrier-selection discretion; stop and escalate before proceeding.”

“The freight should be returned or declined rather than reassigned without verified authority.”

“More facts are required regarding who controls carrier selection.”

Avoid categorical statements such as “dispatchers can never be brokers,” “three carriers is safe,” “a percentage fee makes you a broker,” “a contract makes this legal,” or “not touching freight means you are not brokering.”

## 8. Practical Facilitation
The Agency Boundary Decision Desk is a reasoning assessment, not a vocabulary exercise. For each opportunity require classification, controlling facts, allocation analysis, operational action, and defensible explanation.

The critical-failure logic remains tied to failing to recognize material discretionary allocation risk in the designated scenarios. Do not invent hidden critical failures.

The practical should be remediated with a different fact pattern. Do not simply repeat the original opportunity after giving the answer.

## 10. Diagnostic Remediation
If the learner relies on a job title, return to actual-conduct analysis. If the learner relies on carrier count, compensation, tax status, or money handling as a safe harbor, require a multi-factor comparison exercise. If the learner misses allocation discretion, require a freight-source and carrier-selection map. If the learner treats cancellation and reassignment as identical, require a side-by-side decision trace. If the learner guesses when facts are missing, require a known/unknown/verification-source exercise. If the learner identifies risk but cannot act, require a written STOP/VERIFY/escalation handoff.

Reassessment must use a materially different scenario while measuring the same competency.
