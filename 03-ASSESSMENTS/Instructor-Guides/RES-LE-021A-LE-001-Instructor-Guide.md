## Instructor Purpose

Deliver LE-001 consistently and ensure learners demonstrate disciplined information capture before making a load decision.

## Lesson Outcomes

Learners must identify required load-offer fields, distinguish known from missing or unclear information, ask targeted clarification questions, and complete the standardized capture record without inventing facts.

## Delivery Plan — 60 Minutes

- **0–10 min:** Explain why information quality precedes rate or feasibility analysis.
- **10–20 min:** Teach the V/M/U/N/A field-status method and assumption control.
- **20–30 min:** Walk through the complete worked example.
- **30–45 min:** Guided classification practice; introduce controlled ambiguities.
- **45–55 min:** Independent incomplete-offer exercise.
- **55–60 min:** Debrief and formative check.

## Teaching Emphasis

Do not let learners jump directly to “good load/bad load.” Repeatedly redirect them to: What do we know? What do we not know? What must be clarified before the next decision layer?

## Key Facilitation Questions

- What fact in this offer could change equipment fit?
- What timing fact could change appointment feasibility?
- What does the quoted compensation actually include?
- Which field is estimated rather than exact?
- What are you assuming right now?
- What targeted question removes that assumption?

## Guided-Practice Variations

Change one field at a time: remove delivery time; change “53-foot dry van” to “van”; remove commodity; change exact address to city only; remove compensation basis. Ask learners which classification changes and why.

## Expected Performance

**Meets Standard:** learner identifies all major material fields, correctly flags missing/unclear facts, asks specific questions linked to downstream consequences, and documents the record clearly.

**Needs Remediation:** learner misses several material fields, relies on broad questions, or treats unknown information as favorable.

## Common Misconceptions to Correct

- “City names are enough.”
- “Van tells me everything about equipment.”
- “If special instructions aren’t shown, none exist.”
- “If the rate looks good, I can ask the rest later.”
- “Estimated miles are exact.”

## Debrief Model

Ask learners to defend why each question matters. A strong answer identifies the operational consequence: feasibility, timing, equipment, mileage, revenue basis, risk, or carrier policy.

## Answer Guidance

Use the answer guidance embedded in RES-LE-001. Accept alternate well-targeted clarification questions if they address a material missing/unclear fact. Do not require exact wording.

## Remediation

If a learner repeatedly assumes facts, require a second incomplete-offer exercise where every unsupported assumption must be rewritten as a question. If the learner misses categories, use the flashcard set before reassessment.

## Accessibility

Read scenario facts aloud if needed; provide digital and printable worksheet; do not require color to distinguish status; allow equivalent typed responses.

## Boundaries

This lesson does not establish universal carrier acceptance criteria, rate thresholds, legal advice, or specialty-freight rules. It teaches information completeness and assumption control using approved pilot knowledge.
