## 1. Employment Outcome
By the end of Module 04, the student should be able to build and review an operational carrier/driver onboarding file, distinguish dispatcher readiness work from motor-carrier compliance administration, detect material mismatches or expirations, protect payment-change workflows, and make a documented READY / VERIFY / STOP / ESCALATE / DO-NOT-DISPATCH decision without inventing facts or authority.

The module is not successful if the learner can merely name onboarding documents. The learner must be able to use the documents and profiles to decide whether the dispatch desk is operationally ready to proceed.

## 2. Prerequisites and Imports
The instructor should explicitly reactivate:

- **Module 01:** carrier, driver, broker and dispatcher roles; USDOT versus operating authority; verification discipline.
- **Module 02:** scope/agency boundaries and the rule that possession of information does not create authority.
- **Module 03:** equipment and driver/load compatibility; endorsements; exact-unit verification rather than nominal-label assumptions.

Do not reteach those modules. Ask the learner to use them inside onboarding decisions.

## 3. Core Mental Model
Teach Module 04 around this operating sequence:

**COLLECT → CLASSIFY → VERIFY → RECONCILE → PROTECT → DECIDE → DOCUMENT → TRACK**

For every onboarding item, the learner should be able to answer:
1. What is this item for?
2. Is it a legal/regulatory record, tax/payment document, industry document, carrier policy, or Academy operational record?
3. Who owns responsibility for it?
4. What does the item prove?
5. What does it not prove?
6. Is it current?
7. Does it match the rest of the file?
8. Is another source required?
9. What is the next safe operational action?

The repeated control language is:

**COMPLETE / MISSING / EXPIRED / MISMATCH / VERIFY / ESCALATE / DO NOT DISPATCH**

## 5. Lesson Delivery Notes

### A. Onboarding is operational readiness, not document collection
**Essential point:** A folder full of PDFs can still be operationally unusable.

**Demonstration:** Show a fictional packet with several documents present but one material conflict. Ask: “Is the file complete because the documents exist?” The correct professional response is to identify what remains unresolved.

**Misconception:** “If the carrier sent it, it is verified.”

**Correction:** Presence, facial completeness, currentness, entity match and authoritative verification are separate questions.

### B. Registration, authority and source selection
**Essential point:** Teach students to choose the source that answers the actual question.

The manual currently identifies Motus as the current 2026 registration environment and legacy L&I as historical-only after the May 2026 modernization. This is date-sensitive material. Do not turn the current interface into a permanent procedural fact.

**Misconception:** “SAFER tells me everything I need.”

**Correction:** SAFER is useful but not the complete current answer source for every registration/authority/insurance question.

**Instructor prompt:** “What fact are you trying to prove, and which current source proves that fact?”

### C. UCR
Teach the distinction between:
- the covered entity's underlying registration obligation; and
- a broker/customer/carrier workflow that screens for current status.

Do not teach that federal law automatically makes an outside dispatcher responsible for filing UCR or requires every broker to use one identical screening response.

### D. Insurance and COI literacy
Students should recognize the supplied federal public-liability baseline in the module's defined operating category while avoiding the false rule that every listed coverage is federally required.

**Demonstration:** Ask the learner to mark each of these as federal baseline / contractual or business requirement / varies: BIPD, cargo, physical damage, general liability, bobtail/non-trucking liability, occupational accident/workers' compensation.

**Misconception:** “The COI proves the carrier is authorized.”

**Correction:** A COI summarizes represented insurance information. It is not operating authority and does not prove that every relevant status remains current.

### E. W-9/entity reconciliation
Teach a mismatch as a verification event, not an accusation.

**Professional language:** “The records do not yet reconcile; verify the tax/entity relationship.”

Avoid: “The carrier is fake because the names differ.”

### F. DQ file versus dispatch profile
This is a critical Module 04 boundary.

The dispatcher may need operational driver information. That does not silently convert the dispatcher into the carrier's DQ-file, Clearinghouse, drug/alcohol, hiring or background-compliance administrator.

**Instructor test:** Ask learners to place fields into two buckets: operational dispatch profile versus specialized carrier compliance administration. Discuss overlap without collapsing ownership.

### G. Medical-certification status
Teach only the current verified procedure and the dispatcher's operational role.

During the currently documented 2026 temporary exemption, a qualifying paper MEC inside the described window does not mean “automatic proceed.” The learner must verify the certificate/driver match and carrier process and must not claim to make the carrier's final legal qualification determination.

**High-risk misconception:** “It is under 60 days, therefore I can clear the driver.”

**Correction:** The time window is one fact in a verification process.

### H. CDL class and endorsements
Use Module 03 knowledge operationally. A general statement that a driver “has a CDL” is insufficient. The credential must match the contemplated combination/load.

Do not expand this section into full hazmat-compliance training.

### I. Factoring, NOA and payment security
The learning target is not receivables law. It is safe operational handling of payment information.

Teach:
**NEW PAYMENT INSTRUCTION → STOP → USE PREVIOUSLY VERIFIED CHANNEL → CALLBACK/INDEPENDENT VERIFY → COMPARE → DOCUMENT → UPDATE ONLY AFTER CONFIRMATION**

**Critical misconception:** A familiar-looking email address or urgent message is enough to authorize a bank change.

### J. LPOA and dispatch agreement
Teach scope, not magic words. An LPOA grants only what its terms and applicable law support. A dispatch agreement should make operational authority and limits visible.

### K. Equipment/driver profiles and carrier preferences
Students should build usable records rather than collect every imaginable field.

Use the manual's **ESSENTIAL / USEFUL / OPTIONAL** logic. Teach data minimization for sensitive information.

Carrier preferences such as lanes, deadhead targets, home time or prohibited freight are **carrier policy/business choices**, not automatically law.

### L. Communication hierarchy and expiration tracking
Knowing whom to contact does not prove that person can authorize the decision.

Expiration tracking must distinguish:
- legal expiration;
- annual renewal;
- load-by-load verification;
- company/best-practice review.

Do not invent a universal reverification interval.

## 6. Student Tool Facilitation

### Carrier Onboarding Checklist
**Purpose:** Turn a packet into a readiness decision.

Observe whether the learner distinguishes document presence from verified status and uses STOP/VERIFY/ESCALATE appropriately.

### Driver Dispatch Profile Template
**Purpose:** Build the narrower operational profile needed to dispatch without reproducing the carrier's full compliance file.

Watch for unnecessary medical detail and missing operational fields such as availability, assigned equipment, endorsements or communication method.

### Equipment Profile Template
**Purpose:** Carry Module 03 equipment facts into the carrier's working dispatch file.

Watch for nominal equipment labels being treated as proof of exact dimensions/capability.

### Expiration & Reverification Tracker
**Purpose:** Convert current onboarding into a maintained operating control.

Watch for students inventing legal renewal periods rather than recording the actual expiration/verification trigger.

### Payment-Change STOP-VERIFY-ESCALATE Tool
**Purpose:** Build a repeatable control against unverified payment-direction changes.

The student should never use contact information contained only in the suspicious/new request as the sole independent verification channel.

## 7. Scenario Facilitation
Use the manual scenarios as decision drills, not reading-comprehension questions.

For each scenario require:
- classification;
- controlling fact;
- what is known;
- what remains unresolved;
- who owns the next decision;
- next safe action;
- professional wording for the handoff.

Particularly important scenarios:
- W-9/entity mismatch;
- COI versus inactive authority;
- expiring credential during a trip;
- missing factoring/payment documentation;
- NOA versus new payment request;
- CDL/combination mismatch;
- endorsement mismatch;
- compliance-scope request;
- UCR current-year issue;
- familiar-email bank change;
- altered-looking/inconsistent documents;
- paper MEC during the temporary NRII window.

## 8. Professional-Language Coaching
Require language that separates evidence from conclusion.

Preferred:
- “The entity records do not yet reconcile.”
- “The payment change is unverified; hold the update pending independent confirmation.”
- “The current file does not support dispatching this driver on this load.”
- “This request is outside the ordinary dispatch profile and requires scope/competency review.”
- “The paper MEC is within the stated exemption window, but the certificate/driver match and carrier process still require verification.”

Avoid:
- “This is definitely fraud” from a mismatch alone.
- “The COI proves authority.”
- “The dispatcher is responsible for the DQ file.”
- “The driver is medically cleared because the paper is under 60 days old.”
- “The carrier has to follow my preferred internal review interval because it is federal law.”

## 9. Assessment Administration

### Knowledge Check
Diagnostic/formative. Use wrong answers to locate misconceptions before the graded exam/practical.

### Exam
Current exam standard: **23/26 (85%) and Critical Item 22 correct.**

Critical Item 22 tests whether the learner will dispatch a driver on a fictional placarded hazmat load despite the stated missing required H/X endorsement.

### Practical
Current practical: Lone Star Ridge Transport onboarding file review.

Scoring source: `Modules-01-05-Instructor-Scoring-Guide.md`.

Current standard: **17/21 and no critical failure.**

Critical failures:
1. approving Driver B for the stated placarded hazmat load despite missing required endorsement;
2. redirecting payment from the unverified message alone;
3. treating the dispatch profile as automatic authority to take over the carrier's DQ/drug-alcohol compliance program.

Do not create additional hidden critical failures during administration.

## 10. Diagnostic Remediation

| Observed error | Likely failed mental model | Route back to | Targeted remediation evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| COI treated as authority proof | document presence = complete verification | Manual §§2, 4–5; onboarding checklist | explain what COI proves/doesn't and name next source/action |
| W-9 mismatch called fraud | discrepancy = guilt | Manual §6; Scenario S-04-01 | write a neutral verification note and reconciliation plan |
| dispatcher accepts DQ/compliance administration automatically | operational profile = carrier compliance ownership | Manual §7; S-04-09 | classify five records/tasks by owner/scope |
| paper MEC automatically clears driver | date window = final qualification determination | Manual §8; S-04-13 | identify remaining verification and proper handoff |
| endorsement mismatch ignored | “has CDL” = qualified for every load | Manual §§9–10; S-04-08 | explain exact incompatibility and safe disposition |
| payment redirect accepted | inbound request = authentication | Manual §§13–14; payment-change tool | demonstrate trusted-channel callback workflow |
| preferences described as law | carrier policy = regulation | Manual §20 | relabel sample requirements by authority/source |
| arbitrary reverification interval invented | best practice = legal deadline | Manual §22 | classify legal expiration vs annual/load-by-load/company review |

Reassessment should use an alternate scenario, not merely repeat the exact answer the student just saw.
