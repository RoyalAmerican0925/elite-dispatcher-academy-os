## 1. Employment Outcome
A successful learner can build and review an operational carrier/driver onboarding file, distinguish dispatcher-readiness work from motor-carrier compliance administration, detect missing/conflicting/expired information, authenticate sensitive changes, and make a documented READY / VERIFY / STOP / ESCALATE / DO-NOT-DISPATCH decision without inventing facts or authority.

The module is not successful if the learner can merely name documents. The learner must use evidence to make an operational readiness decision.

## 2. Prerequisite Imports
Reactivate rather than reteach:
- Module 01: carrier, driver, broker, dispatcher roles; registration/authority; verification discipline.
- Module 02: scope/agency boundaries; possession of information does not create authority.
- Module 03: equipment and driver/load compatibility; endorsements; actual-unit verification.

## 3. Core Mental Model
**COLLECT → CLASSIFY → VERIFY → RECONCILE → PROTECT → DECIDE → DOCUMENT → TRACK**

For each onboarding item require the learner to answer: what is it for; what type of requirement/evidence is it; who owns it; what does it prove; what does it not prove; is it current; does it reconcile with the file; what source resolves uncertainty; and what is the next safe action?

Control language: **COMPLETE / MISSING / EXPIRED / MISMATCH / VERIFY / ESCALATE / DO NOT DISPATCH**.

## 5. Source/Authority Taxonomy
Require learners to distinguish **FEDERAL LAW/REGULATION / CURRENT FEDERAL SYSTEM OR PROCEDURE / CONTRACT REQUIREMENT / CARRIER POLICY / INDUSTRY PRACTICE / ACADEMY CONTROL MODEL**.

Do not convert a current interface, broker preference, carrier policy, or Academy workflow into a universal legal rule.

## 6. Lesson Delivery
### A. Onboarding is readiness, not collection
A folder full of documents may still be unusable. Presence, facial completeness, currentness, entity match, and authoritative verification are separate questions.

### B. Registration/authority/source selection
Teach students to ask what fact must be proved and which current authoritative source proves it. Current FMCSA registration modernization is date-sensitive; do not permanently encode an interface name or screenshot as law.

### C. UCR
Distinguish the covered entity's obligation from a customer's screening workflow. Do not assign filing responsibility to an outside dispatcher merely because the dispatcher tracks status.

### D. Insurance/COI literacy
A COI summarizes represented insurance information. It is not operating authority and does not prove every relevant status remains current. Distinguish federal baseline, contract/business requirement, and variable coverage.

### E. W-9/entity reconciliation
A mismatch is a verification event, not proof of fraud. Preferred language: “The records do not yet reconcile; verify the tax/entity relationship.”

### F. DQ file versus dispatch profile
Operational driver information does not silently convert the dispatcher into the carrier's DQ-file, Clearinghouse, drug/alcohol, hiring, or background-compliance administrator. Teach ownership and escalation explicitly.

### G. Medical-certification/NRII
Current September 2026 primary-source control: FMCSA's nationwide temporary exemption permits interstate CDL/CLP holders and motor carriers to rely on a paper MEC as proof of medical certification for up to 60 days after issuance during **April 11, 2026 through October 11, 2026**. It is a temporary transition rule, not a permanent Academy rule. A paper MEC inside that window does not authorize the dispatcher to make the carrier's final qualification determination. Verify certificate/driver match and carrier process. Reverify before any delivery after October 11, 2026.

### H. CDL class/endorsements
“Has a CDL” is insufficient. Credential must match the contemplated vehicle/load. Current FMCSA definitions used here: N = tank vehicle; H = hazardous materials; X = combination tank vehicle/hazardous materials. Do not expand Module 04 into full hazmat-compliance training.

### I. Factoring, NOA, payment security
Teach: **NEW PAYMENT INSTRUCTION → STOP → USE PREVIOUSLY VERIFIED CHANNEL → CALLBACK/INDEPENDENT VERIFY → COMPARE → DOCUMENT → UPDATE ONLY AFTER CONFIRMATION**. Never authenticate a payment redirect solely through contact information contained in the suspicious/new request.

### J. LPOA/dispatch agreement
Teach scope, not magic words. The document does not create authority beyond its terms/applicable law.

### K. Driver/equipment profiles and carrier preferences
Use ESSENTIAL / USEFUL / OPTIONAL discipline and data minimization. Carrier lanes, deadhead targets, home time, prohibited freight, etc. are business/policy controls unless an independent legal requirement applies.

### L. Communication hierarchy and expiration tracking
Knowing whom to contact does not prove authorization. Track actual legal expiration, annual renewal, load-by-load verification, and company review separately. Do not invent universal reverification intervals.

## 8. Scenario Facilitation
For every scenario require: classification; controlling fact; known facts; unresolved facts; evidence/source; decision owner; next action; professional handoff wording.

Priority patterns: W-9/entity mismatch; COI vs authority; expiring credential; factoring/payment documentation; NOA vs new payment request; CDL/combination mismatch; endorsement mismatch; compliance-scope request; UCR status; familiar-email bank change; inconsistent documents; paper MEC during the temporary NRII window.

Change one material fact and require a fresh decision to test understanding rather than memorization.

## 9. Professional Language
Prefer evidence-bounded language: “records do not yet reconcile”; “payment change is unverified”; “current file does not support dispatch”; “outside ordinary dispatch scope; escalate”; “paper MEC is within the temporary window, but remaining verification still applies.”

Reject categorical unsupported statements such as mismatch = fraud, COI = authority, dispatcher owns DQ file, paper MEC automatically clears driver, or internal review interval = federal law.

## 10. Assessment Administration
Knowledge Check is diagnostic.

Exam standard: **23/26 (85%) and Critical Item 22 correct.** Item 22 tests the stated placarded-hazmat/required-H-or-X mismatch.

Practical: Lone Star Ridge Transport onboarding review. Standard: **17/21 and no critical failure**.

Three controlled practical critical failures:
1. approve Driver B for stated placarded hazmat despite missing required endorsement;
2. redirect payment from unverified message alone;
3. treat dispatch profile as automatic authority to take over carrier DQ/drug-alcohol compliance program.

Do not invent hidden critical failures.

## 11. Diagnostic Remediation
- COI treated as authority → identify what COI proves/doesn't; select authoritative next source.
- W-9 mismatch called fraud → neutral verification note and reconciliation plan.
- compliance takeover → classify operational vs carrier-owned tasks and create escalation handoff.
- paper MEC automatically clears → identify temporary-rule conditions, remaining verification, owner.
- endorsement mismatch ignored → state exact incompatibility and do-not-dispatch disposition.
- payment redirect accepted → trusted-channel callback workflow.
- preferences described as law → relabel by authority/source.
- arbitrary reverification interval → classify actual expiration/renewal/verification/company-review trigger.

Retest with materially different scenarios.
