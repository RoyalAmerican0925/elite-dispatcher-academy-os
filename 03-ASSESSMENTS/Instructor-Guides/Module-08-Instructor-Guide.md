# Module 08 Instructor Guide — Rate Negotiation & Broker Communication

**Status:** PREMIUM INSTRUCTIONAL CONTROL — SIM-008 BUILD PENDING  
**Branch:** `academy-master-architecture`

## 1. Employment Outcome
The student can prepare a fact/economics/authority-based negotiation position, conduct professional discovery, make evidence-supported counters and conditional trades, protect carrier authority limits, preserve unresolved terms, document negotiation accurately, and hand the opportunity into Module 09 without falsely representing completed booking.

## 2. Operating Framework
**PREPARE → VERIFY FACTS → SET POSITION → OPEN → QUESTION → LISTEN → COUNTER → TRADE → CONFIRM → DOCUMENT → ACCEPT / DECLINE / ESCALATE**

Teach this as a decision process, not a memorized script.

## 4. Required Instructor Demonstrations
Before graded practical work, instructor must model at least these six contrasts:
1. **Bad call vs corrected call** — emotional bargaining versus prepared position.
2. **Broker silence** — self-negotiation versus disciplined pause/question.
3. **Vague accessorial statement** — assumption versus four-part clarification.
4. **Material fact change** — stale position versus re-evaluation.
5. **Authority boundary** — unauthorized acceptance versus escalation.
6. **Closeout/handoff** — “booked” claim versus agreed-in-principle + Module 09 handoff.

The demonstration should explain *why* the corrected behavior protects the carrier, not merely provide a preferred sentence.

## 5. Progressive Evidence Release
Applied Lab, Practical and SIM-008 must release facts progressively.

Instructor rules:
- Do not reveal later-stage broker movement early.
- Do not reveal the expected final disposition before the learner commits to the current-stage decision.
- Do not supply a missing fact simply because the learner failed to ask for it unless the administration protocol specifically calls for release.
- Preserve learner notes/decisions at each stage.
- Do not allow the student to back-edit the first-attempt record after seeing consequences.

## 6. Controlled Critical-Failure Families
Exactly six families control Module 08:

### CF-01 — Below-floor unauthorized acceptance
Learner knowingly accepts below a supplied carrier minimum/floor where authorization is required and no authorization is present.

### CF-02 — Fabricated leverage
Learner invents competing offers, market facts, truck position, driver facts, urgency or similar leverage.

### CF-03 — Material fact misrepresentation
Learner materially misrepresents carrier, truck, driver, equipment, load or operating facts.

### CF-04 — Unauthorized carrier commitment
Learner promises/commits a term outside supplied authority.

### CF-05 — False agreement state
Learner records a material unresolved term as agreed or otherwise converts uncertainty into agreement.

### CF-06 — Premature booking representation
Learner records verbal negotiation/alignment as completed booking before Module 09 controls.

**Do not invent a seventh family.** Ordinary weak performance may reduce score without becoming a critical failure.

## 7. Critical-Failure Persistence and Remediation
A critical failure is not erased by correction.

Required record:
1. first-attempt decision/statement;
2. triggered critical-failure family;
3. instructor rationale;
4. targeted remediation;
5. materially different reassessment scenario;
6. corrected reassessment performance;
7. final competency state.

A learner may recover competency after successful remediation/reassessment, but the historical first-attempt evidence remains in the training record.

### Materially different reassessment examples
- If learner fabricated a competing offer, reassessment uses a different scenario involving truck-position or schedule leverage.
- If learner accepted below floor, reassessment uses a different target/floor/approval structure and different broker movement.
- If learner mislabeled BOOKED, reassessment uses different negotiated terms with one unresolved item and asks for closeout status.

Repeating the same question until the learner clicks the expected answer is not reassessment.

## 8. Scoring Interpretation
The Final Exam is 30 items; passing raw score is 27/30. Critical items require targeted remediation when missed even if the raw score passes.

The Practical is 30 points; passing is **24/30 AND zero unresolved critical failures**.

Do not allow a high raw score to cancel an unresolved critical failure.

## 9. Acceptable Variation
Negotiation is not a one-script competency. Student wording may vary if it remains:
- truthful;
- professional;
- within authority;
- responsive to actual facts;
- economically grounded;
- clear about conditions and unresolved issues.

Do not score against exact phrasing unless the phrase itself creates a material false statement or commitment.

## 10. Module Boundaries
- **Module 02:** deeper agency/legal boundary analysis.
- **Module 06:** broker/entity/contact/load/payment verification.
- **Module 07:** profitability mathematics and economic threshold arithmetic.
- **Module 08:** negotiation preparation, discovery, countering, trading, confirmation and negotiation documentation.
- **Module 09:** formal booking, rate-confirmation review and dispatch-document handoff.
- **Module 11:** detailed HOS/ELD feasibility.
- **Module 13:** deeper detention/TONU/exception workflow.
- **Module 14:** billing/factoring/closeout.

Do not turn Module 08 into a substitute for these later workflows.

## 11. Source and Currentness Controls
- Do not teach a current market rate as an evergreen benchmark.
- Do not teach universal dispatcher/factoring/accessorial percentages.
- Broker statements such as “everyone is paying X” remain broker statements unless independently supported.
- Agreement-specific accessorial terms must be tied to the applicable document/process.
- Federal legal/regulatory statements used in final commercial content must be reverified before release.
