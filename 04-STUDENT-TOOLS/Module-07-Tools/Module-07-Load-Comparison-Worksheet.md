## Comparison Discipline

### 1. Normalize before ranking
For every load, calculate total trip miles and effective RPM before making a recommendation. Headline revenue and loaded RPM are not sufficient by themselves.

### 2. Compare cost treatment consistently
A comparison can still be valid when one load legitimately has a $0 cost that another load has—for example, one transaction uses factoring and another does not. What makes a comparison unreliable is **inconsistent category treatment, an unresolved material cost, or an assumption hidden as fact**.

For each load, state which cost categories are included and which are excluded.

|---|---|---|---|

### 3. Keep possible revenue separate
Do not add detention, layover, TONU, reimbursement, or other possible revenue unless the relevant trigger, eligibility, documentation, and amount are sufficiently established for the decision being made.

### 4. Separate positioning from guaranteed economics
Destination positioning matters. It is still a planning factor—not guaranteed future revenue. Record the evidence and explain its weight separately from the current load's verified dollars.

### 5. Use HOLD FOR DATA when the math cannot honestly be completed
If a material unknown could change the recommended disposition, identify the missing input and hold the decision rather than manufacturing a number.

### 6. Counter threshold is mathematics here, negotiation in Module 08
If the carrier has supplied sufficient decision criteria, calculate the minimum total revenue required for the load to meet that criterion. Module 07 determines the economic threshold; Module 08 teaches how to negotiate toward it.
