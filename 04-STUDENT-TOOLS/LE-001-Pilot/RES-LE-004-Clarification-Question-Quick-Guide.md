## Purpose

Use this guide when a load offer is missing or unclear. Do not guess. Ask the smallest clear question that resolves the operational uncertainty.

## Question Framework

1. What exact fact is missing or unclear?
2. What downstream decision does it affect?
3. Who is the appropriate source for clarification?
4. What exact question will resolve it?
5. How will the answer be documented?

## Location

Ask: “What is the exact pickup/delivery facility or address?”

Use when: city/region is given but exact facility matters.

## Timing

Ask: “What are the pickup and delivery dates and times? Is each a fixed appointment, FCFS process, or operating window?”

Use when: timing or appointment method is incomplete.

## Commodity

Ask: “What is the commodity and are there any handling restrictions or special requirements?”

Use when: freight characteristics could affect fit, safety, or carrier policy.

## Weight / Dimensions

Ask: “What is the stated weight, and are dimensions material for this load?”

Use when: equipment capacity or specialty requirements could be affected.

## Equipment

Ask: “What exact equipment configuration is required?”

Use when: generic language such as ‘van,’ ‘reefer,’ or ‘flatbed’ is insufficient for the current decision.

## Mileage

Ask: “What loaded mileage or mileage basis is being used?”

Use when: later rate or feasibility analysis requires a defined distance basis.

## Compensation

Ask: “What does the quoted amount include? What revenue basis should be used for the calculation?”

Use when: linehaul, fuel surcharge, accessorials, or other components are unclear.

## Special Instructions

Ask: “Are there any facility, handling, seal, driver-assist, lumper, PPE, washout, tarping, documentation, or other special requirements?”

Use only as a prompt to uncover material conditions; regulated specialty procedures require their own approved guidance.

## Counterparty / Contact

Ask: “What load ID, company/contact information, and authoritative identifiers should be recorded for this transaction?”

Use when: the source or identity information is incomplete.

## Assumption Test

Before you write a fact into the load record, ask:

> “Did the offer or an approved source actually tell me this?”

If NO: classify it Missing or Unclear and ask a question.

## Priority Rule

Ask first about gaps that could change equipment fit, appointment feasibility, mileage/revenue analysis, safety/compliance, carrier acceptance, or the ability to identify the transaction.

## Stop / Escalate Conditions

Do not move forward merely because the rate looks attractive. Hold, clarify, or escalate when missing information prevents reliable evaluation or when the issue exceeds dispatcher authority.

## Do Not Use Vague Questions as Your Only Control

Weak: “Anything else?”  
Better: “Is pickup a fixed appointment or a receiving window?”

Weak: “Is the load normal?”  
Better: “Are there temperature, handling, facility, or documentation requirements beyond standard dry-van movement?”

## Documentation Rule

Record the clarified answer and where it came from. If a fact remains unresolved, preserve the unresolved condition in the load record rather than silently converting it to a yes/no answer.
