## Cards

### Card 1
**Front:** What is load-offer information completeness?  
**Back:** Having the material facts needed to evaluate and communicate a load without filling gaps through assumption.

### Card 2
**Front:** What are the four field-status labels used in LE-001?  
**Back:** Verified, Missing, Unclear, and Not Applicable.

### Card 3
**Front:** What should a dispatcher do when a material fact is missing?  
**Back:** Clarify, verify, hold, or escalate as appropriate; do not invent the missing fact.

### Card 4
**Front:** Name four core timing/location fields.  
**Back:** Exact origin, exact destination, pickup date/time or window, and delivery date/time or window.

### Card 5
**Front:** Why is “Houston to Atlanta” usually incomplete by itself?  
**Back:** It may not identify the exact pickup/delivery facilities or timing needed for reliable evaluation.

### Card 6
**Front:** Does “van” automatically prove full equipment compatibility?  
**Back:** No. Commodity, weight, dimensions, handling, carrier restrictions, and other requirements may still matter.

### Card 7
**Front:** What is the correct response to an unclear compensation quote?  
**Back:** Record the quoted amount and clarify the revenue basis/components instead of silently assuming what is included.

### Card 8
**Front:** What does a missing special-instructions field prove?  
**Back:** Nothing by itself. Missing information is not proof that no special conditions exist.

### Card 9
**Front:** What is the purpose of a targeted clarification question?  
**Back:** To resolve a specific unknown that could affect feasibility, equipment, timing, mileage, revenue, risk, or carrier policy.

### Card 10
**Front:** Why should rate analysis come after information capture?  
**Back:** A rate cannot make an unknown or infeasible load reliable; the dispatcher first needs the facts required for evaluation.

### Card 11
**Front:** Give one example of an Unclear field.  
**Back:** “Pickup today” with no time, appointment method, or operating window.

### Card 12
**Front:** Give one example of a Verified field.  
**Back:** “Pickup August 12 at 08:00 fixed appointment” when supplied by the controlled offer source.

### Card 13
**Front:** What should be recorded after a missing fact is clarified?  
**Back:** The clarified fact and its source/confirmation context.

### Card 14
**Front:** Why should estimated mileage be labeled as an estimate?  
**Back:** To avoid false precision and prevent a routing estimate from being treated as guaranteed actual mileage.

### Card 15
**Front:** What question should you ask yourself before assuming a fact?  
**Back:** “Can I verify this from the offer or an approved source?” If not, turn the assumption into a question.

### Card 16
**Front:** What is a strong lesson-level performance signal?  
**Back:** The learner captures all material facts, flags gaps correctly, asks targeted questions, and makes no unsupported assumptions.

### Card 17
**Front:** What is a critical coaching flag in LE-001?  
**Back:** Recommending that a load proceed while knowingly missing information that materially prevents reliable evaluation.

### Card 18
**Front:** What are examples of material load-offer categories beyond location and timing?  
**Back:** Commodity, weight, equipment, mileage basis, compensation basis, special instructions, counterparty/contact, and material accessorial/operational conditions.

### Card 19
**Front:** Does Not Applicable mean “I don’t know”?  
**Back:** No. N/A means the field reasonably does not apply; unknown information should be Missing or Unclear.

### Card 20
**Front:** Finish the rule: “Unknown is not the same as…”  
**Back:** “…no restriction.”
