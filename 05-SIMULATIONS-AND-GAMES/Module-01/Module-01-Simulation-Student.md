## Before You Start

You are a newly trained dispatcher working with **North Star Transport**. Today, a new freight opportunity comes across your desk. You'll work through it exactly the way it would unfold at a real dispatch desk — piece by piece, with information arriving as you need it, not all at once.

At several points you'll be asked to decide something. Pick your answer, then find the matching response below to see what happens next. If you get something wrong, you'll get a chance to think it through again before moving on — this is a learning exercise, not a test you can fail out of.

## STAGE 1 — New Freight File

Your dispatch inbox shows a new message:

> **FREIGHT OPPORTUNITY NOTICE**
> From: Prairie Freight Brokers
> To: North Star Transport Dispatch
> Re: Truckload — Omaha, NE to Des Moines, IA
>
> We have a truckload available from Blue River Foods (shelf-stable food products, Omaha, NE) to a regional grocery distribution center in Des Moines, IA. Reference #PFB-40217. Let us know if North Star can cover this.

**DECISION 1.1 — Identify the Parties**

Based on this message alone, match each name/party to its role. Write your answer before checking below.

- Blue River Foods
- Prairie Freight Brokers
- North Star Transport
- The grocery distribution center in Des Moines

*(Roles to choose from: Shipper, Broker, Motor Carrier, Consignee/Receiver)*

**Check your answer:**
- Blue River Foods = **Shipper** (it manufactures the goods and needs them moved)
- Prairie Freight Brokers = **Broker** (it arranged the transportation; it does not own the freight or the truck)
- North Star Transport = **Motor Carrier** (the company being asked to physically move the freight)
- The Des Moines distribution center = **Consignee/Receiver** (the party the freight is being delivered to)

**If you matched all four correctly:** Good — proceed to Decision 1.2.

**If you missed one or more:** This is a common beginner mix-up. Remember: the shipper *has* the freight, the broker *arranges* the move without touching the freight, the carrier is *asked to move* the freight, and the receiver is *who gets it*. Re-read the notice above with that in mind, then proceed to Decision 1.2.

**DECISION 1.2 — Two Roles Not Yet Named**

The notice doesn't mention two more roles you'll need for this load: the **dispatcher** and the **driver**. Who fills each role, based on what you know about North Star Transport's structure?

A. The dispatcher and driver are both employees of Prairie Freight Brokers
B. The dispatcher coordinates the load for North Star; a North Star driver physically drives the truck
C. The dispatcher is you, and the driver is also you
D. Blue River Foods assigns both roles

**If you selected B:** Correct. You (the dispatcher) coordinate this load on behalf of North Star Transport — the motor carrier you work for. A separate person, North Star's driver, physically operates the truck. Proceed to Stage 2.

**If you selected A, C, or D:** Not quite. The dispatcher and driver both work in service of the motor carrier (North Star), not the broker — and they are two distinct roles, not the same person or the same role as each other. Re-read the Module 01 role definitions (Section 2 of the Student Manual) if this feels unclear, then proceed to Stage 2.

## STAGE 2 — Who Does What?

Now that the parties are identified, match each **action** below to the role that actually performs it.

**DECISION 2.1 — Match the Actions**

| Action | Who does this? |
|---|---|
| Manufactures the food products | ? |
| Arranges transportation between Blue River and a carrier | ? |
| Agrees to transport the freight as the motor carrier | ? |
| Coordinates the load on the carrier's behalf | ? |
| Physically drives the truck | ? |
| Receives the shipment at the destination | ? |
| Provides accelerated payment after invoicing | ? |

*(Roles: Shipper, Broker, Motor Carrier, Dispatcher, Driver, Receiver, Factoring Company)*

**Check your answers:**
- Manufactures the food products → **Shipper** (Blue River Foods)
- Arranges transportation → **Broker** (Prairie Freight Brokers)
- Agrees to transport as motor carrier → **Motor Carrier** (North Star Transport)
- Coordinates the load on the carrier's behalf → **Dispatcher** (you)
- Physically drives the truck → **Driver**
- Receives the shipment → **Receiver** (the Des Moines distribution center)
- Provides accelerated payment after invoicing → **Factoring Company** (introduced later in this scenario — see Stage 5)

**DECISION 2.2 — The Core Distinction**

Which statement correctly describes the difference between Prairie Freight Brokers and North Star Transport?

A. They do the same job, just with different names
B. Prairie Freight Brokers arranges transportation without transporting the freight itself; North Star Transport actually transports it
C. Prairie Freight Brokers owns the truck; North Star Transport just handles paperwork
D. There is no meaningful difference — both are "the trucking company"

**If you selected B:** Correct. This is the single most important distinction in Module 01: **arranging** transportation is not the same as **physically transporting** it. Proceed to Decision 2.3.

**If you selected A, C, or D:** This mixes up the two roles. A broker arranges; a carrier transports. Neither owns what the other does. Review Module 01, Section 2, then proceed to Decision 2.3.

**DECISION 2.3 — Where Does the Dispatcher Fit?**

Which statement correctly describes your role as North Star's dispatcher in this load?

A. You are effectively acting as a second broker for this load
B. You are effectively part of the motor carrier's operation, coordinating this load on North Star's behalf
C. You are legally the same as the driver
D. Your role only exists after delivery, for invoicing purposes

**If you selected B:** Correct. As the carrier-side dispatcher, you coordinate on behalf of the motor carrier — you are not a broker, and communicating with a broker doesn't change that. Proceed to Stage 3.

**If you selected A, C, or D:** Reconsider — the carrier-side dispatch model taught in this academy has you working on behalf of North Star Transport specifically, not as a broker and not interchangeably with the driver. The deeper legal boundary between dispatching and brokering is Module 02's territory; for now, just hold onto the basic role. Proceed to Stage 3.

## STAGE 3 — Carrier Identity Check

Before you can confidently work this load, you pull up North Star Transport's basic carrier record:

> **CARRIER PROFILE CARD — NORTH STAR TRANSPORT**
> Legal Name: North Star Transport LLC
> USDOT Number: 9284471 (fictional, for training only)
> MC Operating Authority: MC-558213 (fictional, for training only) — Property Carrier
> Equipment on file: Dry van, 53-foot

**DECISION 3.1 — USDOT Number vs. Operating Authority**

Which statement correctly distinguishes the USDOT number from the MC operating authority shown above?

A. They're two numbers for the exact same thing — either one proves the same fact
B. The USDOT number identifies North Star as a registered entity; the MC operating authority is the separate legal permission North Star holds to conduct for-hire transportation
C. The USDOT number is only used for drivers, not companies
D. Having a USDOT number automatically means North Star can haul any type of for-hire interstate freight

**If you selected B:** Correct. A USDOT number identifies *who* the company is in FMCSA's system. Operating authority is the separate registration establishing *what* the company is authorized to do. North Star having both, as shown on this card, is exactly what you'd want to see before dispatching a load.

**If you selected A, C, or D:** Not quite. These are related but distinct — a company can have a USDOT number without necessarily holding the specific operating authority its work requires, and a USDOT number by itself never automatically proves authorization for every kind of freight. Review Module 01, Section 4, then continue.

**IMPORTANT — beginner-level only:** This simulation does not go deeper into registration or authority verification procedure. Full carrier-verification workflow using current FMCSA sources is Module 04's territory, and broker/authority verification depth is Module 06's. Here, you only need to recognize that these are two different things.

**Proceed to Stage 4.**

## STAGE 4 — Dispatch Desk Decisions

The load is confirmed. Over the course of the day, several messages and situations reach your desk. For each, decide who owns the responsibility and/or what you should do next.

**DECISION 4.1 — Driver Asks for Pickup Information**

> **DRIVER DISPATCH MESSAGE**
> From: North Star Driver (assigned to this load)
> To: Dispatch
> "Hey, what time am I picking up at Blue River tomorrow, and what's the dock number?"

What should you do?

A. Tell the driver you don't have that information and to figure it out himself
B. Provide the driver the pickup details you have on file for this load, since coordinating this information for the driver is part of your role
C. Tell the driver to call Prairie Freight Brokers directly and handle it himself
D. Ignore the message — that's not your job

**If you selected B:** Correct. Coordinating operational information between the carrier's driver and the rest of the load is core dispatcher work.

**If you selected A, C, or D:** Reconsider — part of the dispatcher's job is exactly this kind of coordination. Leaving the driver to sort out pickup details alone, or ignoring the request, isn't consistent with the coordinating role you learned in Stage 2.

**DECISION 4.2 — Broker Contacts You About the Load**

> **BROKER CONTACT CARD**
> Prairie Freight Brokers calls: "Just confirming North Star is still good for load PFB-40217, pickup tomorrow morning."

What should you do?

A. Confirm the load is still on, since you're the dispatcher coordinating it for North Star
B. Tell them you're not authorized to speak to brokers at all
C. Refuse to answer because talking to a broker makes you the broker
D. Transfer the call to the driver

**If you selected A:** Correct. Communicating with a broker about a load your carrier is handling is a normal, expected part of the dispatcher's coordinating role — it does not make you a broker. (Module 02 covers the actual legal boundary between dispatching and brokering in depth; simply talking to a broker about your own carrier's load isn't what crosses that line.)

**If you selected B, C, or D:** This overcorrects. Communicating with the broker about your own carrier's load is exactly the kind of coordination a dispatcher does. Review Decision 2.3's lesson, then continue.

**DECISION 4.3 — Receiver Wants an ETA**

> The Des Moines distribution center calls asking when the truck will arrive.

What should you do?

A. Refuse to give any information since they're not "your" customer
B. Provide a reasonable ETA based on what you know about the driver's route and schedule
C. Tell them to call Blue River Foods instead
D. Make up a time so they stop calling

**If you selected B:** Correct. Coordinating with the receiver on delivery timing is a normal, expected dispatcher task — as long as your information is accurate, not guessed (see Stage 6 for what happens when you don't have a real answer).

**If you selected A, C, or D:** Reconsider. Refusing to engage, redirecting unnecessarily, or guessing are all worse choices than simply providing the accurate information you have.

**DECISION 4.4 — Who Physically Moves the Freight?**

A brand-new trainee dispatcher asks you: "Wait, so who actually drives the truck — is that you, or the broker, or who?"

How do you answer?

A. "I do, technically — dispatchers drive too."
B. "The driver does. I coordinate the load; the driver operates the truck."
C. "The broker's driver does."
D. "Nobody drives it, it's all electronic now."

**If you selected B:** Correct — and a good check that you've internalized the core Module 01 distinction.

**If you selected A, C, or D:** These all confuse roles that Module 01 keeps separate. Review Stage 1–2 before continuing.

**Proceed to Stage 5.**

## STAGE 5 — Freight & Document Flow

Delivery day arrives. Use everything you've learned so far to sequence what happens.

**DECISION 5.1 — Sequence the Flow**

Put these events in the correct order:

1. North Star's driver picks up and transports the freight
2. Blue River Foods has freight ready to move
3. Receiver receives shipment; delivery documentation (POD) is created
4. Prairie Freight Brokers arranges transportation with North Star
5. North Star submits its invoice and POD to its factoring company
6. North Star's dispatcher coordinates the carrier-side activity

**Correct sequence:** 2 → 4 → 6 → 1 → 3 → 5

(Blue River has freight ready → Prairie Freight Brokers arranges the move with North Star → the dispatcher coordinates North Star's side → the driver physically transports it → the receiver gets it and POD is generated → North Star submits its invoice and POD to the factoring company.)

**DECISION 5.2 — Four Kinds of Flow**

This sequence actually contains four different *types* of flow happening together. Match each event to its flow type:

| Event | Flow type |
|---|---|
| The driver physically transporting the freight | ? |
| Prairie Freight Brokers arranging the load with North Star | ? |
| The POD being created at delivery | ? |
| North Star submitting its invoice to the factoring company | ? |

*(Flow types: Physical Freight Flow, Communication/Arrangement Flow, Document Flow, Payment/Factoring Flow)*

**Check your answers:**
- Driver transporting freight → **Physical Freight Flow**
- Broker arranging the load → **Communication/Arrangement Flow**
- POD created at delivery → **Document Flow**
- Invoice submitted to factoring company → **Payment/Factoring Flow**

**IMPORTANT:** Factoring is part of *this* fictional scenario because North Star happens to use a factoring company. Not every real trucking transaction involves factoring — don't assume every load works this way.

**Proceed to Stage 6.**

## STAGE 6 — Missing Information Event

Late in the afternoon, a new message arrives:

> **FREIGHT RECORD UPDATE — PFB-40217**
> Pickup location: Blue River Foods, Omaha, NE
> Delivery location: [Regional grocery distribution center — Des Moines, IA]
> Receiver contact name: **[not provided]**
> Dock/appointment instructions: **[not provided]**

You need to give the driver final delivery instructions before he leaves tonight.

**DECISION 6.1 — What Do You Do?**

A. Make a reasonable guess at the dock/appointment details so the driver isn't held up
B. Tell the driver to just show up and figure it out on arrival
C. Recognize that this is missing information you don't have enough to safely assume, and verify it with Prairie Freight Brokers or the receiver before finalizing instructions
D. Use the details from a different load you handled last week, since deliveries are usually similar

**If you selected C:** Correct — and this is the most important lesson in this simulation. Missing information is not permission to guess. A guessed dock number or missing receiver contact can lead to a failed delivery, wasted time, or a frustrated receiver. The correct move is always to verify before proceeding.

**If you selected A, B, or D:** Each of these fills a real gap with something other than verified fact — a guess, an assumption, or borrowed information from an unrelated load. This is exactly the mistake this exercise is built to catch. **STOP → VERIFY → DO NOT ASSUME.** Try Decision 6.1 again with that principle in mind.
