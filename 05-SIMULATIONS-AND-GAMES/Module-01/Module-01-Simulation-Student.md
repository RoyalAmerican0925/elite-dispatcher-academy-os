## Before You Start

You are a newly trained dispatcher working with **North Star Transport**. Today, a new freight opportunity comes across your desk. You'll work through it exactly the way it would unfold at a real dispatch desk — piece by piece, with information arriving as you need it, not all at once.

---

## STAGE 1 — New Freight File

Your dispatch inbox shows a new message:

> **FREIGHT OPPORTUNITY NOTICE**
> From: Prairie Freight Brokers
> To: North Star Transport Dispatch
> Re: Truckload — Omaha, NE to Des Moines, IA
>
> We have a truckload available from Blue River Foods (shelf-stable food products, Omaha, NE) to a regional grocery distribution center in Des Moines, IA. Reference #PFB-40217. Let us know if North Star can cover this.

**SIM01-D01 — Identify the Parties** *(matching)*

Based on this message alone, match each name/party to its role. Write your answers before checking the Stage 1 Feedback section below.

- Blue River Foods
- Prairie Freight Brokers
- North Star Transport
- The grocery distribution center in Des Moines

*(Roles to choose from: Shipper, Broker, Motor Carrier, Consignee/Receiver)*

**SIM01-D02 — Two Roles Not Yet Named** *(choice)*

The notice doesn't mention two more roles you'll need for this load: the **dispatcher** and the **driver**. Who fills each role, based on what you know about North Star Transport's structure? Record your choice before checking below.

A. The dispatcher and driver are both employees of Prairie Freight Brokers
B. The dispatcher coordinates the load for North Star; a North Star driver physically drives the truck
C. The dispatcher is you, and the driver is also you
D. Blue River Foods assigns both roles

---

### Stage 1 Feedback

**SIM01-D01 answer:**
- Blue River Foods = **Shipper** (it manufactures the goods and needs them moved)
- Prairie Freight Brokers = **Broker** (it arranged the transportation; it does not own the freight or the truck)
- North Star Transport = **Motor Carrier** (the company being asked to physically move the freight)
- The Des Moines distribution center = **Consignee/Receiver** (the party the freight is being delivered to)

If you matched all four correctly on your first attempt: good, proceed to Stage 2. If you missed one or more: this is a common beginner mix-up. Remember — the shipper *has* the freight, the broker *arranges* the move without touching the freight, the carrier is *asked to move* the freight, and the receiver is *who gets it*. Re-read the notice above with that in mind before proceeding.

**SIM01-D02 answer: B.** You (the dispatcher) coordinate this load on behalf of North Star Transport — the motor carrier you work for. A separate person, North Star's driver, physically operates the truck.

If you selected A, C, or D: the dispatcher and driver both work in service of the motor carrier (North Star) in this scenario, not the broker — and they are two distinct roles, not the same person or role as each other. Re-read Module 01 Section 2 if this feels unclear.

**Proceed to Stage 2.**

A. They do the same job, just with different names
B. Prairie Freight Brokers arranges transportation without transporting the freight itself; North Star Transport actually transports it
C. Prairie Freight Brokers owns the truck; North Star Transport just handles paperwork

**SIM01-D05 — Where Does the Dispatcher Fit in This Scenario?** *(choice)*

Which statement correctly describes your role as North Star's dispatcher in this specific load? Record your choice before checking below.

A. You are effectively acting as a second broker for this load
B. In this scenario, you are coordinating this load on behalf of North Star Transport, the motor carrier
C. You are legally the same as the driver
D. Your role only exists after delivery, for invoicing purposes

---

### Stage 2 Feedback

**SIM01-D03 answer:**
- Manufactures the food products → **Shipper** (Blue River Foods)
- Arranges transportation → **Broker** (Prairie Freight Brokers)
- Agrees to transport as motor carrier → **Motor Carrier** (North Star Transport)
- Coordinates the load on the carrier's behalf → **Dispatcher** (you)
- Physically drives the truck → **Driver**
- Receives the shipment → **Receiver** (the Des Moines distribution center)
- Provides accelerated payment after invoicing → **Factoring Company** (introduced later in this scenario — see Stage 5)

**SIM01-D04 answer: B.** This is the single most important distinction in Module 01: **arranging** transportation is not the same as **physically transporting** it.

If you selected A, C, or D: this mixes up the two roles. A broker arranges; a carrier transports. Neither owns what the other does. Review Module 01, Section 2.

**SIM01-D05 answer: B.** In this scenario, you are coordinating this load specifically on behalf of North Star Transport. This describes your role in *this* case — it is not a claim that every dispatch arrangement everywhere works identically; a later module covers the deeper legal-boundary analysis between dispatching and brokering in full.

If you selected A, C, or D: reconsider. Your role in this scenario is coordination on behalf of the motor carrier, not equivalent to the broker or the driver.

## STAGE 3 — Carrier Identity Check

Before you can confidently work this load, you pull up North Star Transport's basic carrier record:

> **CARRIER PROFILE CARD — NORTH STAR TRANSPORT**
> Legal Name: North Star Transport LLC
> USDOT Number: 9284471 (fictional, for training only)
> MC Operating Authority: MC-558213 (fictional, for training only) — Property Carrier
> Equipment on file: Dry van, 53-foot

**SIM01-D06 — USDOT Number vs. Operating Authority** *(choice)*

Which statement correctly distinguishes the USDOT number from the MC operating authority shown above? Record your choice before checking below.

A. They're two numbers for the exact same thing — either one proves the same fact
B. A USDOT number and operating authority are distinct regulatory concepts — the USDOT number is used by FMCSA in identifying and tracking a regulated entity, while certain for-hire interstate operations require separate operating authority; the presence of one does not automatically prove the other
C. The USDOT number is only used for drivers, not companies
D. Having a USDOT number automatically means North Star can haul any type of for-hire interstate freight

---

### Stage 3 Feedback

**SIM01-D06 answer: B.** A USDOT number and operating authority are related but distinct — a company can have a USDOT number without that number, by itself, proving it holds the specific operating authority a given operation requires.

If you selected A, C, or D: these each treat the two concepts as interchangeable or treat one as automatic proof of the other, which isn't accurate. Review Module 01, Section 4.

**IMPORTANT — beginner-level only:** This simulation does not go deeper into registration or authority verification procedure. A later module covers the full carrier-verification and broker/authority-verification workflow in depth. Here, you only need to recognize that USDOT identification and operating authority are two distinct concepts.

**Proceed to Stage 4.**

## STAGE 4 — Dispatch Desk Decisions

The load is confirmed. Over the course of the day, several messages and situations reach your desk. Record your answer to each before checking the Stage 4 Feedback section.

**SIM01-D07 — Driver Asks for Pickup Information** *(choice)*

> **DRIVER DISPATCH MESSAGE**
> From: North Star Driver (assigned to this load)
> To: Dispatch
> "Hey, what time am I picking up at Blue River tomorrow, and what's the dock number?"

What should you do?

A. Tell the driver you don't have that information and to figure it out himself
B. Provide the driver the pickup details you have on file for this load, since coordinating this information for the driver is part of your role
C. Tell the driver to call Prairie Freight Brokers directly and handle it himself
D. Ignore the message — that's not your job

**SIM01-D08 — Broker Contacts You About the Load** *(choice)*

> **BROKER CONTACT CARD**
> Prairie Freight Brokers calls: "Just confirming North Star is still good for load PFB-40217, pickup tomorrow morning."

What should you do?

A. Confirm the load is still on, since you're the dispatcher coordinating it for North Star
B. Tell them you're not authorized to speak to brokers at all
C. Refuse to answer because talking to a broker makes you the broker
D. Transfer the call to the driver

**SIM01-D09 — Receiver Wants an ETA** *(choice)*

> The Des Moines distribution center calls asking when the truck will arrive.

What should you do?

A. Refuse to give any information since they're not "your" customer
B. Provide the best verified/current ETA available from the carrier's operational information, or verify before communicating if the information is uncertain
C. Tell them to call Blue River Foods instead
D. Make up a time so they stop calling

**SIM01-D10 — Who Physically Moves the Freight?** *(choice)*

A brand-new trainee dispatcher asks you: "Wait, so who actually drives the truck — is that you, or the broker, or who?"

How do you answer?

A. "I do, technically — dispatchers drive too."
B. "The driver does. I coordinate the load; the driver operates the truck."
C. "The broker's driver does."
D. "Nobody drives it, it's all electronic now."

---

### Stage 4 Feedback

**SIM01-D07 answer: B.** Coordinating operational information between the carrier's driver and the rest of the load is core dispatcher work.

If you selected A, C, or D: reconsider — leaving the driver to sort out pickup details alone, or ignoring the request, isn't consistent with the coordinating role from Stage 2.

**SIM01-D08 answer: A.** Communicating with a broker about a load your carrier is handling is a normal, expected part of the dispatcher's coordinating role in this scenario — it does not, by itself, make you a broker. This doesn't mean broker communication is never relevant to a brokerage analysis in general; actual brokerage analysis depends on the full relationship and conduct involved, which a later module covers in depth. Here, simply confirming a load status with a broker for your own carrier isn't what crosses that line.

If you selected B, C, or D: this overcorrects. Communicating with the broker about your own carrier's load is exactly the kind of coordination a dispatcher does.

**SIM01-D09 answer: B.** Coordinating with the receiver on delivery timing is a normal, expected dispatcher task — but only using accurate, verified information, not a guess (see Stage 6 for what happens when you don't have a real answer).

If you selected A, C, or D: refusing to engage, redirecting unnecessarily, or inventing a time are all worse choices than providing accurate information or verifying first.

**SIM01-D10 answer: B.** A good check that you've internalized the core Module 01 distinction.

If you selected A, C, or D: these all confuse roles that Module 01 keeps separate. Review Stage 1–2.

**Proceed to Stage 5.**

## STAGE 5 — Freight & Document Flow

Delivery day arrives. Record your answers before checking the Stage 5 Feedback section.

**SIM01-D11 — Sequence the Flow** *(matching/sequencing)*

Put these events in the correct order:

1. North Star's driver picks up and transports the freight
2. Blue River Foods has freight ready to move
3. Receiver receives shipment; delivery documentation (POD) is created
4. Prairie Freight Brokers arranges transportation with North Star
5. North Star submits its invoice and POD to its factoring company
6. North Star's dispatcher coordinates the carrier-side activity

**SIM01-D12 — Four Kinds of Flow** *(matching)*

This sequence actually contains four different *types* of flow happening together. Match each event to its flow type:

| Event | Flow type |
|---|---|
| The driver physically transporting the freight | ? |
| Prairie Freight Brokers arranging the load with North Star | ? |
| The POD being created at delivery | ? |
| North Star submitting its invoice to the factoring company | ? |

*(Flow types: Physical Freight Flow, Communication/Arrangement Flow, Document Flow, Payment/Factoring Flow)*

---

### Stage 5 Feedback

**SIM01-D11 answer:** 2 → 4 → 6 → 1 → 3 → 5 (Blue River has freight ready → Prairie Freight Brokers arranges the move with North Star → the dispatcher coordinates North Star's side → the driver physically transports it → the receiver gets it and POD is generated → North Star submits its invoice and POD to the factoring company.)

**SIM01-D12 answer:**
- Driver transporting freight → **Physical Freight Flow**
- Broker arranging the load → **Communication/Arrangement Flow**
- POD created at delivery → **Document Flow**
- Invoice submitted to factoring company → **Payment/Factoring Flow**

Some events reasonably touch more than one flow type — for example, the invoice submission is both a document action and a payment action. If your answer reflects a justified reading like that, it can be an acceptable answer; this classification isn't meant to pretend these categories never overlap in practice.

**IMPORTANT:** Factoring is part of *this* fictional scenario because North Star happens to use a factoring company. Not every real trucking transaction involves factoring — don't assume every load works this way.

**Proceed to Stage 6.**

## STAGE 6 — Missing Information Event

Late in the afternoon, a new message arrives:

> **FREIGHT RECORD UPDATE — PFB-40217**
> Pickup location: Blue River Foods, Omaha, NE
> Delivery location: [Regional grocery distribution center — Des Moines, IA]
> Receiver contact name: **[not provided]**
> Dock/appointment instructions: **[not provided]**

You need to give the driver final delivery instructions before he leaves tonight.

**SIM01-D13 — What Do You Do?** *(choice)*

Record your answer before checking the Stage 6 Feedback section.

A. Make a reasonable guess at the dock/appointment details so the driver isn't held up
B. Tell the driver to just show up and figure it out on arrival
C. Recognize that this is missing information you don't have enough to safely rely on, and verify it with Prairie Freight Brokers or the receiver before finalizing instructions
D. Use the details from a different load you handled last week, since deliveries are usually similar

---

### Stage 6 Feedback

**SIM01-D13 answer: C.** This is the most important lesson in this simulation. When required operational information is missing or uncertain, do not invent it — verify the information through an appropriate source before relying on it. A guessed dock number or missing receiver contact can lead to a failed delivery, wasted time, or a frustrated receiver.

If you selected A, B, or D: each of these fills a real gap with something other than verified fact — a guess, an assumption, or borrowed information from an unrelated load. **STOP → VERIFY → DO NOT ASSUME.**
