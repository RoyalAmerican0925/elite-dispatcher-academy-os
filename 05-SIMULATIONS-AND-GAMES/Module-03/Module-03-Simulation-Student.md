# SIM-003 — Module 03 Load Compatibility Desk

**Student Simulation**  
**Module:** 03 — Trucks, Trailers, Freight & Equipment  
**Build status:** PRODUCT PACKAGE DRAFT — synchronized to current SIM-003 runtime; release QA deferred

## Purpose
This simulation places you at a load-compatibility desk. Your job is not to make a load fit because it looks profitable or convenient. Your job is to determine whether the information actually supports movement with the carrier, driver and equipment combination in front of you.

You will work through equipment facts, commodity requirements, temperature-control needs, dimensions, weight and axle conditions, driver qualifications, and incomplete information.

## Operating rule
Use this sequence throughout the simulation:

**CHECK EQUIPMENT → COMMODITY → TEMPERATURE → DIMENSIONS → WEIGHT/AXLES → DRIVER QUALIFICATIONS → MISSING INFORMATION → VERIFY → PROCEED / REJECT / ESCALATE**

Do not replace missing facts with assumptions.

## Decision classifications
Use the classification that best matches the evidence available:

- **GOOD FIT** — the supplied facts support compatibility at the level being tested.
- **BAD FIT** — a supplied fact creates a direct incompatibility.
- **NEEDS MORE INFORMATION** — a material fact is missing and must be verified before the decision can be completed.
- **SPECIALIZED / ESCALATE** — the load presents a specialized condition requiring additional jurisdictional, permitting, equipment, qualification or management review before proceeding.

These classifications are training controls. They do not replace carrier policy, current law, permit requirements, equipment specifications, shipper instructions or qualified professional review.

## Stage 1 — Equipment File
You receive this carrier/equipment profile:

- 53-foot dry van
- 102-inch legal-width configuration
- no temperature-control capability
- driver with ordinary dry-van experience
- no H or X hazardous-material endorsement on file

### Your task
Separate what is actually **KNOWN** from what still requires load-specific verification. Do not infer the specific trailer's usable interior dimensions or actual current tare weight merely from the nominal trailer description.

## Stage 2 — Standard Dry-Van Load
**Load A**
- packaged home goods
- 31,000 lb
- 96 inches wide
- standard dock loading
- no temperature control required
- no special handling stated

### Your task
Classify the load for the equipment profile and identify the facts that support your decision. Do not use gross weight alone as proof of complete legality or compatibility.

## Stage 3 — Temperature-Control Conflict
**Load B**
- frozen food
- continuous -10°F requirement
- refrigerated equipment requested

### Your task
Determine whether the equipment on file can satisfy the stated requirement and select the correct operational response. A missing reefer unit is an equipment-capability problem, not an invitation to invent a workaround.

## Stage 4 — Overwidth / Specialized Load
**Load C**
- rigid industrial machinery
- 104 inches wide
- permit information not provided
- route/state information not provided

### Your task
Do not classify the load as automatically legal or automatically illegal from the width figure alone. Identify the additional jurisdiction, route, permit-process and carrier-readiness information that must be verified.

## Stage 5 — Driver / Endorsement Event
**Load D**
- placarded hazardous material
- the scenario states that an H or X endorsement is required
- assigned driver has no H or X endorsement on file

### Your task
Determine whether this driver may be dispatched on this specific fictional load and distinguish H, N and X rather than treating the endorsements as interchangeable.

**Critical decision:** approving the fictional Load D for the stated driver despite the stated missing required qualification is a critical failure in this simulation.

## Stage 6 — Weight / Axle Trap
**Load E**
- projected gross combination weight: 79,400 lb
- projected drive tandem: 35,000 lb

### Your task
Evaluate gross and axle conditions separately. A projected gross figure below an 80,000-lb baseline does not by itself establish legal movement.

**Critical decision:** approving the fictional Load E solely because projected gross is below 80,000 lb while ignoring the stated tandem condition is a critical failure.

## Stage 7 — Incomplete Load File
**Load F**

Shipper note: **“53-foot van, 44,500 lb.”**

Not supplied:
- actual equipment tare weight;
- axle/load distribution information;
- commodity details;
- freight dimensions;
- loading method/facility details.

### Your task
Identify the missing information that controls compatibility, choose the correct operational disposition, and sequence the safe response:

**STOP → IDENTIFY → VERIFY → DO NOT ASSUME**

## Student debrief
After completing the simulation, be able to explain:

1. Why equipment labels are starting information rather than proof of exact unit capability.
2. Why commodity and temperature requirements must be checked against actual equipment capability.
3. Why dimensions can trigger specialized verification instead of an automatic yes/no conclusion.
4. Why driver qualifications are part of load compatibility.
5. Why gross and axle conditions must be evaluated separately.
6. Why missing information should produce verification—not guessing.
7. What information you would document before handing a questionable load to a carrier decision-maker, safety function, permit specialist or other appropriate party.
