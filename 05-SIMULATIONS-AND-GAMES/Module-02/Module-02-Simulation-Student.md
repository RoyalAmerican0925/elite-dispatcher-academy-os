## Stage 1 — Who Do You Represent?

> **Carrier Representation Profile**  
> Represented carrier: Meridian Line Freight LLC  
> Relationship: ongoing, preexisting agreement, continuing basis  
> Equipment: 53-foot dry van  
> Standing lanes: Midwest regional  
> Your role: source and coordinate loads for Meridian Line Freight only

### SIM02-D01 — Who You Represent

Who do you represent in this scenario?

A. Central Plains Brokerage  
B. Meridian Line Freight LLC  
C. Whichever carrier offers the best rate  
D. The shipper whose freight is being moved

**Feedback:** **B.** The represented principal is Meridian Line Freight LLC, the carrier with the ongoing relationship.

### SIM02-D02 — Known vs. Unknown Facts

Select every fact currently known:

- The relationship with Meridian is ongoing and preexisting.
- Meridian's equipment type and standing lanes.
- Whether today's specific load was offered to Meridian alone.
- Whether another carrier was considered for today's load.

**Feedback:** The first two are known. The last two remain unknown because today's specific opportunity has not yet been presented.

## Stage 2 — First Freight Opportunity

> **Broker Load Email**  
> From: Central Plains Brokerage  
> To: Dispatch (Meridian Line Freight)  
> “We have a dry van load matching Meridian's lanes and equipment. Offering it to you directly for Meridian. Interested?”

### SIM02-D03 — Classify This Opportunity

A. Consistent with Carrier-Side Agency  
B. More Facts Required  
C. Significant Brokerage Risk

**Feedback:** **A.** On the supplied facts, the opportunity is offered for one represented carrier and no competing-carrier allocation decision is required.

### SIM02-D04 — Supporting Facts

Select the facts that support the classification:

- The load was offered directly for one specific represented carrier.
- No choice among competing carriers was required.
- A broker was involved in the communication.
- The load pays more than average.

**Feedback:** The first two. Broker involvement and rate level do not independently decide the classification.

## Stage 3 — Shipper-Direct Request

> **Shipper-Direct Email**  
> From: Harlow Manufacturing  
> “We have three loads this week. You work with several carriers, right? Just pick whichever of your carriers can take each one and let us know.”

### SIM02-D05 — Source and Control

A. Broker supplied it; shipper selects carrier.  
B. Shipper supplied it directly; dispatcher would select among carriers.  
C. Meridian supplied it; no selection needed.  
D. Source is unclear.

**Feedback:** **B.** TRACE identifies the shipper as the source and the dispatcher as the proposed carrier selector.

### SIM02-D06 — Classify This Situation — Critical

A. Consistent with Carrier-Side Agency  
B. More Facts Required; no concern yet  
C. Significant Brokerage Risk because the dispatcher would allocate shipper-supplied freight among represented carriers

**Feedback:** **C.** The supplied facts show discretionary carrier allocation for shipper freight. Do not proceed as ordinary carrier-side dispatching. Hold/decline or escalate for appropriate authority/review.

### SIM02-D07 — Risk Factors

Select the material risk facts:

- The shipper contacted the dispatch operation directly.
- Multiple loads are involved.
- The dispatcher would choose which carrier receives each load.
- The request arrived by email.

**Feedback:** The first and third. The number of loads and communication channel are not controlling facts.

## Stage 4 — Multi-Carrier Decision

> Meridian Line Freight — dry van — Midwest  
> Falcon Point Transport — dry van — Midwest  
> Redline Freight Co. — dry van — Midwest

### SIM02-D08 — Overlapping Carriers

A. Representing all three is automatically safe.  
B. Multiple representation is not itself the controlling issue; risk arises when the dispatcher exercises discretion allocating a specific opportunity among competing carriers.  
C. More than one carrier is automatically prohibited.  
D. Overlap itself automatically proves brokerage.

**Feedback:** **B.** Do not create a carrier-count or overlap safe harbor. Analyze the actual freight opportunity and selection discretion.

> **Non-Overlapping Roster**  
> Meridian Line Freight — dry van — Midwest only  
> Summit Ridge Tank Lines — tank — Gulf Coast only

### SIM02-D09 — Non-Overlapping Carriers

A. Consistent with Carrier-Side Agency on the supplied facts because the stated non-overlap removes a competing-carrier choice for this specific comparison.  
B. Significant Brokerage Risk because representing two carriers is inherently risky.  
C. More Facts Required solely because compensation is unknown.

**Feedback:** **A.** Non-overlap is relevant evidence because the supplied facts do not create a choice between these carriers for the same freight. **It is not an automatic legal safe harbor.** The complete relationship and conduct still matter.

## Stage 5 — Rejected Load Event

> Meridian: “Can't cover this one — driver's out of hours. You'll have to let the broker know.”

### SIM02-D10 — What Do You Do? — Critical

The load originally came from a broker for Meridian specifically.

A. Return the opportunity to the broker and report that Meridian cannot cover it.  
B. Reassign it to another represented carrier without returning it.  
C. Hold it while deciding later.  
D. Give it to whichever carrier pays the highest dispatch fee.

**Feedback:** **A.** Returning an opportunity to its source is materially different from independently selecting another carrier for it.

### SIM02-D11 — Why Redirecting Is Risky

Select all that apply:

- The dispatcher would select another carrier for freight not offered to that carrier directly.
- Choosing based on dispatch compensation adds a self-interested allocation motive.
- It might deliver faster.
- The broker may not notice.

**Feedback:** The first two. Speed and concealment do not resolve the regulatory-role question.

## Stage 6 — Contract Label Event

> Agreement title: “Bona Fide Agent Agreement.”

### SIM02-D12 — Does the Label Settle It?

A. Yes; label controls.  
B. No; label is relevant evidence, but actual conduct must be analyzed.  
C. No; written agreements are always irrelevant.  
D. Yes, if notarized.

**Feedback:** **B.** Labels neither automatically establish nor erase the operational relationship.

> **Actual Practice**  
> A shipper sends freight to the dispatch business first. The dispatch business later chooses which unrelated represented carrier will move it.

### SIM02-D13 — Classify the Actual Conduct — Critical

A. Consistent with Carrier-Side Agency because the contract label settles it.  
B. Significant Brokerage Risk because the supplied conduct involves shipper-first freight followed by discretionary carrier selection.  
C. More Facts Required only because the contract needs more detail.  
D. Cannot be analyzed without a lawyer physically present.

**Feedback:** **B.** Actual conduct cannot be overridden by the label.

## Stage 7 — Final Boundary Decision

> **Freight Opportunity Sheet — Incomplete**  
> Known: the dispatcher has an ongoing carrier relationship.  
> Unknown: whether this load was offered specifically for that carrier and whether the dispatcher had any role selecting among carriers.

### SIM02-D14 — What Is the Correct Response?

A. Assume it is safe because there is an ongoing relationship.  
B. Assume it is risky because missing facts probably indicate brokerage.  
C. Use the full workflow: STOP; IDENTIFY the principal and missing facts; TRACE the opportunity; CHECK/VERIFY evidence; CLASSIFY as insufficient verified information while the gap remains; ACT by HOLD/VERIFY; DOCUMENT the unresolved facts; ESCALATE if qualified review is required.  
D. Guess based on the most common answer in the simulation.

**Feedback:** **C.** Material uncertainty is a valid professional outcome. Do not convert missing facts into permission to proceed or into an unsupported accusation.

### SIM02-D15 — Sequence the Complete Response

Place these in order:

1. **STOP** — do not proceed on an incomplete fact pattern.  
2. **IDENTIFY** — identify the represented principal and material gaps.  
3. **TRACE** — trace the freight opportunity to its source and determine whether a specific carrier was already tied to it.  
4. **CHECK** — verify the missing facts and evidence.  
5. **CLASSIFY** — classify from verified facts; preserve “insufficient verified information” while a material gap remains.  
6. **ACT** — PROCEED, HOLD, VERIFY, ESCALATE, or DO NOT PROCEED AS PROPOSED according to the verified facts.  
7. **DOCUMENT** — record facts, unknowns, reasoning, action, and follow-up owner.  
8. **ESCALATE** — make a specific professional handoff when qualified review is required.

**Correct sequence:**  
**STOP → IDENTIFY → TRACE → CHECK → CLASSIFY → ACT → DOCUMENT → ESCALATE**
