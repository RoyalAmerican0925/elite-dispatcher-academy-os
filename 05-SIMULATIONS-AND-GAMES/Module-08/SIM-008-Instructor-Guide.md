# SIM-008 — Broker Negotiation Lab
## Instructor Guide

**Module:** 08 — Rate Negotiation & Broker Communication  
**Commercial build role:** simulation facilitation, evidence control, remediation, critical reassessment and competency review.

## Instructional intent

SIM-008 is designed to test occupational judgment under progressive negotiation pressure. The learner should not be rewarded for simply choosing the highest rate, refusing every risk or memorizing scripts. Strong performance means the learner can use verified facts, ask useful questions, make a defensible counter, trade conditionally, preserve authority, classify agreement state correctly and hand the negotiation to Module 09 without inventing booking completion.

## Required facilitation controls

1. Release evidence progressively. Do not reveal later-stage facts early.
2. Preserve first-attempt evidence. Do not overwrite the learner's first response after coaching.
3. Separate feedback from answer-key leakage. Explain the principle that failed; do not simply tell the learner which button to click.
4. Treat all rates and companies as fictional instructional case data.
5. Keep Module 08 boundaries intact. Module 07 owns profitability mathematics; Module 09 owns formal booking/rate-confirmation review; Module 11 owns detailed HOS feasibility; Module 13 owns deeper detention/TONU exception handling; Module 14 owns billing/factoring/closeout.

## 12-stage facilitation arc

### 1. Case Intake & Position Build
Look for: verified/supplied inputs, separation of target/floor/asking rate, rejection of unrelated historical noise.

### 2. Missing-Fact Verification & Truthful Opening
Look for: VERIFY behavior and accurate equipment representation.

### 3. Discovery & Broker-Statement Analysis
Look for: distinction between broker statement and established fact; purposeful question rather than accusation or auto-concession.

### 4. Counter & Truthful Leverage
Look for: concise/evidence-supported counter; no fabricated competing offer; no unnecessary disclosure of internal economics.

### 5. Conditional Trade & Agreement-Specific Terms
Look for: something-for-something trade logic and the four-part term control: TRIGGER → AMOUNT/METHOD → EVIDENCE → PROCESS.

### 6. Vague Terms & Floor Discipline
Look for: vague assurance remains unresolved; exact-floor rate is distinguished from below-floor rate.

### 7. Material Change & Position Recalibration
Look for: re-evaluation after changed facts without inventing a new floor or new authority.

### 8. Broker Pressure & Professional Resistance
Look for: composure under urgency and silence; no self-negotiation simply to fill the pause.

### 9. Authority Boundary & Escalation
Look for: below-floor decline/escalate and refusal to promise unspecified operating conditions outside supplied authority.

### 10. Closeout Classification & Negotiation Record
Look for accurate three-bucket classification: AGREED IN PRINCIPLE / UNRESOLVED / NOT ESTABLISHED.

### 11. Agreement-in-Principle & Module 09 Handoff
Look for: no false `BOOKED` state; accurate transfer of both resolved and unresolved items.

### 12. Professional Decline & Final Disposition
Look for: professional decline when the carrier cannot perform the separate opportunity and accurate final state for the primary negotiation.

## Exactly six critical-failure families

No additional critical family may be invented in delivery or scoring without formal architecture reconciliation.

- **CF-01 — Unauthorized below-floor acceptance.** Learner knowingly accepts below supplied minimum/floor without required authorization.
- **CF-02 — Fabricated leverage.** Learner invents competing offers, market facts, truck/driver facts or other leverage.
- **CF-03 — Material fact misrepresentation.** Learner materially misrepresents carrier/truck/driver/load facts.
- **CF-04 — Unauthorized carrier commitment.** Learner commits the carrier outside supplied authority.
- **CF-05 — False agreement state.** Learner treats an unresolved material term as agreed.
- **CF-06 — Premature booking representation.** Learner represents agreement in principle as completed booking before Module 09 controls.

## Critical-failure remediation protocol

A critical failure is an event, not merely a wrong answer count.

When triggered:
1. preserve the first triggering response, timestamp and failure family;
2. explain the occupational principle violated;
3. allow the main scenario to continue so the student can still produce additional evidence;
4. do not clear the critical history because the learner clicks the correct answer on the original screen;
5. after the main scenario, require the approved materially different reassessment case for that family;
6. record reassessment response separately;
7. mark the family resolved only after correct performance on the materially different case.

The original failure must remain visible in the final record even after resolution.

## Approved materially different reassessments

Use only the separate reassessment cases defined in runtime content and the Scenario & Evidence Packet:
- CF-01 uses a new $2,450/$2,425 rate-authority case.
- CF-02 uses a different no-competing-offer leverage prompt.
- CF-03 uses a no-liftgate equipment fact.
- CF-04 uses a weekend-layover commitment request.
- CF-05 uses a new rate/TONU/layover closeout classification.
- CF-06 uses a new verbal-agreement/no-reviewed-rate-confirmation status case.

## Scoring gate

The browser runtime preserves:
- first-attempt correct count out of 24;
- scaled practical score out of 30;
- final mastery count;
- remediation count;
- competency status by M08-C01 through M08-C15;
- critical-failure status by CF-01 through CF-06;
- reassessment evidence;
- final instructor-review/completion state.

Commercial completion requires:
- scaled first-attempt score **24/30 or higher**;
- final mastery evidence across the 24 decisions or approved reassessment equivalent;
- zero unresolved critical failures.

If the raw scaled score is below 24/30, the runtime should require instructor review even when later remediation produces final mastery.

## Coaching boundaries

Appropriate coaching:
- “What fact are you relying on?”
- “Is that term actually specific enough to document as agreed?”
- “What authority was supplied?”
- “What changed when the appointment changed?”
- “What belongs in Module 08 versus Module 09?”

Inappropriate coaching:
- giving the correct rate/status before the learner reasons through the evidence;
- telling the learner to choose a specific option letter;
- treating fabricated leverage as acceptable because it is common in informal negotiation culture;
- inventing a universal detention/TONU amount;
- teaching that a verbal rate agreement automatically equals a completed booking.

## Instructor completion statement

After reviewing the runtime summary and any reassessment evidence, the instructor may record one of:
- **COMPETENCY GATE CLEARED**
- **COMPETENCY GATE CLEARED AFTER REASSESSMENT**
- **INSTRUCTOR REVIEW / ADDITIONAL REMEDIATION REQUIRED**

Do not use `PUBLICATION_READY` as a student performance status. Publication/release status belongs to product QA, not learner competency.
