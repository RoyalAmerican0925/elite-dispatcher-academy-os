# SIM-007 — Rate & Profitability Decision Desk
## Student Instructions

**Module:** 07 — Rate Mathematics & Profitability  
**Purpose:** demonstrate that you can turn load-board numbers into a defensible carrier-facing economic recommendation without confusing rate, loaded RPM, contribution margin, cash-flow speed or possible accessorial revenue with actual profit.

> All companies, rates, percentages and fuel prices in this simulation are fictional instructional assumptions. They are not current market-rate claims.

## What you will do

You will work through one progressive dispatch period across **10 stages and 33 occupational decisions**. Evidence arrives in layers. Later facts may require you to recalculate an earlier result.

The operating sequence is:

**RECEIVE LOAD DATA → ISOLATE KNOWN / UNKNOWN / ASSUMED INPUTS → CALCULATE TOTAL TRIP MILES → CALCULATE LOADED + EFFECTIVE RPM → ESTIMATE FUEL → APPLY VERIFIED FEES/COSTS → VERIFY ACCESSORIAL TREATMENT → CALCULATE AND LABEL MARGIN → WEIGH POSITIONING/CONSTRAINTS → COMPARE OPTIONS → TAKE / COUNTER / HOLD FOR DATA / DECLINE → RECORD BASIS**

## Core doctrine

- **Rate is not profit.**
- **Loaded RPM is not effective RPM.**
- **Contribution margin is not net profit.**
- **A missing input is not zero.**
- **Possible accessorial revenue is not earned revenue.**

## Calculation rules

Use total trip miles whenever the case requires total-trip economics.

- Loaded RPM = revenue ÷ loaded miles
- Effective RPM = relevant revenue ÷ total trip miles
- Gallons = total trip miles ÷ carrier MPG
- Estimated fuel cost = gallons × fictional instructional fuel price
- Contribution margin = revenue minus **only** the costs explicitly included in the exercise
- Counter threshold = target effective RPM × total trip miles

Round final displayed RPM, gallons and dollar figures as directed in the runtime. Do not silently round an unknown into zero.

## Input-state discipline

A value can be:
- **known/usable**;
- **unknown/not yet provided**;
- **assumed**;
- or irrelevant to the current calculation.

A legitimate confirmed zero is different from an unknown. If a material unknown could reverse a recommendation, **HOLD FOR DATA** can be the correct professional answer.

## Margin language

When the exercise subtracts only fuel, tolls and dispatcher fee, the result is an:

**estimated contribution margin under stated included-cost assumptions**

It is not automatically net profit, accounting profit, guaranteed profit or take-home pay. Driver pay, insurance, equipment payment, maintenance, permits, taxes and other overhead may be outside that calculation unless explicitly included.

## Accessorial treatment

A written detention or layover clause is not automatically earned revenue. Before counting an accessorial, confirm that the event was triggered, documentation supports it and the actual load-specific terms apply.

## Cash flow versus profitability

Quick-pay or factoring may move cash faster, but a fee reduces the economics of the load. Faster cash is not the same thing as higher profit.

## Positioning

Destination positioning can matter to the carrier, but it is a planning factor—not guaranteed future revenue. Keep qualitative positioning separate from the current load’s dollar calculation.

## Critical-failure controls

Five behaviors require materially different reassessment before competency completion:

1. **CF-01 Unsupported revenue** — adds unearned/unverified accessorial revenue to current/guaranteed revenue.
2. **CF-02 Profit mislabeling** — represents partial-cost margin as guaranteed profit/net profit/take-home.
3. **CF-03 Loaded-mile blindness** — makes the economic choice from loaded RPM/headline rate while bypassing material total-mile evidence.
4. **CF-04 Fabricated input** — converts a material unknown into zero/assumed fact without verification or disclosure.
5. **CF-05 Carrier-authority override** — represents the dispatcher recommendation as the carrier’s final decision.

If you trigger one, your first critical response remains in the record. Clicking the correct answer afterward on the original screen does not erase it. You must later demonstrate the competency using a different fact pattern.

## Final professional standard

A strong dispatcher gives the carrier a clear recommendation and the economic basis for it while preserving the carrier’s final authority. Your job is not to force a decision; your job is to make the decision easier to make correctly.
