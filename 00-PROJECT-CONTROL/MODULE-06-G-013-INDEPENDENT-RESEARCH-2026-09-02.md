# Module 06 — G-013 Independent Research & Occupational Verification

**Module:** 06 — Broker Verification & Fraud Prevention  
**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Gate:** G-013 independent research / regulatory and occupational verification  
**Status:** RESEARCH PASS COMPLETE — REPOSITORY RECONCILIATION NEXT  

## 1. Research purpose
This pass independently tests what a commercially serious dispatcher-training module must teach before Module 06 production is unlocked. It is not a rewrite of the existing curriculum and does not by itself make Module 06 production-complete.

The occupational question is not merely “Can the student recognize a scam?” It is:

> Can the student verify a broker/opportunity through independent evidence, separate entity legitimacy from contact identity and transaction legitimacy, preserve the evidence trail, classify unresolved risk without making unsupported accusations, and stop/escalate a transaction when verification fails?

## 2. Research verdict
The existing Academy spine remains sound:

**VERIFY THE ENTITY → VERIFY THE CONTACT → VERIFY THE LOAD → VERIFY THE PAYMENT PATH**

Research supports expanding it into an evidence-controlled desk workflow:

**RECEIVE → ISOLATE CLAIMS → VERIFY ENTITY → AUTHENTICATE CONTACT → CROSS-CHECK LOAD/DOCUMENTS → VERIFY PAYMENT PATH → RECORD EVIDENCE → CLASSIFY RISK → PROCEED / VERIFY / HOLD / ESCALATE / DECLINE**

The paid-training value must come from the sequence, evidence discipline, decision language, realistic ambiguity, reusable records, remediation and simulation—not from a list of fraud red flags available on the web.

## 3. Primary-source findings

### A. Entity legitimacy is not contact authentication
FMCSA's fraud guidance specifically recommends confirming broker/carrier phone numbers through SAFER and, when a supplied number does not match the displayed number, calling the number posted in SAFER to discuss the load. FMCSA also warns that even an employee's identity may be stolen.

**Instructional consequence:** a valid broker/MC/company record cannot be taught as proof that an inbound email, phone number, person, domain or payment instruction is authorized.

### B. Search-engine results are not a trusted callback source by themselves
FMCSA warns that top search results may be fake profiles created by scammers and says not to trust that information unless it can be confirmed on multiple sites.

**Instructional consequence:** students need source hierarchy and trusted-callback training, not “Google the phone number.”

### C. Document examination is an operational verification task
FMCSA states that document examination is critical and notes that insurance certificates can be fraudulent; where something appears wrong, the numbers should be researched and the companies called.

**Instructional consequence:** the student must cross-check documents against independently obtained entity/contact facts instead of treating polished paperwork as self-authenticating.

### D. FMCSA identifies transaction-level stop indicators
FMCSA says to stop a transaction in examples including being asked to misrepresent the carrier/driver identity, questionable “blind load” handling, unusually easy agreement to higher pay, or rates far above current market conditions.

**Instructional consequence:** red flags are not all equal. Some observations create a VERIFY task; supplied high-risk transaction facts can create a STOP/HOLD condition. The course must teach the difference.

### E. Evidence preservation matters after a fraud event
FMCSA recommends confirming the truck/carrier identity and preserving tractor/trailer plate information and pictures where relevant, noting their importance for law-enforcement follow-up after fraudulent loads.

**Instructional consequence:** evidence logging is an employable operational skill, not optional paperwork.

### F. Current registration environment changed in 2026
FMCSA identifies Motus as its current registration environment. Public users can search an entity's registration record in Motus. FMCSA states that, as of May 14, 2026, legacy Licensing & Insurance is available only for reviewing historical motor-carrier records and Motus filings are not reflected there.

**Instructional consequence:** Module 06 must not teach legacy L&I as the single current source of truth for 2026 registration activity. Students must understand source purpose and currentness.

### G. Broker financial-responsibility baseline and distinct clocks
Current 49 CFR § 387.307, effective January 16, 2026, requires a broker surety bond or trust fund of **$75,000**. BMC-84 is the prescribed surety-bond form; BMC-85 is the prescribed trust-fund form.

Trust-fund assets under § 387.307(b) must aggregate to $75,000 and be capable of liquidation to cash within **7 calendar days**.

Separate provisions in § 387.307(e) use **7 business days** for specified broker responses concerning claim validity / financial-failure determinations. These are not the same clock.

Current FMCSA guidance also states that, when FMCSA determines a BMC-85 provider is ineligible under the updated requirements, affected brokers/freight forwarders relying on that trust have **30 days** to obtain a compliant replacement filing before suspension.

**Instructional consequence:** never collapse “7 calendar days,” “7 business days,” and “30 days” into one generic broker-bond rule.

### H. Financial-security status does not resolve private payment disputes
FMCSA's June 26, 2026 guidance says its role is regulatory oversight of required financial security, not serving as an intermediary in the merits of individual financial-security/payment disputes.

**Instructional consequence:** students must not teach or infer that FMCSA registration/financial-security information proves whether a specific invoice will be paid, proves fraud, or resolves a private commercial dispute.

## 4. Occupational workflow the module must teach

### Step 1 — Receive and isolate claims
Separate what the inbound party claims from what independent sources establish:
- legal/business identity claimed;
- MC/USDOT identifiers claimed;
- contact identity and role claimed;
- load facts claimed;
- document facts claimed;
- payment/remittance facts claimed.

### Step 2 — Verify the entity
Use authoritative/current public registration information appropriate to the fact being checked. Record source, date/time and result. Do not convert “entity exists” into “contact authenticated.”

### Step 3 — Authenticate the contact
Compare supplied contact information against independently obtained trusted information. Where appropriate, perform a trusted callback using a number obtained independently rather than relying on the suspicious/inbound message itself.

### Step 4 — Cross-check the load and documents
Compare names, identifiers, addresses, phone numbers, domains, pickup/delivery facts, commodity, rate, reference numbers and other supplied fields across the opportunity and documents. A mismatch is an evidence event requiring resolution, not automatic proof of criminal conduct.

### Step 5 — Verify the payment path
Treat new or changed payment instructions, remittance destinations or unusual payment requests as separate authentication events. Do not rely on the same inbound channel requesting the change as the only verification channel.

### Step 6 — Record evidence
Record what was checked, source/channel, timestamp, result, unresolved conflict and next action. Preserve material screenshots/documents according to lawful company policy where appropriate.

### Step 7 — Classify risk
Recommended Academy decision family:
- CLEAR
- CLEAR WITH DOCUMENTED QUALIFICATION
- VERIFY
- HOLD
- ESCALATE
- DO NOT USE / DO NOT BOOK

The status must be supported by evidence and unresolved facts, not intuition alone.

### Step 8 — Route the next action
Proceed only within the dispatcher's actual authority and carrier policy. Escalate legal/compliance/fraud allegations rather than making unsupported legal conclusions.

## 5. Beginner failure modes that premium instruction must prevent
1. “The MC number is active, so the email/person is legitimate.”
2. “The email has the company logo, so it is real.”
3. “The rate confirmation looks professional, so it is authentic.”
4. “The first Google result matches, so verification is complete.”
5. “A duplicate posting proves double brokering.”
6. “A factoring/credit warning proves fraud.”
7. “Any mismatch proves a crime.”
8. “No visible phone number means fraud” rather than unresolved verification risk.
9. “BMC-84/BMC-85 means the broker will definitely pay this invoice.”
10. Collapsing the 7-calendar-day asset rule, 7-business-day response rule and 30-day replacement guidance into one deadline.
11. Calling the suspicious inbound number to verify the suspicious inbound caller.
12. Accepting a payment-change request through the same unverified channel that requested the change.
13. Failing to preserve evidence because the transaction was stopped.
14. Treating a red-flag checklist as a fraud verdict instead of a verification trigger.

## 6. Evidence hierarchy for training
Teach students to distinguish:

### Primary/current authority
Current eCFR, current FMCSA registration/Motus information, current FMCSA public records and official agency guidance.

### Independent corroboration
Trusted company contact channels, directly contacted insurers/sureties/financial institutions where appropriate and permitted, contractual/internal carrier records, established business channels.

### Risk signals
Load-board alerts, factoring/credit signals, unusual market/rate conditions, domain/contact anomalies, duplicate postings, document inconsistencies.

Risk signals may justify VERIFY/HOLD/ESCALATE. They do not automatically establish fraud.

## 7. Tool requirements validated by research
The research supports distinct occupational tools, provided Module 05 duplication is controlled:

1. **Broker Verification & Evidence Record** — entity identifiers, authoritative sources, timestamps and findings.
2. **Entity-vs-Contact Authentication Worksheet** — separates valid company record from inbound-person/channel authentication.
3. **Trusted Callback Record** — independently sourced callback channel, person/role reached, result and unresolved issues.
4. **Load & Document Cross-Check Sheet** — field-level comparison and mismatch resolution.
5. **Payment-Instruction Change Verification Record** — isolates and independently verifies changed payment/remittance instructions.
6. **Risk Classification / STOP-HOLD-ESCALATE Decision Record** — evidence, unresolved facts, classification and authorized next action.
7. **Verification Evidence Packet / Escalation Handoff** — compact chronology and evidence list for supervisor/carrier/lawful reporting follow-up.

Module 05's first-pass broker verification worksheet should remain sourcing-stage triage. Module 06 tools must go deeper into authentication, evidence and risk disposition.

## 8. Practical-assessment requirements validated
A premium practical should provide a legitimate broker entity with an unfamiliar contact and multiple pieces of imperfect evidence. The learner must:
- verify current entity facts;
- authenticate or fail to authenticate the contact;
- identify at least one document mismatch;
- handle a changed payment instruction through independent verification;
- correctly treat a factor/credit signal as risk evidence rather than proof;
- correctly treat duplicate/similar postings as verification evidence rather than a fraud verdict;
- build an evidence chronology;
- issue a supported CLEAR / VERIFY / HOLD / ESCALATE / DO NOT USE / DO NOT BOOK decision;
- state exactly what fact would change the disposition.

Critical failures should focus on bypassing high-risk verification controls, not trivia misses.

## 9. SIM-006 requirements validated
**SIM-006 — Broker Verification & Fraud Prevention Desk** should use progressive events and incomplete information.

Recommended spine:
**ENTITY → CONTACT → LOAD → DOCUMENT → PAYMENT PATH → EVIDENCE → RISK CLASSIFICATION → PROCEED / HOLD / ESCALATE / DECLINE**

The simulation should include:
- legitimate entity / impersonated or unverified contact ambiguity;
- conflicting contact channels;
- changing load facts;
- document mismatch;
- payment-redirection event;
- duplicate-posting risk signal;
- factoring/credit risk signal;
- one tempting but unsafe shortcut;
- evidence preservation;
- remediation after incorrect reasoning;
- first-attempt evidence retained after remediation;
- competency result rather than score-only completion.

## 10. Commercial-depth conclusion
A sellable Module 06 cannot be “10 red flags for freight fraud.” Free FMCSA guidance already supplies useful warning signs. Academy value must be the operational system that turns those warnings into repeatable desk behavior:

**claim separation + source hierarchy + trusted callback + field cross-check + payment-path authentication + evidence chronology + calibrated risk classification + escalation + realistic practice + graded practical + simulation + remediation.**

That is the difference between searchable information and occupational training.
