## Verified federal baseline

### $75,000 financial responsibility
Current 49 CFR § 387.307(a) requires a broker to have a $75,000 surety bond or trust fund in effect. BMC-84 is the prescribed surety-bond form; BMC-85 is the prescribed trust-fund form.

### Acceptable BMC-85 trust assets
Current § 387.307(b) requires trust assets aggregating to $75,000 and capable of liquidation to cash within **7 calendar days**. The regulation identifies acceptable assets as cash, qualifying irrevocable letters of credit, and Treasury bonds.

**Teaching control:** this 7-calendar-day period is an **asset-liquidity** rule. It is not the broker's FMCSA notice-response/suspension period.

### Shortfall / immediate-suspension process
Current § 387.307(e) uses **business days** in the claim-response and FMCSA notice/suspension process.

Relevant distinctions include:
- a broker may have 7 business days to respond to certain claim-validity determinations under § 387.307(e)(1);
- after a qualifying surety/financial-institution notification, FMCSA provides notice that operating authority will be suspended within **7 business days** unless qualifying evidence is supplied under § 387.307(e)(5);
- failure to respond to that notice within **7 business days** results in suspension under § 387.307(e)(6).

**Teaching control:** exam/current-regulation wording should use the exact regulatory context. Do not collapse every rule into a generic statement such as “the broker always has seven days to replenish.”

### Trustee/provider eligibility replacement period
FMCSA's current broker financial-responsibility FAQ states that when FMCSA determines a BMC-85 trust provider is ineligible under the updated requirements, affected brokers/freight forwarders have **30 days** to obtain a compliant replacement filing before authority suspension.

This is a different event from the § 387.307(e) financial-security shortfall process.

## Important agency-summary discrepancy
FMCSA's current public **Overview and Compliance Requirements** page contains simplified language stating that if available financial security falls below $75,000 and is not replenished within **7 calendar days**, FMCSA will suspend authority.

The current eCFR text for the FMCSA notice/suspension mechanism in § 387.307(e)(5)–(6), however, expressly uses **7 business days** after service of FMCSA's notice.

### Curriculum control decision
For graded legal/regulatory statements:
1. use the actual current regulatory text and identify the context;
2. distinguish the 7-calendar-day asset-liquidity rule from the 7-business-day notice/response rules;
3. do not use the simplified FMCSA overview sentence to overwrite the current eCFR wording;
4. where a student-facing explanation summarizes the process, avoid implying that every possible shortfall event follows one single seven-day clock.

This is not a criticism of FMCSA guidance. It is a precision control for curriculum that is expected to distinguish multiple regulatory clocks accurately.
