# MODULE 08 — RATE NEGOTIATION AND BROKER COMMUNICATION
## Student Manual — Part 05: Terms Beyond the Headline Rate

## Purpose
A higher headline rate does not automatically mean every material term is acceptable. Professional negotiation requires identifying agreement-specific conditions that can change the carrier's decision and making sure unresolved terms remain visibly unresolved.

---

## 1. Rate is one term, not the whole agreement
Depending on the load and agreement, discussion may involve:
- linehaul or total stated compensation;
- extra stops;
- detention provisions;
- layover provisions;
- truck ordered not used (TONU) provisions;
- lumper/reimbursement process;
- loading/unloading expectations;
- appointment conditions;
- required tracking or communication procedures;
- documentation requirements;
- other load-specific operating/commercial conditions.

This list does not mean every load includes every term.

## 2. Never invent universal accessorial amounts
There is no Academy rule such as “detention is always $X after Y hours.” Commercial terms can vary by agreement, customer, broker, carrier, event and documentation.

The professional question is:
> “What applies to this specific load, what triggers it, and where is it documented?”

Later modules handle exception management and billing/closeout in greater depth.

## 3. Trigger, amount, evidence, process
When an agreement-specific term matters, separate four questions:
1. **Trigger** — what event activates the provision?
2. **Amount/method** — what compensation or method is stated?
3. **Evidence** — what documentation is required?
4. **Process** — how/when must the carrier report or request it?

Knowing only the word “detention” is not enough.

## 4. Material unresolved terms
Broker: “We can do $2,400, but I'll have to check on the extra stop.”

Do not record: `Agreed $2,400 including extra stop.`

Record: `Rate discussed at $2,400; extra-stop compensation unresolved pending broker confirmation.`

A professional record preserves uncertainty.

## 5. Rate increase versus term improvement
Suppose the broker cannot reach the target rate but can change another material condition within the supplied case.

The dispatcher must evaluate the entire supplied decision—not automatically treat any non-rate concession as equal to money.

Example:
- broker cannot increase from $2,300;
- broker can confirm a schedule condition that removes a material uncertainty;
- carrier's supplied decision rule says $2,300 becomes acceptable if that condition is confirmed.

That is a legitimate conditional trade because the case defines why the term matters.

## 6. Verbal statement versus documented term
Negotiation notes capture the conversation. They do not replace Module 09 formal booking/document review.

If a material term is discussed verbally, record it and ensure it enters the proper booking/document workflow. Do not teach students that a phone statement eliminates the need to review the formal document.

## 7. Agreement-Term Drill
Fictional call:
> Broker offers $2,350 for one pickup and two deliveries. The original posting appeared to show one delivery. Broker says, “The second stop is easy; we'll take care of you on it.” Delivery 2 has no confirmed appointment in the information supplied.

Identify:
- what changed;
- which facts are material;
- which terms are unresolved;
- what questions to ask;
- what must be documented in the negotiation record;
- why “we'll take care of you” is not a precise term.

## 8. Do not negotiate away later-module controls
Module 08 may discuss a term. It does not replace:
- Module 09 rate-confirmation/document verification;
- Module 11 HOS/ELD feasibility;
- Module 13 event-specific detention/TONU/exception workflow;
- Module 14 billing/factoring/closeout.

A dispatcher must know when negotiation ends and another workflow begins.

## 9. Part 05 standard
The student can identify material terms beyond headline rate, clarify trigger/amount/evidence/process, distinguish agreement-specific terms from universal rules, preserve unresolved terms, use conditional trades appropriately, and route negotiated points into formal booking/document controls.
