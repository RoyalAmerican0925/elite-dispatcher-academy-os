# MODULE 08 — RATE NEGOTIATION AND BROKER COMMUNICATION
## Student Manual — Part 03: Broker Call Discovery

## Purpose
A negotiation call is not just a rate exchange. It is also an information event. The dispatcher must learn enough to understand the opportunity, expose material unknowns, identify constraints, and avoid making commitments from assumptions.

The Part 03 sequence is:

**OPEN → QUESTION → LISTEN → VERIFY → SUMMARIZE → POSITION**

---

## 1. Open with identity and purpose
A professional opening identifies the carrier-side role and the opportunity being discussed without implying authority or facts that are not supplied.

Example:
> “This is Jordan calling for Northstar Transport about your Dallas-to-Tulsa dry-van load. Is it still available?”

The opening establishes who is calling, for whom, and about what. It does not require a speech.

## 2. Discovery before commitment
A posted load can omit facts that matter to economics or operating fit. Depending on what prior modules have already verified, discovery may include:
- exact pickup/delivery facilities or areas;
- appointment or FCFS details;
- commodity and stated weight;
- equipment or special operating requirements;
- number of stops;
- stated load status;
- compensation currently offered;
- agreement-specific terms that materially affect the decision;
- which information remains subject to formal documentation.

Do not mechanically re-ask facts already reliably established. Ask questions that reduce decision uncertainty.

## 3. Material versus interesting information
A material fact can change fit, economics, authority, timing, or the accept/decline/escalate decision.

Interesting but nonmaterial conversation can consume time without improving the decision.

Ask: **If this answer changes, could our position change?** If yes, it is likely material.

## 4. Listen for constraints
A broker response may reveal a constraint rather than merely an objection.

Examples:
- “Pickup closes at 14:00.” → timing constraint.
- “Customer will not move the appointment.” → stated scheduling constraint.
- “I have approval up to $2,300.” → stated negotiating constraint; do not automatically treat it as independently verified fact.
- “I need a truck within 30 miles.” → stated capacity/location need.

The dispatcher should distinguish **what the broker said** from **what has been independently established**.

## 5. Clarifying vague language
Vague: “It picks up early.”
Useful follow-up: “What is the actual pickup appointment or receiving window?”

Vague: “It's light.”
Useful follow-up: “What weight is stated on the load information?”

Vague: “Detention is standard.”
Useful follow-up: “What detention terms apply to this specific load, and where will those terms be documented?”

Vague: “Send the truck and we'll work it out.”
Useful response: do not convert unresolved material terms into assumptions.

## 6. Listening for the objection beneath the words
Broker: “Your rate is too high.”
Possible meanings include:
- broker has a lower target;
- broker has a hard customer budget;
- broker is testing whether the carrier will move;
- another carrier is available;
- broker simply disagrees with the counter.

Do not invent which one is true. Ask a useful question.

> “Where do you need to be to make this work?”

or

> “Is the issue the total rate, or is there another term you're trying to solve?”

## 7. Do not negotiate against yourself
Dispatcher counters $2,400. Silence follows.

Weak:
> “Okay, maybe $2,300... or $2,250.”

The dispatcher just made multiple concessions without new information.

Stronger: allow the broker to respond. Silence is not evidence that the counter failed.

## 8. Discovery Call Drill
Fictional broker says:
> “I have Dallas to Memphis tomorrow, 53-foot van, 38,000 pounds. Pays $2,100. Pickup is early and delivery is next day. Need somebody dependable.”

Before countering, identify:
1. facts stated;
2. vague statements;
3. material unknowns;
4. five purposeful questions;
5. facts that belong to later formal booking/document control.

## 9. Professional call notes
Record facts as facts and statements as statements.

Weak note: `Broker max is 2300.`

Better note: `10:18 — broker contact stated $2,300 was current maximum available during call.`

The second preserves source and context without converting a negotiation statement into universal truth.

## 10. Part 03 standard
The student can open professionally, ask purposeful questions, identify material unknowns, listen for constraints, clarify vague terms, avoid self-negotiation, and record what was actually said before advancing the negotiation.
