# MODULE 08 — RATE NEGOTIATION AND BROKER COMMUNICATION
## Student Manual — Part 04: Counteroffers & Value-Based Negotiation

## Purpose
A counteroffer should express a controlled carrier position, not a random higher number. The dispatcher uses verified facts, supplied economics, authority limits and professional communication to test whether acceptable terms can be reached.

---

## 1. Anatomy of a useful counter
A strong counter usually contains four elements:
1. **Position** — what you are asking for.
2. **Basis** — concise relevant reason when useful.
3. **Boundary** — what you can or cannot do under supplied authority.
4. **Question** — invites the broker to respond.

Example:
> “With the truck position and total-trip requirement on this one, we're at $2,400. Can you get there?”

Not every counter needs a long justification. Overexplaining can weaken clarity.

## 2. Evidence-supported does not mean argumentative
Useful basis can include actual supplied facts: total-trip economics, verified truck position, schedule burden, a real alternative, or another material operating condition.

Do not turn a carrier's internal requirement into a false statement about what every carrier or “the market” must receive.

## 3. Controlled concession
A concession is movement from an earlier position.

If target is $2,400 and authorized floor is $2,250, the dispatcher should know why and when to move. Do not drop $100 merely because the broker repeats the same offer.

Ask what changed:
- Did the broker move?
- Did a material fact improve?
- Did the broker clarify a term?
- Is the dispatcher trading movement for something of value?

## 4. Conditional trades
A trade connects movement on one point to movement or clarification on another.

Example:
> “If the delivery appointment is confirmed at 08:00 and the rate can move to $2,325, I can continue within the supplied position.”

A conditional trade is not an unconditional promise. Record the condition.

## 5. Multiple-counter sequence
Fictional case:
- offer $2,050;
- target $2,400;
- floor $2,250.

Possible controlled sequence:
- dispatcher: $2,400;
- broker: $2,150;
- dispatcher: $2,350;
- broker: $2,225;
- dispatcher: $2,300;
- broker: $2,250.

If all material terms are acceptable and $2,250 is within supplied authority, the negotiation may reach agreement in principle. The sequence is an example, not a universal formula.

## 6. When not to split the difference
“Meet me halfway” sounds neutral but may ignore the carrier's actual economics and authority.

If broker is at $2,100 and dispatcher floor is $2,300, halfway between a $2,400 counter and $2,100 is $2,250—below supplied authority. Arithmetic symmetry is not decision quality.

## 7. Productive persistence versus friction
Productive:
- asks a new question;
- presents a supported counter;
- clarifies a constraint;
- offers a controlled trade;
- seeks approval when appropriate.

Unproductive:
- repeats the same demand without information;
- insults the broker;
- threatens consequences the carrier cannot impose;
- fabricates urgency or alternatives;
- keeps negotiating after the supplied walk-away condition has been reached.

## 8. Professional decline
A decline can preserve the relationship.

> “I appreciate you checking. At $2,175 it doesn't meet this carrier's supplied position, and I don't have authority to go lower. We'll pass unless the rate changes.”

No lecture is required.

## 9. Counteroffer Lab
For each fictional situation, write:
- opening counter;
- concise basis;
- one discovery question;
- one possible conditional trade;
- decline/escalation condition.

### A
Offer $1,900; target $2,200; floor $2,050; broker says pickup is urgent.

### B
Offer $2,300; target $2,450; floor $2,325; delivery appointment is still unconfirmed and can materially change supplied economics.

### C
Offer $2,150; target $2,350; floor $2,250; a real verified alternative produces a stronger supplied total-trip result.

## 10. Critical distinction
A successful negotiator is not the person who always gets the highest gross rate. A successful dispatcher makes disciplined decisions inside verified facts, supplied carrier economics and authority.

Part 04 standard: construct defensible counters, make controlled concessions, use conditional trades, avoid fabricated leverage, know when not to split the difference, and decline/escalate professionally.
