# MODULE 08 — RATE NEGOTIATION AND BROKER COMMUNICATION
## Student Manual — Part 06: Closing, Documentation & Difficult Conversations

## Purpose
A negotiation is not professionally complete when someone says “sounds good.” The dispatcher must confirm what was discussed, separate resolved from unresolved terms, document the result, choose the correct status, and hand the opportunity into the next workflow.

---

## 1. Closing means reducing ambiguity
Before ending the conversation, summarize the material points.

Example:
> “To confirm our discussion: we're aligned in principle at $2,350 for the stated one-pick/one-delivery movement, pickup tomorrow 10:00–12:00 and delivery appointment Thursday 08:00. The extra-stop issue is not part of this agreement because the load is currently stated as one delivery. Please send the formal rate confirmation for review under our booking process.”

The summary creates a check against misunderstanding. It does not override the formal document.

## 2. Three buckets at close
Classify terms as:

### AGREED IN PRINCIPLE
Both sides verbally aligned during negotiation, pending applicable booking/document controls.

### UNRESOLVED
Still needs clarification, approval or negotiation.

### NOT DISCUSSED / NOT ESTABLISHED
Do not fill this bucket with assumptions after the call.

## 3. Negotiation call record
A professional record should capture:
- date/time;
- carrier/load identifier;
- contact and communication channel;
- verified facts relied upon;
- opening offer;
- counters and concessions;
- conditions/trades;
- material statements made by the broker;
- supplied authority boundary;
- terms aligned in principle;
- unresolved points;
- final negotiation status;
- required next action;
- escalation/approval if applicable.

## 4. Difficult conversation: broker pressures immediate commitment
Broker:
> “I need yes or no right now. I'll send paperwork later.”

If supplied carrier process requires formal booking/document controls, urgency does not erase them.

Response:
> “We're aligned in principle on the negotiated points. I still have to complete our booking/document process before I mark it booked.”

## 5. Difficult conversation: broker changes a fact
Earlier: one delivery.
Later: “Actually there are two stops.”

Do not pretend the original negotiation still covers an unchanged opportunity. Reassess the material change under supplied economics/authority and negotiate or escalate as necessary.

## 6. Difficult conversation: broker challenges your number
Broker:
> “That's ridiculous. Nobody is paying that.”

Do not mirror hostility.

Possible response:
> “I understand we're apart. That number is the supplied position for this carrier on this opportunity. Where can you get to?”

If the conversation no longer has a viable path, decline professionally.

## 7. Difficult conversation: authorization boundary
Broker reaches $2,225. Supplied floor is $2,250.

Correct:
> “I don't have authority below $2,250. I can take $2,225 back for approval.”

Incorrect:
> “Close enough. Send it.”

The $25 difference is not the core issue. Authority is.

## 8. Difficult conversation: broker asks for a false statement
If any party pressures the dispatcher to materially misstate truck position, driver status, identity, equipment, timing or another fact, do not comply. Record/escalate according to the carrier's process.

## 9. Accept / Decline / Escalate
### ACCEPT / AGREED IN PRINCIPLE — PENDING BOOKING CONTROL
Use when negotiated material points meet supplied carrier requirements and authority, while formal Module 09 controls remain pending.

### DECLINE
Use when the opportunity fails supplied requirements or the authorized decision is not to proceed.

### ESCALATE
Use when an otherwise viable decision exceeds supplied authority or a material issue requires higher review.

### VERIFY BEFORE DECISION
Use when missing/conflicting material facts prevent a defensible close.

## 10. Full Desk Case
Fictional carrier supplied position:
- target $2,500;
- floor $2,325;
- below floor requires owner approval;
- one pickup/one delivery assumed only if confirmed;
- formal booking requires Module 09 document review.

Progressive call:
1. Broker offers $2,150.
2. Dispatcher confirms one pickup/one delivery and counters $2,500.
3. Broker moves to $2,250.
4. Dispatcher counters $2,425.
5. Broker states $2,325 is available.
6. Broker then reveals a second delivery stop not in the original information.
7. Broker says, “Just say yes and we'll work out the second stop later.”

Student must:
- identify the material change;
- avoid treating $2,325 as automatically acceptable after facts changed;
- identify what must be re-evaluated;
- document the unresolved second-stop term;
- choose VERIFY / COUNTER / ESCALATE / DECLINE based on supplied follow-up facts;
- avoid calling the load booked.

## 11. Professional-language closeout
Weak: “We got it.”

Strong: “Negotiation aligned in principle at $2,350; formal booking/document review pending.”

Weak: “Broker promised detention.”

Strong: “Broker stated detention applies; trigger, amount and documentation terms must be confirmed in the applicable agreement/document workflow.”

Weak: “I had to take it.”

Strong: “The offer did not meet supplied authority, so I escalated rather than committing the carrier.”

## 12. Module operating standard
A Module 08 graduate should be able to take a verified, economically understood opportunity and either:
- negotiate it into an opportunity that meets supplied carrier requirements;
- identify what remains unresolved and continue appropriately;
- escalate at the authority boundary; or
- walk away professionally.

The final handoff is explicit:

**NEGOTIATION RESULT → DOCUMENTED STATUS → MODULE 09 BOOKING/RATE-CONFIRMATION CONTROL**

Negotiation skill is not measured by how often the student says yes. It is measured by whether the student makes truthful, economically grounded, authorized and documented decisions.
