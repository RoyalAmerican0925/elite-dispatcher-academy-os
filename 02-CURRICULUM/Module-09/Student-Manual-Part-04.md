# Module 09 Student Manual — Part 04
## Authority, Acceptance, Signature & Document Integrity

### Employment outcome
The learner can determine whether the supplied carrier instructions authorize a booking action and can preserve the evidence without fabrication, silent alteration, or false approval history.

## 1. Authority is supplied evidence
Do not assume that "dispatcher" automatically means unlimited power to sign or accept every term. Use the supplied carrier/dispatcher agreement, written carrier instruction, approval record, or scenario authority matrix.

Classify:
- AUTHORIZED
- AUTHORIZED WITH CONDITIONS
- NOT AUTHORIZED
- UNKNOWN — ESCALATE

## 2. Acceptance control
Before accepting/signing, verify:
1. correct carrier and load;
2. controlling document version;
3. material terms reconciled;
4. required corrections incorporated or documented approval obtained;
5. supplied authority permits the action;
6. acceptance/signature method and evidence are recorded.

## 3. Worked authority case
Carrier instruction: dispatcher may accept confirmations only when written terms match the approved M08 negotiation record. Any changed rate, equipment, pickup/delivery appointment, or added service obligation requires carrier approval.

RC v2 changes pickup from 14:00 to 12:00.

Even if the dispatcher thinks noon is better, the supplied authority condition is not satisfied. Correct response: HOLD/ESCALATE or obtain documented carrier approval. Personal judgment does not expand supplied authority.

## 4. Document integrity
Never:
- edit a broker-issued confirmation to make it match;
- delete a term from a PDF and present it as original;
- backdate approval;
- create a fake signature;
- claim a carrier approved a change when approval is absent;
- destroy the original version to hide a discrepancy.

If annotation is needed for training/office notes, keep it separate or clearly marked so the source document remains distinguishable.

## 5. Version control
For each booking document record:
- document ID/name;
- source;
- received timestamp;
- version label or internal control ID;
- key changes from prior version;
- current/superseded status;
- who reviewed it;
- resolution link/approval.

A filename such as `ratecon_final_FINAL2.pdf` is not a professional version-control system.

## 6. Electronic acceptance
Electronic records and signatures can be used in commerce, but M09 does not teach that one vendor, click method, or platform is universally required. Follow controlling law, the parties' process, carrier policy, and supplied authority. Preserve evidence of what was accepted, by whom, how, and when.

## 7. Coached practice
Carrier permits dispatcher to acknowledge receipt but reserves final acceptance of any rate confirmation containing added accessorial obligations.

Broker asks dispatcher to "just DocuSign it so I can hold the truck" before carrier responds.

Correct action: acknowledgement authority does not equal acceptance authority. Do not sign beyond supplied authority. Document request and escalate.

## 8. Common failures
- confusing negotiation authority with signature authority;
- using convenience as authority;
- altering documents instead of requesting correction;
- failing to preserve superseded versions;
- recording approval without source/time;
- assuming an electronic signature tool itself proves proper authority.

### Part 04 operating rule
**Authority must be evidenced. Integrity must be preserved. A clean-looking file is worthless if the history is false.**
