# Module 09 Student Manual — Part 01
## From Negotiation to Booking Control

### Employment outcome
A dispatcher must know when a promising load is merely negotiated, when it is pending documentation, and when the carrier actually has a controlled booking record ready for the next operational workflow. The expensive mistake is treating enthusiasm, a phone conversation, or a load-board post as proof that every material booking term is settled.

## 1. M08 ends where M09 begins
Module 08 may end with an agreement in principle or an ACCEPT decision pending formal booking control. That does **not** authorize you to invent missing terms, ignore a conflicting written confirmation, sign beyond supplied authority, or tell a driver the load is ready when the booking file is not controlled.

M09 uses this chain:

**HANDOFF → VERIFY AGREEMENT → RECEIVE DOCUMENT → IDENTIFY → READ → RECONCILE → CLASSIFY → CORRECT/ESCALATE → CONFIRM AUTHORITY → ACCEPT/SIGN → RECORD → PACKET → HANDOFF**

## 2. Booking-state vocabulary
- **AGREED IN PRINCIPLE:** negotiation evidence indicates apparent agreement, but formal booking controls are not yet complete.
- **PENDING DOCUMENT:** the expected booking document has not been received or identified.
- **VERIFY:** a supplied fact exists but its controlling source/version/accuracy still requires confirmation.
- **MISMATCH — MATERIAL:** written and supplied agreement evidence conflict on a term that can change money, service, identity, obligation, risk, or authorization.
- **MISMATCH — NONMATERIAL / CLERICAL:** a discrepancy appears clerical and does not change the substantive obligation, but it still must be documented and corrected when appropriate.
- **UNKNOWN / MISSING:** the file does not support a safe conclusion.
- **SUPERSEDED:** a newer controlled version replaced this document for the booking decision.
- **HOLD:** do not advance the booking/dispatch state until the identified control is satisfied.
- **CORRECT:** obtain or document correction of the controlling evidence.
- **ESCALATE:** supplied authority or evidence is insufficient for the dispatcher to decide.
- **BOOKED / DISPATCH PACKET READY:** M09 booking controls are satisfied and the controlled packet is ready for handoff. It does not mean trip planning, HOS feasibility, active monitoring, or billing is complete.

## 3. Source hierarchy is a reasoning problem
A dispatcher may have several pieces of evidence:
1. M08 negotiation call record;
2. M08 Accept/Decline/Escalate decision;
3. broker email or message;
4. rate confirmation version 1;
5. corrected rate confirmation version 2;
6. carrier-supplied authority instructions;
7. driver/truck/equipment identity facts.

Do not choose whichever document is easiest. Identify what each source proves, when it was created, whether it is current, and whether another source supersedes or contradicts it.

## 4. Booking-file anatomy
Before acceptance or dispatch packet assembly, the file should let a reviewer trace at least:
- broker/customer identity and contact source;
- carrier identity;
- load/reference number;
- equipment and supplied carrier requirements;
- commodity/weight when supplied and material;
- origin/destination and pickup/delivery dates/times as written;
- agreed rate and material money terms;
- agreement-specific operating/accessorial/documentation terms;
- document version/source/received time;
- discrepancy/correction history;
- supplied acceptance/signature authority;
- final booking status and who approved any escalation.

A blank field is not permission to guess. A populated field is not automatically verified.

## 5. Worked demonstration — Summit Lane
**Fictional training case.** M08 records show:
- broker: Northline Brokerage;
- carrier: Summit Lane Transport;
- apparent agreed linehaul: $2,450;
- dry van;
- Dallas, TX → Nashville, TN;
- pickup Tuesday 14:00;
- delivery Wednesday 10:00;
- driver-assist: broker said "no" during the call;
- dispatcher may negotiate within supplied limits but may not accept a new material obligation without carrier approval.

A rate confirmation arrives showing $2,450 but also states **driver assist required**.

Correct reasoning:
1. The rate matches.
2. The operational obligation does not match the negotiation record.
3. The dispatcher does not average the two sources or assume the written term is harmless.
4. Classify **MISMATCH — MATERIAL** because the obligation can affect service and carrier acceptance.
5. Place booking on **HOLD / CORRECT**.
6. Request corrected written terms or obtain documented carrier authorization if supplied policy allows escalation.
7. Preserve both the original and corrected evidence in the booking history.

## 6. Common failure patterns
- "The money matches, so we're good." — headline-rate scanning.
- "The broker said it on the phone, so ignore the PDF." — unsupported source preference.
- "I'll sign it and fix it later." — unauthorized commitment risk.
- "The new PDF looks almost the same." — version-control failure.
- "The driver can figure the rest out." — uncontrolled handoff.
- "Booked" while a material discrepancy remains — false completion.

## 7. Coached practice
For each fact below, classify **AGREED / CONDITIONAL / UNRESOLVED / UNVERIFIED** at M09 intake:
1. Broker and carrier verbally agreed to $2,300, but no written confirmation has arrived.
2. Rate is agreed, but broker said appointment time would be confirmed later.
3. Carrier supplied written authority allowing dispatcher acceptance only when all terms match the approved negotiation record.
4. A screenshot of a load-board post shows a pickup time, but the broker later stated the appointment changed.

**Reasoning target:** 1 = agreed in principle/pending document; 2 = unresolved; 3 = verified authority instruction if source is controlled; 4 = verify current controlling time rather than treating the screenshot as final.

## 8. Boundary note
M09 can record pickup/delivery facts exactly as booked and flag a later feasibility issue. Detailed routing, fuel/time planning and appointment management belong to M10; detailed HOS/ELD belongs to M11.

### Part 01 operating rule
**Never convert a negotiation outcome into a completed booking merely because the load feels settled. Build the evidence trail first.**
