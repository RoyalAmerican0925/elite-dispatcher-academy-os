**ELITE DISPATCHER ACADEMY**

**Student Manual — Module 06**

Broker Verification & Fraud Prevention

*Assignment C-006*

# 1\. Module Introduction

Module 05 taught you how to find and screen a load candidate. This module answers the next question: before you trust this broker, this contact, this document, or this payment instruction, how do you verify it's safe enough to proceed?

**IMPORTANT:** You do not have to prove fraud. You have to verify enough to know whether you can safely proceed. That distinction — proof versus sufficient verification — is the single most important idea in this module.

Layered verification means checking multiple things, in sequence, rather than relying on any one signal:

**VERIFY THE ENTITY** → VERIFY THE CONTACT → VERIFY THE LOAD → VERIFY THE PAYMENT PATH.

# 2\. Why Verification Matters

A red flag is not proof of fraud. This module teaches a disciplined sequence for handling uncertainty professionally: observe → cross-check → verify → document → classify risk → proceed / hold / escalate / decline.

**IMPORTANT:** This module does not train you to make unsupported accusations. You'll learn to say "unable to proceed under carrier policy," not "you are a scammer" — unless a competent authority has actually established fraud. Professional caution and professional restraint work together here.

# 3\. Entity vs. Contact

**FACT-M06-004.** A legitimate company with a valid MC/USDOT record does not, by itself, prove that the individual actually contacting you is an authorized representative of that company.

**IMPORTANT:** These are two separate verification questions: is this a real, authorized company (entity verification), and is this person actually who they say they are, speaking for that company (contact verification)? A clean entity check does not answer the contact question.

# 5\. Broker Authority

**FACT-M06-001.** A person brokering non-exempt interstate property transportation generally must hold applicable FMCSA broker authority.

*Classification: LAW / REGULATION (49 CFR Part 371 and applicable FMCSA registration rules)*

Teaching rule: if current official FMCSA data shows the broker lacks required active authority for the contemplated activity, do not book the load through that broker.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not teach that a merely "pending" status automatically means unlawful activity — check what status is actually pending before drawing a conclusion.

# 6\. BMC-84 / BMC-85 — Broker Financial Security

**FACT-M06-003.** Property brokers must maintain the applicable $75,000 financial responsibility requirement through either a BMC-84 surety bond or a BMC-85 trust fund agreement.

*Classification: LAW / REGULATION (49 CFR § 387.307)*

**[CURRENT-DATE MATERIAL — 2026]** Beginning January 16, 2026, updated federal requirements govern BMC-85 trustee eligibility. If FMCSA determines a broker's trustee no longer qualifies, that broker has 30 days from notification to obtain a compliant replacement filing before operating authority is suspended. This is distinct from a straight financial-security shortfall, where current rules generally require replenishment within 7 calendar days of notice. Do not conflate the two timelines.

Teach students: BMC-84/85 status is one verification layer among several — it is not a payment guarantee for any individual transaction.

# 7\. Broker Verification Workflow

A repeatable operational sequence:

1. Load candidate identified
2. Broker name/identifier captured
3. Current authority checked
4. Entity name/DBA cross-checked
5. Contact channel verified
6. Financial security status reviewed
7. Platform/credit/factor signals reviewed
8. Documents cross-checked
9. Payment/factoring path verified
10. Red flags resolved
11. Risk status assigned

## Risk Classification System

* CLEAR — no material unresolved mismatch remains; proceed to the next workflow stage.
* CLEAR WITH DOCUMENTED QUALIFICATION — a minor inconsistency was independently resolved and documented.
* VERIFY — a red flag exists; pause and independently confirm before proceeding.
* HOLD — a material mismatch exists and cannot yet be resolved; do not sign, dispatch, reroute, or change payment instructions.
* ESCALATE — the issue exceeds beginner authority or competency.
* DO NOT USE — the broker fails carrier/factoring/internal risk policy; this is not automatically an allegation of fraud.
* DO NOT BOOK — the transaction cannot proceed safely or lawfully based on verified facts.

**IMPORTANT:** Never accuse someone of fraud without evidence. Use professional operational language — "unable to proceed under carrier policy" — not accusatory language, unless a competent authority has actually established fraud.

# 8\. Contact Verification

Core principle: TRUSTED CHANNEL beats the message itself, every time.

Trusted channels include: a previously verified corporate number, an established broker contact already in carrier records, an official corporate site verified independently, official FMCSA public records where appropriate, and previously confirmed relationship records.

**IMPORTANT:** Never rely on a phone number found inside a suspicious email, a reply-to address on a suspicious email, a number sent in an unsolicited text, or a number found inside a questionable attachment. If the contact information itself is what's in question, it can't be the thing you use to verify itself.

# 9\. Email / Domain Risk

Recognize these beginner-level warning patterns: typosquatting, added hyphens, extra words, character substitution, mismatched reply-to addresses, free email accounts used where a corporate domain would be expected, display-name spoofing, a compromised real account, and thread hijacking.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not teach "Gmail = fraud" — plenty of legitimate small operations use free email accounts. Teach "unexpected contact method = verify" instead.

# 10\. Business Email Compromise (BEC) / Account Takeover (ATO)

**FACT-M06-005.** A legitimate email account can be compromised. Unexpected changes involving payment, factoring, routing, pickup/delivery, bank information, new attachments, or urgent requests must trigger independent verification through a trusted channel — regardless of how legitimate the account otherwise looks.

Required rule: STOP → do not click or change anything → use a trusted channel → verify → document → escalate if needed.

**IMPORTANT:** This module does not teach email-header forensics, IP tracing, or hacking investigation — that's specialized cybersecurity work outside dispatcher instruction.
