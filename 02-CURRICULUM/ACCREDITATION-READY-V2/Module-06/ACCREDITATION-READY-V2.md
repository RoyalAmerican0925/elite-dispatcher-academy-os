# Module 06 — Broker Verification & Fraud Prevention
## Accreditation-Ready Version 2

**Status:** Accreditation-readiness revision; not an accreditation claim.
**Source body:** Existing Module 06 student manual and instructor answer guide remain the instructional content body unless expressly superseded by a later V2 revision.
**Version:** 2.0-AR
**Review date:** 2026-08-15

## Module purpose
Develop the learner's ability to verify broker/entity/contact/load/payment information through layered checks, classify unresolved risk, and make disciplined proceed/hold/escalate/decline decisions without unsupported fraud accusations.

## Measurable learning outcomes
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Distinguish entity verification from contact verification.
2. Check current broker authority and applicable financial-security information using authoritative or approved sources.
3. Apply a layered verification workflow covering entity, contact, load/document, and payment path.
4. Recognize common business-email-compromise, account-takeover, document-mismatch, payment-change, and impersonation warning patterns.
5. Distinguish co-brokering, suspected unauthorized re-brokering/double brokering, credit risk, factor policy, and DNU policy without overstating what any single indicator proves.
6. Classify a case as CLEAR, CLEAR WITH DOCUMENTED QUALIFICATION, VERIFY, HOLD, ESCALATE, DO NOT USE, or DO NOT BOOK and justify the decision from documented facts.
7. Use trusted-channel callback verification when contact or payment information changes.
8. Document verification steps and unresolved discrepancies in a professional, non-accusatory manner.

## Required student evidence
- Completed broker-verification case file.
- Contact-verification/BEC scenario.
- Document and payment-path mismatch scenario.
- Risk-classification exercise requiring written justification and source identification.
- Knowledge assessment mapped to Outcomes 1–8.

## Assessment alignment
The Module 06 Instructor Answer Guide is the controlled answer-key source. Performance scoring must reward correct verification sequence, use of trusted sources, calibrated risk classification, documentation quality, and proper escalation. Unsupported accusations should be treated as a competency error.

## Completion criteria
Learner must meet the academy's approved passing standard and achieve a satisfactory result on the verification case-file rubric. A learner who proceeds despite an unresolved material identity/payment mismatch or fails to verify required authority must remediate before completion.

## Evidence retained
Retain assessment score, verification case/rubric result, remediation/reassessment history, completion status, and exact module/assessment version.

## Source and revision controls
Current FMCSA system descriptions, broker authority procedures, financial-security rules, and other time-sensitive federal information must be reverified at publication and on the academy's regulatory-review schedule. Cybersecurity examples must remain operational guidance, not claims that any single indicator proves fraud.
