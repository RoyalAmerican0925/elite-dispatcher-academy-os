**ELITE DISPATCHER ACADEMY**

**Student Manual — Module 04**

Carrier & Driver Onboarding

# 1\. Module Introduction

Modules 01 through 03 gave you the industry map, your legal boundaries, and the equipment vocabulary to recognize a load-to-equipment fit. This module answers a different question: before you dispatch a carrier or a driver at all, what do you actually need to know, verify, document, protect, and keep current?

Onboarding is not paperwork collection. It's the process that determines whether you're operationally ready to dispatch someone — and whether you're accidentally taking on compliance duties that legally belong to the motor carrier, not to you.

**IMPORTANT:** Core distinction for this entire module: MOTOR CARRIER COMPLIANCE RESPONSIBILITY is not the same thing as DISPATCHER OPERATIONAL READINESS. You're building the second one. Confusing the two is the most consequential mistake this module exists to prevent.

**IMPORTANT:** Central rule: onboarding is not just collecting PDFs. For every piece of information, ask: Is it complete? Is it current? Do the names, numbers, and entities make sense together? Is the carrier operationally ready? Is the driver compatible with the work? Are there expirations or restrictions? Are payment instructions verified? If the answer to any of these is unclear — STOP, VERIFY, ESCALATE.

# 2\. Verifying Carrier Registration and Operating Status

**FACT-M04-001.** As of 2026, FMCSA's registration environment is undergoing a major modernization. Motus is the current, centralized FMCSA registration platform — as of May 14, 2026, it replaced the legacy registration systems (including the FMCSA Portal's registration functions) that dispatchers previously relied on.

* Motus — the current FMCSA registration environment for the 2026 modernization. Registration, authority actions, and related filings now run through this system.

* Legacy Licensing & Insurance (L\&I) — as of May 14, 2026, this is a historical-record system. FMCSA has stated that Motus filings are not reflected in legacy L\&I.

* SAFER — a public safety/entity snapshot tool, useful as a supplemental lookup, but not a complete substitute for checking current registration/authority/insurance status directly.

**\[CURRENT-DATE MATERIAL — REVERIFY BEFORE PUBLICATION\]** Do not teach the old workflow of "check L\&I to confirm today's current filing status" — L\&I no longer reflects current Motus activity. Do not imply that SAFER alone answers every registration, authority, or insurance question either; it's a snapshot, not the authoritative current source for every fact. Teach students to identify which current FMCSA source actually answers the specific question they're asking, rather than defaulting to one familiar tool out of habit.

Building on Module 01: do not teach that every carrier with a USDOT number is automatically cleared to haul for-hire interstate freight. A dispatcher onboarding a new carrier should verify identity, USDOT status, operating authority where required, the authority type, and relevant insurance/registration status — using a current official source, not just a carrier-provided certificate or screenshot when an authoritative current source is available.

# 3\. Unified Carrier Registration (UCR)

**FACT-M04-002.** UCR is an annual registration framework applicable to covered interstate/international transportation entities.

The carrier is responsible for its own required UCR registration — a dispatcher is not automatically the person responsible for filing it on the carrier's behalf.

**IMPORTANT:** Keep these two things distinct: the underlying registration requirement for covered entities is a LAW. Whether a broker or customer chooses to screen for or request proof of current UCR status is INDUSTRY PRACTICE. Do not teach "federal law requires every broker to reject a carrier whose UCR is expired" — that overstates a screening practice as a legal mandate.

# 4\. Insurance Recognition

**FACT-M04-003.** For applicable non-hazardous for-hire property carriers operating vehicles at or above 10,001 lb GVWR, the federal public-liability (BIPD) minimum is generally $750,000. Certain hazardous-material operations require higher limits.

Several different coverage types come up in carrier onboarding, and they are not all federally required:

* Federal public-liability requirement (BIPD) — federally required at the minimum described above.

* Cargo insurance — not federally required for most general-property carriers, though commonly required contractually by brokers or shippers.

* Physical damage — covers the carrier's own equipment; not a federal requirement.

* General liability — broader business liability coverage; not a federal trucking-specific requirement.

* Bobtail / non-trucking liability — covers the tractor when not under dispatch; not universally federally mandated.

* Occupational accident / workers' compensation — addresses on-the-job injury; requirements vary and are not uniform across this list.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not imply every coverage type listed above is federally required — only the public-liability minimum described above carries that federal floor for the described operations. For cargo insurance specifically, use "commonly required contractually" rather than "universally required by brokers" — some brokers or shippers may not require it, or may require different amounts.

# 5\. Certificate of Insurance (COI)

A COI is a document summarizing represented insurance information — issued by or on behalf of an insurer, typically for the requesting party's convenience.

What a COI is NOT:

* Operating authority.

* Proof that every relevant filing is currently effective at the moment you're looking at it.

* A guarantee that coverage cannot have changed since the COI was issued.

* A substitute for appropriate current verification through an authoritative source.

**IMPORTANT:** Better onboarding review looks like: COI \+ current official/insurer verification as appropriate \+ entity name match \+ dates/limits check — not COI alone.

# 6\. Form W-9

Form W-9 is an IRS tax-identification/certification document commonly used to set up business payments — it's how a carrier provides its tax ID information for payment and reporting purposes.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not teach "the W-9 name must always visually match the FMCSA name character-for-character." A disregarded LLC's tax reporting can legitimately show the owner's name on Line 1 and the business/disregarded entity name on Line 2 — a name difference in this pattern is normal, not automatically suspicious. Treat a name mismatch as VERIFY / RESOLVE, not automatically FRAUD — confirm the relationship (e.g., disregarded entity structure) rather than assuming the worst or ignoring the difference.

# 7\. DQ File vs. Dispatch Profile

**FACT-M04-004.** This distinction is central to the entire module.

* Motor carrier — maintains required driver-qualification and compliance records under applicable law (the Driver Qualification, or DQ, file).

* Dispatcher — maintains an operational profile containing information reasonably necessary to dispatch the driver — a different, narrower set of information for a different purpose.

Reasonable dispatch-profile information may include: driver name, contact information, assigned carrier, CDL class, expiration date, relevant endorsements, medical qualification status/expiration as operationally necessary, assigned equipment, current availability, ELD communication process, and home-time or carrier-approved restrictions.

**IMPORTANT:** This module does not turn into DQ-file administration, Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse administration, drug/alcohol compliance, driver hiring, or employment-background compliance instruction. Those are separate, specialized functions — unless a dispatcher is separately contracted and trained for them, they remain outside this operational profile.

# 8\. Medical Certification — 2026 Transition

**FACT-M04-005 — CURRENT-DATE MATERIAL.** The federal system for transmitting medical-examiner results is moving toward electronic transmission through the NRII (National Registry II) framework, coordinated with State Driver's Licensing Agencies (SDLAs) and CDLIS.

As of this drafting, FMCSA has an active temporary nationwide exemption in effect from April 11, 2026 through October 11, 2026, allowing covered interstate CDL/CLP holders and motor carriers to rely on a paper copy of the Medical Examiner's Certificate for up to 60 days after issuance — extended specifically because five states (Alaska, California, Kentucky, Louisiana, and New Hampshire) had not yet fully implemented electronic NRII transmission.

**\[CURRENT-DATE MATERIAL — REVERIFY BEFORE PUBLICATION, especially after October 11, 2026\]** Do not state that after October 11, 2026 there is definitely a universal "15-day paper rule" or that any one specific record source immediately becomes the exclusive proof source. FMCSA has stated it does not anticipate granting additional nationwide NRII waivers after this exemption's six-month duration — but that is a stated expectation, not a guarantee of what the rule will be after expiration. Teach students: "This temporary 2026 exemption is scheduled to expire October 11, 2026\. The academy must reverify FMCSA's current procedure before final publication or after that date." The dispatcher's role here is operational verification of current status — not administering medical-qualification compliance, which remains the carrier's responsibility.

# 9\. CDL Class Recognition

At a beginner level: Class A generally covers a combination at or above the applicable 26,001 lb GCWR threshold, when the towed vehicle itself exceeds 10,000 lb GVWR. A driver holding a Class B license does not gain Class A authorization simply by having some commercial license — the specific combination and towed-unit weight thresholds are what determine which class is required.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not reduce CDL classification to "anything over 26,000 pounds." The combination weight threshold and the towed-unit weight threshold both matter, and a driver's actual license class must match what the specific combination requires — see Scenario S-04-09.

# 10\. Endorsements

Recognize these common endorsements at a beginner level:

* H — Hazardous Materials.

* X — Combined Tank Vehicle / Hazardous Materials endorsement.

* N — Tank Vehicle.

* T — Doubles / Triples.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not teach that holding a CDL, or holding a specific endorsement, alone establishes complete load qualification. Shipment-specific, carrier-specific, vehicle-specific, and regulatory requirements may still apply on top of the base endorsement. Detailed hazmat qualification analysis remains outside this module — see Module 03, Section 25\.

# 11\. Factoring and Notice of Assignment (NOA)

**FACT-M04-006.** A Notice of Assignment (NOA) is commonly used to notify an account debtor (such as a broker) that a carrier's payment rights have been assigned to a factoring company.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not turn the dispatcher into a UCC lawyer. Do not teach that every NOA lets the dispatcher conclusively determine who legally owns a receivable — state enactments, the underlying assignment facts, proper notification, proof, releases, and other legal issues can all matter, and resolving genuine disputes is not a dispatcher-level task.

### Dispatcher-safe rule

If payment instructions conflict: STOP. Do not redirect funds. Verify through previously trusted contact information. Confirm with the appropriate parties. Escalate unresolved conflicts rather than making a unilateral call.

# 12\. Factoring Profile

Useful operational fields for a factoring relationship include: the factoring company name, an official contact, whether an NOA is on file, payment/remittance instructions, any broker credit-check relationship if used, and a verified change-contact process.
