# 9\. CDL Class Recognition

At a beginner level: Class A generally covers a combination at or above the applicable 26,001 lb GCWR threshold, when the towed vehicle itself exceeds 10,000 lb GVWR. A driver holding a Class B license does not gain Class A authorization simply by having some commercial license — the specific combination and towed-unit weight thresholds are what determine which class is required.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not reduce CDL classification to "anything over 26,000 pounds." The combination weight threshold and the towed-unit weight threshold both matter, and a driver's actual license class must match what the specific combination requires — see Scenario S-04-09.

# 10\. Endorsements

Recognize these common endorsements at a beginner level:

* H — Hazardous Materials.
* X — Combined Tank Vehicle / Hazardous Materials endorsement.
* N — Tank Vehicle.
* T — Doubles / Triples.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not teach that holding a CDL, or holding a specific endorsement, alone establishes complete load qualification. Shipment-specific, carrier-specific, vehicle-specific, and regulatory requirements may still apply on top of the base endorsement. Detailed hazmat qualification analysis remains outside this module.

# 11\. Factoring and Notice of Assignment (NOA)

**FACT-M04-006.** A Notice of Assignment (NOA) is commonly used to notify an account debtor that a carrier's payment rights have been assigned to a factoring company.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not turn the dispatcher into a UCC lawyer. Do not teach that every NOA lets the dispatcher conclusively determine who legally owns a receivable — state enactments, the underlying assignment facts, proper notification, proof, releases, and other legal issues can all matter, and resolving genuine disputes is not a dispatcher-level task.

### Dispatcher-safe rule

If payment instructions conflict: STOP. Do not redirect funds. Verify through previously trusted contact information. Confirm with the appropriate parties. Escalate unresolved conflicts rather than making a unilateral call.

# 12\. Factoring Profile

Useful operational fields for a factoring relationship include: the factoring company name, an official contact, whether an NOA is on file, payment/remittance instructions, any broker credit-check relationship if used, and a verified change-contact process.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not interpret recourse provisions, reserves, security interests, or factoring-contract enforceability — those are legal/contractual analysis questions outside this module's scope unless separately assigned.

# 13\. Banking / Payment Security

Sensitive onboarding items may include the W-9, ACH data, voided checks, bank information, factoring instructions, and identity information. Apply basic cybersecurity discipline to all of it:

* Data minimization — collect only what's operationally necessary.
* Access control — limit who can see or edit sensitive records.
* Secure storage — don't leave sensitive documents in unsecured or overly shared locations.
* Trusted-contact verification — confirm identity through channels you already trust, not just the channel a request arrived on.
* Payment-change callback — never update payment instructions from an inbound message alone.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not teach that ordinary consumer email is automatically illegal for this purpose. Treat this as a cybersecurity-risk-management issue to be handled with the controls above, unless a specific law or contract states otherwise for a given situation.

# 14\. Payment-Change Protocol

Required workflow when new bank or factoring instructions are received:

1. New bank/factoring instructions received.
2. Do not trust the message by itself.
3. Use a previously verified phone number or contact method — not contact information supplied in the new message.
4. Callback verify.
5. Compare entity/account/factor information against what's on file.
6. Document the verification.
7. Update only after confirmation.

**IMPORTANT:** This protocol anticipates the deeper fraud-prevention training in Module 06 — treat it as a foundational habit now, not a complete fraud curriculum.

# 15\. Limited Power of Attorney (LPOA)

A Limited Power of Attorney may grant specifically defined agency authority according to its own written terms and applicable law.

**IMPORTANT:** An LPOA is not universally required for dispatching, and it is not automatically accepted by every broker or load board. It does not automatically determine worker classification, employment status, broker status, or any authority beyond what it actually says in writing. Do not teach "an LPOA lets you sign anything for the carrier." Instead teach: read the scope, act only within authorized limits, and recognize that legal review may be appropriate for anything ambiguous or high-stakes.

# 16\. Dispatch Agreement

Building on Module 02, a dispatch agreement should operationally address: the parties, scope of services, the carrier's final decision authority, communication authority, compensation, payment timing, confidentiality, document access, termination, operational limitations, and any signing authority granted.

**IMPORTANT:** This module does not draft individualized legal advice or a specific contract for the student's business — it teaches what a complete agreement should address at an operational level.
