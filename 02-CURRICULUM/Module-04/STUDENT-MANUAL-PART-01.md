**ELITE DISPATCHER ACADEMY**

**Student Manual — Module 04**

Carrier & Driver Onboarding

*Assignment C-004*

# 1\. Module Introduction

Modules 01 through 03 gave you the industry map, your legal boundaries, and the equipment vocabulary to recognize a load-to-equipment fit. This module answers a different question: before you dispatch a carrier or a driver at all, what do you actually need to know, verify, document, protect, and keep current?

Onboarding is not paperwork collection. It's the process that determines whether you're operationally ready to dispatch someone — and whether you're accidentally taking on compliance duties that legally belong to the motor carrier, not to you.

**IMPORTANT:** Core distinction for this entire module: MOTOR CARRIER COMPLIANCE RESPONSIBILITY is not the same thing as DISPATCHER OPERATIONAL READINESS. You're building the second one. Confusing the two is the most consequential mistake this module exists to prevent.

**IMPORTANT:** Central rule: onboarding is not just collecting PDFs. For every piece of information, ask: Is it complete? Is it current? Do the names, numbers, and entities make sense together? Is the carrier operationally ready? Is the driver compatible with the work? Are there expirations or restrictions? Are payment instructions verified? If the answer to any of these is unclear — STOP, VERIFY, ESCALATE.

# 2\. Verifying Carrier Registration and Operating Status

**FACT-M04-001.** As of 2026, FMCSA's registration environment is undergoing a major modernization. Motus is the current, centralized FMCSA registration platform — as of May 14, 2026, it replaced the legacy registration systems that dispatchers previously relied on.

* Motus — the current FMCSA registration environment for the 2026 modernization.
* Legacy Licensing & Insurance (L&I) — as of May 14, 2026, this is a historical-record system. FMCSA has stated that Motus filings are not reflected in legacy L&I.
* SAFER — a public safety/entity snapshot tool, useful as a supplemental lookup, but not a complete substitute for checking current registration/authority/insurance status directly.

**[CURRENT-DATE MATERIAL — REVERIFY BEFORE PUBLICATION]** Do not teach the old workflow of "check L&I to confirm today's current filing status" — L&I no longer reflects current Motus activity. Do not imply that SAFER alone answers every registration, authority, or insurance question either; it's a snapshot, not the authoritative current source for every fact.

Building on Module 01: do not teach that every carrier with a USDOT number is automatically cleared to haul for-hire interstate freight. A dispatcher onboarding a new carrier should verify identity, USDOT status, operating authority where required, the authority type, and relevant insurance/registration status — using a current official source.

# 3\. Unified Carrier Registration (UCR)

**FACT-M04-002.** UCR is an annual registration framework applicable to covered interstate/international transportation entities.

The carrier is responsible for its own required UCR registration — a dispatcher is not automatically the person responsible for filing it on the carrier's behalf.

**IMPORTANT:** Keep these two things distinct: the underlying registration requirement for covered entities is a LAW. Whether a broker or customer chooses to screen for or request proof of current UCR status is INDUSTRY PRACTICE.

# 4\. Insurance Recognition

**FACT-M04-003.** For applicable non-hazardous for-hire property carriers operating vehicles at or above 10,001 lb GVWR, the federal public-liability (BIPD) minimum is generally $750,000. Certain hazardous-material operations require higher limits.

Several different coverage types come up in carrier onboarding, and they are not all federally required:

* Federal public-liability requirement (BIPD) — federally required at the minimum described above.
* Cargo insurance — not federally required for most general-property carriers, though commonly required contractually by brokers or shippers.
* Physical damage — covers the carrier's own equipment; not a federal requirement.
* General liability — broader business liability coverage; not a federal trucking-specific requirement.
* Bobtail / non-trucking liability — covers the tractor when not under dispatch; not universally federally mandated.
* Occupational accident / workers' compensation — addresses on-the-job injury; requirements vary and are not uniform across this list.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not imply every coverage type listed above is federally required — only the public-liability minimum described above carries that federal floor for the described operations.

# 5\. Certificate of Insurance (COI)

A COI is a document summarizing represented insurance information — issued by or on behalf of an insurer, typically for the requesting party's convenience.

What a COI is NOT:

* Operating authority.
* Proof that every relevant filing is currently effective at the moment you're looking at it.
* A guarantee that coverage cannot have changed since the COI was issued.
* A substitute for appropriate current verification through an authoritative source.

**IMPORTANT:** Better onboarding review looks like: COI + current official/insurer verification as appropriate + entity name match + dates/limits check — not COI alone.

# 6\. Form W-9

Form W-9 is an IRS tax-identification/certification document commonly used to set up business payments — it's how a carrier provides its tax ID information for payment and reporting purposes.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not teach "the W-9 name must always visually match the FMCSA name character-for-character." A disregarded LLC's tax reporting can legitimately show the owner's name on Line 1 and the business/disregarded entity name on Line 2. Treat a name mismatch as VERIFY / RESOLVE, not automatically FRAUD.

# 7\. DQ File vs. Dispatch Profile

**FACT-M04-004.** This distinction is central to the entire module.

* Motor carrier — maintains required driver-qualification and compliance records under applicable law.
* Dispatcher — maintains an operational profile containing information reasonably necessary to dispatch the driver — a different, narrower set of information for a different purpose.

Reasonable dispatch-profile information may include: driver name, contact information, assigned carrier, CDL class, expiration date, relevant endorsements, medical qualification status/expiration as operationally necessary, assigned equipment, current availability, ELD communication process, and home-time or carrier-approved restrictions.

**IMPORTANT:** This module does not turn into DQ-file administration, Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse administration, drug/alcohol compliance, driver hiring, or employment-background compliance instruction.

# 8\. Medical Certification — 2026 Transition

**FACT-M04-005 — CURRENT-DATE MATERIAL.** The federal system for transmitting medical-examiner results is moving toward electronic transmission through the NRII framework, coordinated with State Driver's Licensing Agencies and CDLIS.

As of this drafting, FMCSA has an active temporary nationwide exemption in effect from April 11, 2026 through October 11, 2026, allowing covered interstate CDL/CLP holders and motor carriers to rely on a paper copy of the Medical Examiner's Certificate for up to 60 days after issuance.
