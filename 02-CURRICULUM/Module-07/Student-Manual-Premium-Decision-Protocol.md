# 1. A Dispatcher Does Not Get to Invent Missing Economics

Real load decisions are often made before every desirable fact is available. That does **not** mean every blank becomes zero.

Before calculating, classify each material input:

- **Provided / Verified (P):** supported by the current scenario, carrier, agreement, route source, rate confirmation, fee schedule, or other identified evidence.
- **Unknown / Unresolved (U):** not currently known well enough to use honestly.
- **Assumed for Training (A):** a fictional number supplied by the Academy so you can practice the calculation.

A true zero is a number. An unknown is not.

If the carrier has no factoring fee on a transaction and that fact is known, the fee is **$0 — P**.

If nobody has established whether a factoring fee applies, the fee is **U**, not $0.

That distinction can change a load decision.

## The materiality question

Ask:

> If this unknown were materially different from what I am tempted to assume, could it change TAKE, COUNTER, or DECLINE?

If yes, it is decision-critical.

Do not manufacture the answer. Use **HOLD FOR DATA**.

---

# 2. The Four Economic Dispositions

Module 07 uses four controlled economic dispositions.

## TAKE

Use **TAKE** when the currently established economics meet the carrier's stated criteria and no material unresolved input prevents a defensible recommendation.

TAKE does not mean the dispatcher has magically become the carrier. The carrier's operating authorization still controls.

## COUNTER

Use **COUNTER** when the load may become economically acceptable at a higher total revenue and the carrier has provided enough decision criteria to calculate a defensible threshold.

Module 07 teaches the **math** of the threshold.

Module 08 teaches the **negotiation**.

Do not invent a counter number because it “sounds right.”

## HOLD FOR DATA

Use **HOLD FOR DATA** when a material unknown prevents a supportable recommendation.

Examples:

- the truck's actual current location is unknown, so origin deadhead cannot be established;
- a material toll/route cost is unresolved;
- it is unclear whether the invoice will be factored and that fee could change the result;
- the carrier's economic floor is needed to decide whether the load meets its criteria;
- a claimed accessorial is being used to make the economics work but eligibility or triggering facts have not been established.

HOLD is not indecision. It is a professional decision that the current evidence is insufficient.

## DECLINE

Use **DECLINE** when the currently established economics do not meet the carrier's stated criteria and the available evidence does not support a viable counter or further data hold.

Declining every uncertain load is not professional judgment. Neither is accepting every high-rate load. The purpose is disciplined classification.

# 3. Counter-Threshold Mathematics

A dispatcher should not negotiate blindly. When the carrier has supplied a valid economic floor, Module 07 can calculate the revenue required to meet it.

## Simple revenue-per-mile threshold

If a carrier states that a load must produce at least a certain effective revenue per total mile:

```text
Required Effective RPM × Total Trip Miles = Minimum Required Total Revenue
```

### Example

**Fictional instructional assumptions:**

- Carrier-required effective RPM: $2.35
- Total trip miles: 720

```text
$2.35 × 720 = $1,692 minimum total revenue
```

If the current offer is $1,560:

```text
$1,692 − $1,560 = $132 revenue gap
```

The economic analysis supports a minimum target of **$1,692** under the supplied carrier criterion.

Module 07 stops there.

Whether to ask for $1,700, $1,750, or use a different negotiation anchor is a Module 08 negotiation question.

## Contribution requirement

If the carrier instead supplies a required contribution target and the dispatcher has all relevant included-cost inputs:

```text
Required Contribution + Included Costs = Minimum Required Revenue
```

Example:

- Required stated-cost contribution: $900
- Estimated fuel: $410
- Tolls: $35
- Applicable dispatcher fee calculated on revenue: percentage-based

Be careful: a fee that itself changes with revenue can make the equation circular. Do not fake precision. Solve the actual relationship or use a tool designed for it.

For example, if dispatcher fee is 5% of revenue and other included costs equal $445:

```text
Revenue − (0.05 × Revenue) − $445 = $900
0.95 × Revenue = $1,345
Revenue = $1,415.79
```

Rounded to cents, the minimum mathematical revenue is **$1,415.79** under those fictional assumptions.

Again: this is a threshold calculation, not full accounting profit.

# 4. Changed Evidence Means Recalculate

A load decision is not frozen when the first calculation is completed.

If a material fact changes, recalculate the affected economics.

Examples:

- actual origin deadhead is 95 miles, not the 25 initially believed;
- the truck's usable MPG for this freight is 6.2 rather than 7.0;
- a toll route is required;
- an assumed factoring option does not apply;
- a stop charge becomes verified and earned;
- a claimed detention amount turns out to be only a possible future payment.

Professional error:

> “I already ran the load, so I'm sticking with the first answer.”

Professional response:

> “The evidence changed. I am updating the calculation and reclassifying the recommendation.”

A changed answer based on changed evidence is not inconsistency. It is disciplined decision-making.

# 5. Possible Revenue Is Not Current Revenue

A load may contain opportunities for additional revenue.

That does not mean the money exists yet.

Keep three states separate:

1. **Verified current revenue** — supported and countable now.
2. **Possible conditional revenue** — may become payable if a triggering event occurs and requirements are satisfied.
3. **Earned but pending documentation/payment** — triggering event occurred, but documentation or payment processing may still be incomplete.

Never move State 2 into State 1 just to make a load meet a target.

Example:

A rate confirmation says detention may pay after a documented threshold. The truck has not been detained.

Correct current revenue treatment: **$0 detention earned at this time**.

The existence of a clause is not the same as the occurrence of the event.

---

# 6. Cash Flow Is a Timing Question; Profitability Is an Economics Question

Factoring and quick pay can put cash into the carrier's hands sooner.

That can be operationally valuable.

But faster payment does not automatically make the load more profitable.

Suppose a $2,000 invoice can be:

- paid later at $2,000; or
- quick-paid now with a fictional 3% fee.

Quick-pay fee:

```text
$2,000 × 3% = $60
```

Cash arrives faster, but the transaction gives up $60.

The dispatcher should present both facts:

- **timing benefit:** earlier cash;
- **economic cost:** $60 fee under the stated fictional terms.

The carrier decides which matters more in its current situation.

---

# 7. Positioning Must Stay Outside Guaranteed Revenue

A destination can improve the next move.

That is important.

But “good market” does not equal guaranteed dollars.

Correct reasoning:

> “Load B leaves the truck in the exercise's stronger outbound area with less expected repositioning, so positioning favors Load B.”

Incorrect reasoning:

> “Load B is worth another $500 because the market is better.”

Unless there is actual evidence supporting a defined economic value, keep positioning as a separately stated planning factor.

---

# 8. Do Not Let One Metric Control the Entire Decision

A professional comparison uses multiple layers:

1. verified applicable revenue;
2. total trip miles;
3. loaded RPM;
4. effective RPM;
5. fuel;
6. verified trip-specific costs/fees;
7. stated-cost contribution margin;
8. possible revenue excluded from current economics;
9. carrier-specific threshold if supplied;
10. positioning and supplied operational constraints;
11. material unknowns;
12. carrier final authority.

A high loaded RPM can coexist with poor deadhead economics.

A lower effective RPM can coexist with more absolute contribution dollars.

A mathematically strong load can still require HOLD if a decision-critical fact is unresolved.

A lower current contribution can still be rational if the carrier explicitly values another verified factor.

The job is not to find one magic number. The job is to build the clearest economic picture possible from the evidence available.

---

# 9. Decision Record Standard

Before presenting a recommendation, the dispatcher should be able to answer:

- What revenue did I count?
- What possible revenue did I exclude?
- What total miles did I use?
- What costs did I subtract?
- What costs did I not subtract?
- Which inputs were provided/verified?
- Which were fictional training assumptions?
- Which remain unknown?
- What changed since the first calculation?
- What carrier criterion am I applying?
- What is my recommended disposition?
- What decision still belongs to the carrier?

If those answers cannot be reconstructed, the analysis is not yet a premium professional record.

# 10. Practice Cases

## Case 1 — Unknown Deadhead

**Fictional training case:**

Revenue $1,500; loaded miles 550; truck MPG 6.8; fuel $3.70. Truck location has not been confirmed.

What is the correct immediate disposition?

**Answer standard:** HOLD FOR DATA. Origin deadhead is decision-critical and cannot be assumed to be zero.

## Case 2 — True Zero vs Unknown

Carrier confirms it is not using factoring for this load.

Correct entry: **Factoring fee = $0 — P**.

This is different from leaving the factoring field unresolved.

## Case 3 — Counter Arithmetic

Carrier states minimum acceptable effective RPM is $2.20. Total trip miles are 640.

```text
$2.20 × 640 = $1,408
```

Current offer: $1,320.

Economic gap:

```text
$1,408 − $1,320 = $88
```

Correct Module 07 conclusion: current offer is $88 below the carrier-provided mathematical threshold. A counter disposition is supportable if the carrier's operating procedure permits it. Module 08 owns negotiation execution.

## Case 4 — Changed Fuel Input

Initial training assumption: 7 MPG.

Carrier then provides truck-specific MPG: 6.1.

Correct action: recalculate gallons and fuel using 6.1 MPG, then reconsider the load. Do not preserve the first economics just because they looked better.

## Case 5 — Possible Detention

A rate confirmation contains a detention clause. No qualifying delay has occurred.

Correct treatment: do not add detention to current revenue.

## Case 6 — Faster Cash

Two payment options produce different fee/timing outcomes.

Correct analysis: compare both the cash timing and the fee. Do not state that the faster option is “more profitable” solely because the money arrives sooner.

# 11. Final Module 07 Decision Sequence

```text
RECEIVE LOAD DATA
↓
CLASSIFY P / U / A
↓
IDENTIFY MATERIAL UNKNOWNS
↓
HOLD FOR DATA IF DECISION-CRITICAL INPUT IS UNRESOLVED
↓
CALCULATE TOTAL TRIP MILES
↓
CALCULATE LOADED RPM + EFFECTIVE RPM
↓
CALCULATE FUEL + VERIFIED TRIP COSTS
↓
SEPARATE CURRENT REVENUE FROM POSSIBLE REVENUE
↓
CALCULATE + CORRECTLY LABEL CONTRIBUTION MARGIN
↓
APPLY CARRIER-PROVIDED ECONOMIC CRITERIA
↓
WEIGH POSITIONING / PROVIDED OPERATIONAL FACTORS
↓
TAKE / COUNTER / HOLD FOR DATA / DECLINE
↓
RECORD BASIS
↓
PRESENT TO CARRIER / FOLLOW AUTHORIZED CARRIER PROCEDURE
```

That is the decision discipline SIM-007 will require.
