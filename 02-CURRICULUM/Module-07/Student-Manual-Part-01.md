**ELITE DISPATCHER ACADEMY**

**Student Manual — Module 07**

Rate Mathematics & Profitability

# 1. Module Introduction

Module 06 taught you how to verify that a broker, contact, and payment path are safe enough to proceed with. This module answers a different question: once a load is verified, is it actually a good load?

**IMPORTANT:** A high loaded rate per mile does not necessarily mean a better load. This is the single most important idea in this module. A load that looks great on a rate confirmation can perform poorly once deadhead, fuel, and other costs are accounted for — and a load that looks average can position the truck better for what comes next.

A dispatcher evaluates load economics. The dispatcher does not set the carrier's cost structure, does not decide the carrier's acceptable margin, and does not replace the carrier's own accounting. The carrier determines its actual costs, operating requirements, and final load-acceptance criteria. Your job is to bring the carrier accurate numbers, not to make the carrier's business decisions for it.

# 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

* Calculate total trip miles, loaded rate per mile, and effective revenue per total mile for a proposed load.
* Estimate fuel gallons and fuel cost for a trip using a truck's fuel efficiency.
* Calculate dispatcher fees and illustrative factoring/quick-pay costs.
* Calculate estimated contribution margin and explain why it is not the same as net profit.
* Explain the break-even concept and why break-even figures are carrier-specific.
* Compare two or more competing loads using effective revenue rather than loaded rate per mile alone.
* Identify accessorial items (detention, TONU, layover, lumper fees) that must be verified against actual documentation rather than assumed.
* Correctly distinguish revenue, cash flow, contribution, and profit — and avoid using them interchangeably.

# 3. Key Vocabulary

This module uses precise terms. They are not interchangeable, and confusing them leads to bad load decisions.

* **Gross linehaul revenue** — the base line-haul payment for moving the freight, before accessorials.
* **Accessorial revenue/reimbursements** — additional payments tied to specific events (detention, layover, TONU, lumper reimbursement) when they apply and are documented.
* **Total gross load revenue** — gross linehaul revenue plus any applicable accessorial revenue actually earned on that load.
* **Loaded miles** — miles driven while carrying the paid freight, from pickup to delivery.
* **Origin deadhead** — miles driven empty to reach the pickup location.
* **Destination/repositioning deadhead** — miles driven empty after delivery when relevant to evaluating that load's overall cost (for example, to reach the next viable load zone).
* **Total trip miles** — origin deadhead plus loaded miles plus relevant destination/repositioning deadhead.
* **Rate per loaded mile (Loaded RPM)** — gross load revenue divided by loaded miles only. This is the number most commonly advertised on a load board or rate confirmation.
* **Effective/all-in revenue per total mile (Effective RPM)** — total gross load revenue divided by total trip miles. This is a more complete picture of what the load actually pays per mile driven.
* **Fuel efficiency / MPG** — miles per gallon for the specific truck and load condition.
* **Estimated gallons consumed** — total trip miles divided by MPG.
* **Estimated fuel cost** — estimated gallons multiplied by fuel price per gallon.
* **Tolls and route-specific expenses** — documented costs tied to the specific route.
* **Dispatcher compensation** — the agreed fee paid to the dispatcher, commonly structured as a percentage of applicable revenue under the dispatcher services agreement.
* **Factoring costs** — the fee a factoring company charges to purchase a carrier's invoice early.
* **Quick-pay costs** — a fee some brokers or factors charge for expedited payment.
* **Variable operating costs** — costs that change with the trip (fuel, tolls, this-load-specific fees).
* **Fixed/period operating costs** — costs that exist regardless of any single load (insurance, equipment payments, permits) and are not calculated per-trip in this module.
* **Estimated contribution margin** — applicable load revenue minus the included variable trip costs for that specific calculation. This is *not* net profit.
* **Break-even concept** — the cost per mile at which a truck's applicable operating costs are exactly covered, with no margin above or below. Carrier-specific.
* **Cash flow** — the actual movement of money in and out over a period; not the same as revenue or profit.
* **Revenue** — money earned from a load before any costs are subtracted.
* **Expense** — a cost incurred in earning that revenue.
* **Margin** — the difference between revenue and a defined set of costs, always with those costs specified.
* **Accounting profit** — a carrier's full financial result after all recognized costs, calculated using the carrier's own accounting methodology — not something a dispatcher calculates from a single load.

**IMPORTANT:** Notice how many of these terms sound similar but mean different things. "Contribution margin" is not "profit." "Revenue" is not "cash flow." Getting this vocabulary precise is not pedantic — it's the difference between giving a carrier an honest picture and giving them a misleading one.

# 4. Core Formulas

Learn these formulas in order. Each later formula builds on an earlier one.

**Total Trip Miles**

```
Origin Deadhead
+ Loaded Miles
+ Relevant Destination/Repositioning Deadhead
= Total Trip Miles
```

**Loaded Rate Per Mile**

```
Gross Load Revenue ÷ Loaded Miles = Loaded RPM
```

**Effective Revenue Per Total Mile**

```
Total Gross Load Revenue ÷ Total Trip Miles = Effective RPM
```

**Estimated Fuel Gallons**

```
Total Trip Miles ÷ Truck MPG = Estimated Gallons
```

**Estimated Fuel Cost**

```
Estimated Gallons × Fuel Price Per Gallon = Estimated Fuel Cost
```

**Percentage Dispatcher Fee**

```
Contractually Applicable Revenue × Dispatcher Percentage = Dispatcher Fee
```

**Factoring/Transaction Fee (Illustrative)**

```
Applicable Invoice Amount × Contractual Fee Percentage = Estimated Fee
```

**Estimated Contribution Margin**

```
Applicable Load Revenue − Included Variable Trip Costs = Estimated Contribution Margin
```

**IMPORTANT:** Never call contribution margin "net profit." Every time you calculate it, state exactly which costs were included and which were not. A contribution-margin figure that doesn't include driver pay, maintenance allocation, insurance, or equipment cost is not a complete profitability picture — it's one useful piece of one.

**Break-Even Cost Per Mile**

```
Applicable Operating Costs ÷ Applicable Miles = Break-Even Cost Per Mile
```

Actual carrier break-even figures depend entirely on that carrier's own cost structure and accounting methodology. A dispatcher does not calculate a carrier's true break-even point from a single load — that number belongs to the carrier's own books.

# 5. Worked Example 1 — Full Walkthrough

**FICTIONAL INSTRUCTIONAL ASSUMPTIONS — not sourced, not current market rates:**

* Gross Load Revenue: $1,500
* Loaded Miles: 600
* Origin Deadhead: 100 miles
* Destination/Repositioning Deadhead: 50 miles
* Fuel Efficiency: 7 MPG
* Illustrative Diesel Price: $4.00/gallon
* Tolls: $40
* Illustrative Dispatcher Fee: 5%
* Illustrative Factoring Fee: 3%

**Step 1 — Total Trip Miles**

100 + 600 + 50 = **750 miles**

**Step 2 — Loaded RPM**

$1,500 ÷ 600 = **$2.50 per loaded mile**

**Step 3 — Effective RPM**

$1,500 ÷ 750 = **$2.00 per total mile**

Notice already: the loaded RPM ($2.50) looks better than the effective RPM ($2.00). That 50-cent gap is exactly what "loaded mile blindness" hides — see Section 9 in Part 02.

**Step 4 — Estimated Fuel**

750 ÷ 7 = approximately **107.14 gallons**

107.14 × $4.00 = approximately **$428.57**

**Step 5 — Dispatcher Fee**

$1,500 × 5% = **$75**

**Step 6 — Factoring Fee**

$1,500 × 3% = **$45**

**Step 7 — Included Variable Costs**

$428.57 (fuel) + $40 (tolls) + $75 (dispatcher) + $45 (factoring) = **$588.57**

**Step 8 — Estimated Contribution Margin**

$1,500 − $588.57 = **$911.43**

**MANDATORY CAUTION: $911.43 IS NOT NET PROFIT.** This simplified exercise has not deducted driver compensation, maintenance/repair allocation, tires, DEF, insurance, equipment payments, permits, taxes, administrative expenses, or other carrier-specific costs. It shows only what's left after the specific variable costs listed above. All monetary percentages and prices in this scenario are fictional instructional assumptions unless independently sourced and dated.
