Common categories of missing information: exact origin, exact destination, commodity, weight, dimensions, stop count, loading method, unloading method, pickup hours, delivery hours, appointment/FCFS details, temperature, hazmat, special handling, equipment requirements, team/solo status, and accessorial expectations.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not make students (or, later, yourself on the job) guess at missing information to keep a deal moving. A gap gets verified or the load gets set aside — it doesn't get filled in with an assumption.

# 15\. Posting Age

**FACT-M05-004.** Posting age is a priority signal — it helps you decide what to look at first. It is NOT proof that a load is available or unavailable.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not teach universal age thresholds like "under 15 minutes is good" or "14 hours is stale" or "24 hours means unavailable." Platform refresh and removal behavior varies by platform and by poster — a load's actual availability always has to be verified directly, regardless of how old or fresh the posting looks. See Scenario S-05-06.

# 16\. Duplicate / Reposted Freight

**FACT-M05-005.** Similar-looking listings from multiple sources can have many different explanations.

* Separate legitimate freight that simply looks similar.
* Authorized co-brokering between two legitimate parties.
* A shipper tendering the same freight through multiple channels at once.
* A reposted or duplicated platform record with no ill intent.
* Unauthorized brokerage or fraud — a real possibility, but only one of several.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not diagnose double brokering just because two postings look alike. This module's response to a duplicate-looking posting is: flag it, verify the basic details, and route it to Module 06 for fraud screening if something remains genuinely unresolved — not to jump straight to a fraud conclusion here. See Scenario S-05-07.

# 17\. Availability Verification

**FACT-M05-009.** FOUND does not equal AVAILABLE.

A posting must be confirmed through the posting party or the applicable platform booking process before it's treated as currently available. Do not assume a load is still open simply because the posting remains visible — see Scenario S-05-12, where a promising-looking load turns out to already be covered.

# 18\. Broker Information at the Sourcing Stage

At this early stage, you may reasonably note: the broker/company name, MC number when displayed, a contact method, any internal Do-Not-Use restriction your company already maintains, and whatever basic information the platform itself provides.

**IMPORTANT:** Do not perform a complete fraud investigation at the sourcing stage. Identity verification, authority verification, fraud-indicator analysis, double-brokering investigation, suspicious contact-change detection, broker impersonation, and payment/credit risk assessment are all Module 06's territory — this module's job is simply to flag when something warrants that closer look.

# 19\. Shipper-Direct Sourcing — the Module 02 Boundary

**FACT-M05-010.** A dispatcher operating as a qualifying carrier-side agent may seek freight for the carrier they represent — including, where appropriate, directly from a shipper.

**IMPORTANT:** This does not license everything that might be called "shipper-direct sourcing." Do not teach independent dispatchers to solicit freight generally for an undefined network, to secure freight first and find a carrier for it afterward, to allocate shipper freight among multiple competing represented carriers, or to hold themselves out as a general transportation arranger. All of these cross back into the broker-territory conduct Module 02 describes — the bona-fide-agent/broker boundary from Module 02 applies here exactly as it did there. If shipper-direct sourcing starts to feel like arranging transportation generally rather than sourcing for one represented carrier, that's the signal to revisit Module 02's framework.

# 20\. Search Workflow

A repeatable workflow for sourcing:

* 1\. Carrier ready
* 2\. Driver ready
* 3\. Equipment ready
* 4\. Define search parameters
* 5\. Search sources
* 6\. Filter results
* 7\. Remove obvious bad fits
* 8\. Shortlist candidates
* 9\. Review postings
* 10\. Identify missing information
* 11\. Verify availability / details
* 12\. Route qualified candidate to next stage

Possible next stages, depending on what the candidate needs: Module 06 (broker/fraud screening), Module 07 (rate evaluation), Module 08 (negotiation), Module 09 (booking), Module 10 (trip planning), or Module 11 (HOS feasibility).

**IMPORTANT:** Real company workflows may overlap these steps or run them in a different order — this sequence is a teaching model, not a rigid procedure every dispatch desk follows identically.

# 21\. Common Beginner Mistakes

* Treating a posting as guaranteed available because it's still showing on the board (see Section 17).
* Assuming FCFS means the facility is always open (see Section 11).
* Filling in missing information with a guess instead of verifying it (see Section 14).
* Jumping to a fraud conclusion from a duplicate-looking posting instead of flagging and verifying (see Section 16).
* Pitching a load that obviously fails the carrier's own stated restrictions (see Sections 12–13).
* Treating posting age as a hard cutoff rather than a priority signal (see Section 15).

# 22\. Scenario Lab

### S-05-01: Equipment Mismatch — Van vs. Flatbed

A carrier has a dry van available. A posting explicitly requires flatbed/open-deck equipment with a 4-foot tarp requirement.

**Classification:** BAD FIT / REJECT.

This is a straightforward equipment-type mismatch from the fit screen in Section 12 — a dry van cannot fulfill an explicit open-deck-with-tarp requirement. No further investigation is needed; this candidate is screened out immediately.

### S-05-02: Deadhead Exceeds Carrier Policy

The carrier's profile caps origin deadhead at 100 miles. The candidate load's pickup is 350 miles from the truck's current position.

**Classification:** BAD FIT / REJECT based on carrier policy.

This isn't a regulatory issue — it's a straightforward carrier-policy violation (Section 13). The carrier has already told you their limit; a 350-mile deadhead against a 100-mile policy is outside it, regardless of how good the load otherwise looks.

### S-05-03: Restricted Lane

The carrier's profile states "no Northeast." The candidate load delivers to Brooklyn, New York.

**Classification:** BAD FIT / REJECT.

Brooklyn falls within the Northeast region the carrier has explicitly restricted. This is a direct carrier-preference conflict (Section 13), not a case requiring further judgment.

A posting shows origin, destination, weight, and equipment type, but no commodity description.
