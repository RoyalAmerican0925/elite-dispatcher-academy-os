# Module 02 Student Manual — Part 03

## 21. Operational Boundary Decision Tool

Knowing definitions is not enough. A working dispatcher needs a repeatable method for deciding what to do when a freight opportunity, carrier relationship, or request creates a boundary question.

Use this full operational chain:

**STOP — IDENTIFY — TRACE — CHECK — CLASSIFY — ACT — DOCUMENT — ESCALATE**

This is an academy decision-control model for issue spotting and operational discipline. It is not a federal legal test, individualized legal advice, or a legal safe harbor.

### STOP — Pause Before Acting

Do not assume a title — “dispatcher,” “dispatch service,” “bona fide agent,” “1099 contractor,” or any other label — makes proposed conduct lawful. Pause before accepting, assigning, redirecting, or representing that you can cover freight.

Ask first: **What am I actually being asked to do?**

A professional dispatcher does not let urgency erase the boundary analysis.

### IDENTIFY — Determine Who You Are Acting For

Identify the represented principal and the relationship before analyzing the load.

Ask:

- Which carrier am I representing in this transaction?
- Is there a preexisting continuing relationship with that carrier?
- Am I following that carrier's instructions, equipment, lane, and operating requirements?
- Are multiple represented carriers relevant to the same freight?
- Has no specific represented carrier been identified yet?
- Am I being asked to act for a shipper or to arrange transportation generally rather than act for a named carrier?

Do not infer the principal merely from who sent the latest email or who is willing to pay a fee.

### TRACE — Follow the Freight Opportunity From Its Source

Trace the opportunity before deciding what may be done with it.

Ask:

- Where did this freight opportunity originate?
- Did it come from a broker, shipper, load board, represented carrier, or another source?
- Was a particular represented carrier already tied to the opportunity when it reached me?
- If that carrier cannot or will not perform, am I returning/canceling the opportunity or redirecting it to another carrier?

This distinction matters. Returning an opportunity to its source does not require the dispatcher to select another carrier. Redirecting or substituting another independent represented carrier can introduce an allocation decision.

### CHECK — Separate Verified Facts From Assumptions

Check the facts that actually control the situation.

At minimum determine:

- Who selected or will select the carrier?
- Could more than one represented carrier reasonably haul this load?
- If several carriers could haul it, who decides which one receives it?
- Who pays the dispatch service, and what is the source of the funds?
- Does the dispatch service receive or control freight funds between shipper and carrier?
- Is freight being sourced for a named represented carrier, or is transportation being offered generally?
- Is there a preexisting continuing carrier relationship?
- Are actual operations consistent with the agreement or relationship being claimed?

Write **UNKNOWN** when a material fact has not been verified. Missing information is not permission to choose the most convenient answer.

### CLASSIFY — Make a Preliminary Operational Read

After identifying and checking the material facts, classify the situation as one of the following:

1. **Consistent with carrier-side agency** — the verified facts fit the carrier-representation model taught in this module.
2. **Fact-specific / insufficient verified information** — a material fact is missing or mixed, so a reliable operational decision cannot yet be made.
3. **Raises significant brokerage risk** — the facts show arranging or allocation conduct that materially conflicts with the carrier-side agency boundary taught in this module.

A classification without reasoning is incomplete. Record the controlling facts and identify what fact, if changed, could materially change the classification.

“More facts required” is a valid professional outcome when the missing information is genuinely material. Guessing is not.

### ACT — Convert the Analysis Into an Operational Decision

Choose the next action supported by the verified facts:

- **PROCEED** — current facts support ordinary carrier-side activity.
- **HOLD** — pause the proposed activity while material facts remain unresolved.
- **VERIFY** — obtain specified evidence or information before deciding.
- **ESCALATE** — qualified regulatory/legal review is needed before proceeding.
- **DO NOT PROCEED AS PROPOSED** — the current fact pattern creates a material boundary problem; return, decline, or restructure the proposed action rather than improvising.

“Proceed” is an operational decision based on the facts currently verified. It is not a legal certification or guarantee.

### DOCUMENT — Leave a Professional Decision Record

A dispatcher should be able to explain what happened without reconstructing the situation from memory.

A usable case note records:

- the situation or request;
- the represented carrier/principal;
- freight source;
- key verified facts;
- material unknowns;
- preliminary classification and reasoning;
- action taken or held;
- verification performed or requested; and
- next owner/follow-up.

Do not write unsupported conclusions such as “this is definitely legal” or “this company is definitely acting illegally.” Record facts, operational concerns, actions, and unresolved questions.

### ESCALATE — Make a Professional Handoff

If the verified facts create genuine uncertainty about whether broker authority or another regulatory requirement may apply, do not guess through the boundary.

A useful escalation states:

1. what is known;
2. what remains unresolved;
3. what activity is being held or creates the operational risk;
4. what supporting documents/facts have been gathered; and
5. what decision requires qualified review.

Example professional handoff:

> “I have paused this action because the verified facts raise a carrier-agency/brokerage boundary question. I confirmed the represented carrier and source of the opportunity, but who has authority to redirect the freight remains unresolved. Before proceeding, I need a qualified determination or additional verified information on that issue.”

### Full Decision Chain Example

A shipper sends a load directly to a dispatch service that represents three overlapping dry-van carriers and says, “Give this to whichever truck can cover it fastest.”

**STOP:** Do not immediately promise coverage.

**IDENTIFY:** No single represented carrier was tied to the opportunity when the shipper sent it. Three client carriers could potentially be relevant.

**TRACE:** The opportunity came directly from the shipper to the dispatch service, not through one represented carrier.

**CHECK:** All three carriers have overlapping equipment/lane capability, and the shipper is asking the dispatch service to select the carrier. Those facts create discretionary allocation. If the relationship, authority, or source facts are incomplete, record the missing facts rather than assuming them.

**CLASSIFY:** Significant brokerage risk on the supplied facts because the dispatcher is being asked to select among competing carriers for shipper freight.

**ACT:** Do not proceed as proposed. Hold/return/decline the activity or escalate for qualified review before changing the business model.

**DOCUMENT:** Record the shipper request, candidate carriers, freight source, selection request, unresolved authority question, and action taken.

**ESCALATE:** Provide the known facts and documents and ask whether the proposed activity requires broker authority or restructuring before proceeding.

**IMPORTANT:** This tool is educational issue-spotting and operational discipline. Correct use does not guarantee a particular legal outcome.

# 22. Scenario Lab

### Scenario 1: One-Carrier Load-Board Sourcing

*All names and companies are fictional.*

Dispatcher Renata has an ongoing agreement with Hallmark Freightways, a single for-hire carrier. She searches a load board daily for freight that fits Hallmark's trucks and books loads exclusively on Hallmark's behalf.

**Classification:** Consistent with carrier-side agency, subject to the complete relationship and conduct.

Renata represents one carrier under an ongoing relationship and is not allocating traffic between competing carriers. This is a clear example of lower-risk carrier-side dispatching in this module, though the complete relationship and conduct still control rather than the single-carrier fact alone.

**Operational application:** IDENTIFY Hallmark as the represented carrier; TRACE the opportunity from the load board for Hallmark; CHECK that no competing-carrier allocation is occurring; CLASSIFY from the complete facts; ACT only within Hallmark's authorized scope; DOCUMENT material booking facts.

### Scenario 2: General Shipper Solicitation

*All names and companies are fictional.*

A dispatch service sends shippers a message stating: “I can arrange transportation for your freight using my network of carriers.”

**Classification:** Raises significant brokerage risk.

This is an offer to arrange transportation generally, not sourcing for one specific represented carrier. It closely tracks the broker-function concern discussed earlier in this module.

**Operational application:** STOP before accepting shipper freight; IDENTIFY whether any specific carrier is actually represented for the opportunity; TRACE the shipper-direct source; CHECK who will choose the carrier; CLASSIFY the supplied conduct as high risk; DO NOT PROCEED AS PROPOSED without appropriate authority/restructuring or qualified review; DOCUMENT and ESCALATE.

### Scenario 3: Non-Overlapping Equipment

*All names and companies are fictional.*

A dispatch service represents Carrier A (reefer-equipped only) and Carrier B (flatbed-equipped only). A frozen-freight opportunity comes in that only reefer equipment can haul.

**Classification:** Lower allocation-of-traffic risk due to non-overlap on the supplied facts.

Because only Carrier A can physically haul the stated freight, the supplied facts do not present a meaningful choice between the two carriers. Non-overlap is relevant to allocation analysis, but it is not an automatic legal safe harbor.

**Operational application:** TRACE the freight source, verify the equipment requirement and carrier capabilities, and record why the supplied facts do not create a competing-carrier selection decision. If another carrier could actually perform, re-run the analysis.

### Scenario 4: Overlapping Carriers

*All names and companies are fictional.*

A dispatch service represents three dry-van carriers, all capable of hauling a given lane. The dispatcher picks one to receive the load.

**Classification:** Raises significant allocation-of-traffic concerns.

All three carriers could reasonably haul the freight, and the dispatcher is deciding which receives it.

**Operational application:** STOP; TRACE the freight source; CHECK who has selection discretion and whether a carrier was already tied to the opportunity; CLASSIFY based on those facts; do not invent a carrier-count safe harbor; HOLD or ESCALATE when the proposed selection crosses the carrier-side boundary.

### Scenario 5: Breakdown and Cancellation

*All names and companies are fictional.*

Carrier A, the only carrier represented for this load, experiences a mechanical breakdown and cannot perform. The dispatcher cancels the load and returns the opportunity to the broker who originally offered it.

**Classification:** Lower risk — consistent with carrier-side agency on the supplied facts.

The dispatcher is not redirecting the freight to another represented carrier. The opportunity returns to its source and no competing-carrier allocation decision is made.

**Operational application:** TRACE the original broker source, DOCUMENT the breakdown/cancellation, return the opportunity through the authorized channel, and avoid treating the cancellation as authority to find a replacement carrier for the broker.

### Scenario 6: Breakdown and Reassignment

*All names and companies are fictional.*

Carrier A cannot perform due to a breakdown. Instead of canceling, the dispatcher substitutes Carrier B — a different, independent carrier the dispatch service also represents — to complete the load.

**Classification:** Materially different, higher-risk analysis.

The dispatcher has moved from representing Carrier A on its load to selecting a different independent carrier for the freight. That introduces an allocation concern even though the problem began with a breakdown.

**Operational application:** STOP the substitution; TRACE who controls the freight after Carrier A cannot perform; CHECK whether anyone authorized the dispatch service to arrange replacement transportation and whether that authority itself changes the regulatory role; CLASSIFY from the verified facts; return/cancel or ESCALATE rather than assuming replacement authority.

### Scenario 7: Contract Title vs. Conduct

*All names and companies are fictional.*

A dispatch service has a signed agreement titled “Bona Fide Agent Agreement” with each carrier it represents. In daily practice, however, the dispatcher regularly decides which of several overlapping carriers receives each incoming load.

**Classification:** Raises significant brokerage risk on the supplied conduct.

The agreement title does not erase contradictory day-to-day conduct. The operational analysis must follow what the business actually does.

**Operational application:** CHECK the actual freight-source and selection workflow; DOCUMENT the mismatch between claimed relationship and conduct; do not approve the activity merely because of the contract title; HOLD/ESCALATE the conflicting business process.

### Scenario 8: Compensation Source Comparison

*All names and companies are fictional.*

Compare two dispatch services: Service X is paid a monthly fee directly by the one carrier it represents. Service Y receives the freight charge from the shipper, deducts its fee, and forwards the balance to the carrier.

**Classification:** Service X's supplied facts support carrier-agent treatment. Service Y's money flow is a strong brokerage-risk indicator requiring full analysis.

Payment source is relevant but not a single-factor legal test.

**Operational application:** TRACE the funds and freight separately; CHECK the represented principal, freight source, carrier-selection authority, and actual conduct; do not classify solely from the fee method.

# 24. Module Summary

You should now be able to:

- describe what a carrier-side dispatcher does and identify the represented principal;
- distinguish carrier-side dispatch activity from freight-brokerage risk using the complete fact pattern rather than titles;
- explain broker and bona fide-agent concepts in plain language;
- trace a freight opportunity to its source and distinguish cancellation/return from reassignment;
- analyze allocation of traffic and explain why discretionary carrier selection matters;
- recognize how compensation, money flow, contracts, carrier count, and tax status can be relevant without becoming single-factor safe harbors;
- preserve **insufficient verified information** as a valid professional classification instead of guessing;
- identify the controlling facts and explain what changed fact could alter the classification;
- select an operational response — PROCEED, HOLD, VERIFY, ESCALATE, or DO NOT PROCEED AS PROPOSED;
- create a professional case note that separates verified facts from assumptions;
- make a professional escalation handoff without pretending to provide a legal conclusion; and
- use the full **STOP — IDENTIFY — TRACE — CHECK — CLASSIFY — ACT — DOCUMENT — ESCALATE** workflow on realistic dispatch-desk situations.
