# Module 02 Student Manual — Part 05

## 25. Dispatcher Authority Matrix

A dispatcher does not possess unlimited authority merely because a carrier has hired or contracted with the dispatcher. Authority is delegated. It must be identified by function.

| Authority Domain | Lower-Risk Authorized Activity When Delegated | Hold / Escalate Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Search Authority | Search for freight matching the represented carrier's equipment, location, availability, and instructions. | General freight solicitation not tied to an identified represented carrier; freight obtained first and allocated later. |
| Negotiation Authority | Negotiate rates and ordinary accessorials for the represented carrier within stated limits. | Negotiating an undisclosed spread; negotiating as though the dispatcher owns the freight; unclear authority to bind carrier. |
| Acceptance Authority | Accept a load that fits preapproved carrier parameters if carrier has expressly delegated this authority. | Load exceeds equipment, insurance, geographic, commodity, timing, or operational limits; unusual terms; carrier approval reserved. |
| Signature Authority | Sign a load-specific rate confirmation when written or otherwise reliable delegated authority clearly permits it. | Broad broker-carrier agreement, indemnity, waiver, guaranty, settlement, unusual liability language, or unclear signature capacity. |
| Driver Instruction Authority | Communicate pickup/delivery data, tracking instructions, routing information, appointment changes, and carrier-approved operating directions. | Instruction requires unsafe, unlawful, deceptive, or contractually unauthorized conduct. |
| Claims Authority | Gather facts, preserve documents, report OS&D, and escalate. | Admit liability, waive defenses, settle a claim, sign releases, or authorize deductions without carrier authority. |
| Billing Authority | Assemble POD, BOL, lumper receipts, rate confirmation, invoice package, and submit according to carrier/factor workflow. | Redirect payment, alter payee, accept freight proceeds personally, or change factoring instructions without verified authority. |
| Payment Authority | Limited administrative support according to carrier-approved workflow. | Receiving shipper freight charges and redistributing carrier funds; controlling transaction proceeds in a manner resembling brokerage. |
| Contract Authority | Routine load-specific commitments only if expressly delegated. | New broker-carrier master terms, personal guarantees, broad indemnity, forum/venue changes, arbitration terms, or material amendments. |
| Legal / Insurance Authority | Gather documents, verify records, report discrepancies. | Interpret coverage conclusively, make legal admissions, bind insurance, alter COIs, or use another carrier's credentials. |

### Authority Rule

Before acting, ask:

1. **What authority am I using?**
2. **Where does that authority come from?**
3. **Is the authority specific enough for this action?**
4. **Does the carrier retain any approval right?**
5. **Would the action create a new contractual, financial, insurance, or legal obligation?**

When the answer is unclear, the correct operational response is **HOLD → VERIFY → ESCALATE**.

## 26. Rate Confirmation Authority Exercise

A rate confirmation is load-specific, but it can still contain consequential terms. Before accepting or signing, confirm:

- legal carrier name;
- carrier MC/USDOT information where applicable;
- pickup and delivery locations and appointments;
- commodity and weight;
- equipment requirements;
- rate and accessorial structure;
- detention, layover, TONU, lumper, tracking, and paperwork conditions;
- whether the document incorporates a separate broker-carrier agreement;
- whether special indemnity, cargo, offset, rebrokering, confidentiality, tracking, or penalty terms appear;
- whether the dispatcher has authority to sign in the carrier's name or as authorized agent.

### Decision Outcomes

- **ACCEPT** — within delegated authority and carrier parameters.
- **COUNTER** — commercial terms require negotiation but remain within delegated authority.
- **HOLD** — material fact or authority is missing.
- **CARRIER APPROVAL REQUIRED** — carrier reserved the decision or term exceeds dispatch authority.
- **LEGAL/CONTRACT ESCALATION** — unusual or material liability/contract terms require qualified review.
- **DECLINE** — load or terms do not satisfy carrier instructions or cannot be accepted lawfully/safely.

### Exercise 26-A

The carrier gave the dispatcher written authority to negotiate and sign ordinary rate confirmations up to $4,000 in linehaul revenue. A rate confirmation offers $3,250 but adds a clause stating that the carrier waives all claims against the broker for cargo deductions and agrees to indemnify the broker for any third-party loss, whether caused by carrier negligence or not.

**Correct operational response:** **HOLD / CARRIER APPROVAL REQUIRED / LEGAL-CONTRACT ESCALATION.** The dollar amount is inside the dispatcher's commercial ceiling, but the liability language is not an ordinary rate term. Delegated price authority is not the same as authority to accept broad indemnity or waiver obligations.

## 27. Dispatcher-Carrier Agreement Educational Model

This model is an instructional framework for clause recognition and operating design. It is not represented as guaranteed legal in every jurisdiction and should receive jurisdiction-appropriate legal review before commercial use.

### 27.1 Parties and Legal Identity

Identify the carrier and dispatcher accurately. The carrier's legal name, operating identifiers, business address, and authorized representative should match independently verified records.

### 27.2 Continuing Relationship

The agreement should describe an ongoing carrier-service relationship rather than a freight-first arrangement in which the dispatch service acquires traffic and later selects a carrier.

### 27.3 Scope of Services

Services may include carrier-directed load search, rate negotiation, appointment coordination, tracking support, document collection, accessorial support, and administrative billing/factoring assistance.

### 27.4 Carrier Direction and Control

The carrier retains control over its equipment, drivers, safety obligations, operating authority, insurance, commodities, service areas, and any load-acceptance decisions not expressly delegated.

### 27.5 Load-Selection Authority

The agreement should state who holds ultimate selection authority. Even when acceptance authority is delegated within defined parameters, the workflow must not give the dispatcher independent discretion to allocate freight among competing carriers in a manner inconsistent with the bona fide-agent framework.

### 27.6 Negotiation Authority

Define whether the dispatcher may negotiate rate, detention, layover, TONU, lumper, and other accessorials and whether minimum/maximum thresholds require carrier approval.

### 27.7 Signature Authority

Define exactly what documents may be signed and what documents require carrier approval. A narrow authority to execute routine rate confirmations should not be read as blanket authority to sign master broker-carrier agreements, guarantees, claim releases, or material liability amendments.

### 27.8 Driver Communication

Define dispatch communications allowed for pickup, delivery, tracking, routing, appointment, and operating updates. Nothing authorizes unsafe, unlawful, deceptive, or misrepresentative instructions.

### 27.9 Billing and Factoring Support

Define administrative responsibilities for assembling BOL/POD, receipts, rate confirmation, invoices, and factor submissions. The carrier remains the transportation provider and payee unless a lawful separate arrangement says otherwise.

### 27.10 Claims and OS&D

Dispatcher may gather and relay facts. Liability admissions, settlements, releases, claim waivers, and material deductions require carrier authority or escalation.

### 27.11 Compensation

State the dispatcher's fee and payer clearly. A percentage fee, fixed fee, weekly charge, monthly retainer, or other structure does not by itself decide broker status; the full relationship and conduct remain controlling.

### 27.12 No Unauthorized Brokerage / Rebrokering

The dispatcher does not acquire shipper freight for later allocation among carriers, hold itself out as arranging transportation generally without required authority, conceal the actual carrier, or transfer committed freight to another independent carrier without required authorization and boundary analysis.

### 27.13 No Assumption of Carrier Liability

Dispatcher does not become the motor carrier, insurer, cargo claimant, guarantor, or legal decision-maker merely by providing dispatch services.

### 27.14 Data, Confidentiality, and Records

Define access to carrier credentials, load records, rate data, driver information, factoring data, and termination handling. Credentials must not be shared with unauthorized parties.

### 27.15 Termination

Define termination rights, treatment of in-transit loads, outstanding invoices, return/deletion of credentials, and record retention.

### 27.16 Governing Law / Disputes / Entire Agreement

These are jurisdiction-sensitive commercial terms. Students must recognize them and know when legal review is appropriate rather than invent a universal clause.

## 28. Defective Agreement Lab

For each clause below, identify the risk, severity, operational consequence, and conceptual correction.

### Defect A

**Clause:** “Dispatcher may assign any freight opportunity to any carrier in Dispatcher's network.”

**Risk:** High. It describes discretionary freight allocation, a core brokerage indicator.

**Conceptual correction:** Tie searches and opportunities to an identified represented carrier and remove independent network-allocation authority.

### Defect B

**Clause:** “Dispatcher may collect all shipper freight charges, deduct any amount it determines, and remit the balance to carrier.”

**Risk:** High. This creates strong brokerage/payment-flow indicators and undefined fee discretion.

**Conceptual correction:** Carrier compensation and dispatcher compensation should be stated transparently and handled through a structure consistent with the actual lawful relationship.

### Defect C

**Clause:** “Dispatcher may subcontract transportation to any third party.”

**Risk:** Critical. A dispatcher operating as carrier agent should not assume unrestricted power to arrange replacement transportation using unrelated carriers.

**Conceptual correction:** No substitution or re-tendering without appropriate carrier/broker authorization and regulatory analysis.

### Defect D

**Clause:** “Dispatcher may sign any document necessary to perform services.”

**Risk:** High. Unlimited signature authority can bind the carrier to unknown liabilities.

**Conceptual correction:** Enumerate permitted document types and escalation categories.

### Defect E

**Clause:** “Dispatcher may settle cargo claims on carrier's behalf.”

**Risk:** High.

**Conceptual correction:** Dispatcher gathers and transmits claim facts; settlement authority requires separate express delegation or qualified handling.

### Defect F

**Clause:** “Carrier has no right to reject loads selected by Dispatcher.”

**Risk:** High. It undermines carrier control and may strengthen evidence that the dispatch service is independently selecting transportation.

**Conceptual correction:** Carrier retains ultimate load-selection authority unless narrow acceptance authority is expressly delegated within defined parameters.

## 29. Broker Authority Verification Worksheet

Verification must answer separate questions. Do not collapse them.

### Layer 1 — Entity

- What is the legal business name?
- What MC/USDOT identifiers are associated with the entity?
- Does the entity name match the load documentation?

### Layer 2 — Authority

- Is the required broker authority currently active?
- Is the authority type appropriate for the transaction?
- Are there revocation or inactive indicators requiring hold?

### Layer 3 — Financial Responsibility

- Is the required $75,000 broker financial responsibility reflected through an accepted BMC-84 or qualifying BMC-85 filing?
- Are current 2026 provider requirements satisfied where visible/appropriate to verify?

### Layer 4 — Identity

- Does the email domain match independently verified company contact information?
- Does the callback number match an independently obtained number rather than only the number in the incoming message?
- Are payment instructions consistent with known company information?
- Are there suspicious domain substitutions, free-email accounts, recently changed banking instructions, or mismatched signatures?

### Layer 5 — Credit / Commercial Risk

Authority and bond status do **not** establish credit quality. Factoring approval, payment history, credit terms, and business-risk review are separate.

### EDA Rule

**AUTHORITY ≠ IDENTITY ≠ FINANCIAL RESPONSIBILITY ≠ CREDIT QUALITY ≠ FACTORING APPROVAL.**

All five can produce different answers.

## 30. 2026 BMC-84 / BMC-85 Introductory Guide

For property brokers and applicable freight forwarders, 49 CFR § 387.307 requires $75,000 in qualifying financial security through the prescribed surety-bond or trust mechanism.

- **BMC-84** — evidence of surety bond.
- **BMC-85** — evidence of trust fund.

Beginning January 16, 2026, current rules include additional provider and trust-asset requirements. Students must keep distinct clocks distinct:

1. **Provider ineligibility:** FMCSA guidance states that affected brokers/freight forwarders relying on an ineligible BMC-85 trustee receive a 30-day replacement period after FMCSA determines the provider is ineligible.
2. **FMCSA pending-suspension response process:** § 387.307(e) contains a 7-business-day response/suspension process after FMCSA serves the applicable notice. Do not mislabel this as a generic “seven-day replenishment rule.”
3. **Trust liquidity:** qualifying BMC-85 trust assets must be capable of liquidation to cash within 7 calendar days.
4. **Provider notification:** specified provider notification duties can operate on a 2-business-day clock under § 387.307(e).

The purpose of this section is issue recognition, not broker-licensing instruction.

## 31. Broker-Carrier Agreement Forensics

The **broker-carrier agreement** generally governs the commercial relationship between broker and carrier. The **rate confirmation** generally contains shipment-specific commercial and operational terms. A rate confirmation may incorporate the broker-carrier agreement, and conflicts can require escalation.

Students must recognize clauses concerning:

- payment timing and conditions;
- offset/setoff;
- cargo claims and deductions;
- indemnity;
- insurance;
- subcontracting/rebrokering;
- tracking and technology;
- detention, layover, TONU, lumper, and accessorial proof;
- confidentiality and non-solicitation;
- records;
- dispute resolution;
- governing law and venue;
- attorney fees;
- termination;
- limitation or expansion of liability.

**Professional rule:** **RECOGNIZE → FLAG → DOCUMENT → ESCALATE.**

## 32. Regulatory Source Lab

Rank the following for legal-authority purposes:

1. Federal statute.
2. Federal regulation.
3. Controlling judicial authority where relevant.
4. Official agency guidance.
5. Commercial contract.
6. Industry article or training website.
7. Social-media post.

### Key lesson

A source can be commercially important without being controlling law. A contract may bind the parties on lawful commercial terms, but it cannot legalize conduct that controlling law prohibits. Agency guidance can be highly important to understanding enforcement and interpretation while remaining distinct from the CFR itself.

## 33. Dispatcher Red-Flag System

Treat these statements as **stop-and-verify triggers**, not automatic proof of guilt:

- “We don't need a contract.”
- “Just use my friend's MC.”
- “Grab the load first and we'll find a truck later.”
- “The shipper pays you and you pay us.”
- “Don't tell the broker another carrier is hauling it.”
- “Change the carrier name on the paperwork.”
- “Use this other company's COI.”
- “The MC doesn't matter.”
- “Book it under Carrier A but Carrier B will actually run it.”
- “We call ourselves consultants, so Part 371 doesn't apply.”
- “If anybody asks, you're an employee.”
- “Just sign the broker agreement for the carrier.”
- “Don't show the owner the RC.”

**Response protocol:** **STOP → VERIFY → DOCUMENT → ESCALATE.**

A professional dispatcher does not solve an authority problem by hiding it.
