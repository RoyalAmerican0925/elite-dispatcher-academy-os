## Current-Source Verification Performed

Authoritative FMCSA sources were checked on 2026-08-27 for current registration terminology and requirements.

Verified current points:

- FMCSA continues to distinguish USDOT numbers from operating authority.
- FMCSA still describes operating authority using MC/FF/MX terminology, while Motus also displays new USDOT-number suffixes for entity/registration types.
- Motus registration modernization is current and date-sensitive.
- FMCSA currently describes a broker as arranging transportation without transporting the property, while a freight forwarder assumes transportation responsibility and performs additional consolidation/break-bulk/distribution functions.
- USDOT-number requirements depend on operation and vehicle/cargo/passenger criteria; private-carrier status alone does not create a universal exemption.

## Corrections / QA Items Identified

### 1. Module 01 Exam date-sensitivity label
The current exam states: `Date-sensitive content: None.` This is inaccurate because Module 01 includes current FMCSA registration-system language, including Motus and evolving registration identifiers. The exam/source-control metadata should require current-source reverification before publication.

### 2. Operating-authority wording
Exam wording such as `legal permission to operate as a for-hire carrier, broker, or freight forwarder in interstate commerce` is directionally useful but too broad if presented without qualification. FMCSA operating-authority requirements depend on the operation and regulated transportation involved. Final student wording should say that operating authority is the FMCSA registration required for specified regulated operations when applicable, rather than implying every possible for-hire movement requires identical authority.

### 3. Dispatcher location/relationship wording
The manual repeatedly describes the dispatcher as sitting or working `inside the carrier.` This is appropriate for an internal employee model but could mislead students about independent carrier-side dispatch services. Module 01 should preserve the academy's carrier-side model while avoiding a universal implication that every carrier-side dispatcher is literally an internal employee. Module 02 should remain the authoritative legal-boundary lesson.

### 4. Commercial-use student tools
Module 01 previously lacked dedicated standalone student tools. Two tools were added during this audit to satisfy the academy asset expectation for reusable operational/guided-practice resources.
