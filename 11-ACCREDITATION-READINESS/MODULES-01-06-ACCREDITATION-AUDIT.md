# Modules 01–06 Accreditation-Readiness Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-08-15
**Scope:** Existing Elite Dispatcher Academy student manuals and instructor answer guides for Modules 01–06.
**Purpose:** Curriculum-readiness review against current accreditation-oriented expectations from DEAC, ACCET, and Texas Career Schools & Colleges requirements. This is not a finding that the institution or program is accredited.

## Governing distinction
Accreditation is institutional. Strong modules are necessary but not sufficient. DEAC evaluates institutional mission, educational objectives/curricula/materials, educational and student-support services, achievement/satisfaction, faculty/administration qualifications, admissions, advertising/recruitment, financial responsibility, tuition policies, facilities/equipment, and institutional research/self-improvement. Texas authorization separately requires approved programs, catalog/enrollment materials, records, outcomes, policies, and other operational evidence.

## Overall finding
Modules 01–06 are substantively strong and usable as the instructional body for an accreditation-ready version. They already demonstrate organized sequencing, professional terminology, scope boundaries, practical application, scenario-based reasoning, explicit distinction among law/regulation/guidance/industry practice, and current-date verification flags. Instructor answer guides exist for all six modules.

The principal gap is not lack of teaching content. The principal gap is inconsistent formal accreditation evidence at the module level: measurable learning outcomes, outcome-to-assessment mapping, competency evidence, completion standards, source/revision metadata, and a uniform record showing how each module supports program outcomes.

## Required V2 corrections applied
1. Add stable module version and revision metadata.
2. Add measurable learning outcomes using observable verbs.
3. Map each outcome to required student evidence.
4. Identify assessment instruments and practical/scenario evidence.
5. Establish module completion criteria.
6. Add source-control and current-date review requirements.
7. Identify required student records/evidence to retain.
8. Identify prerequisite/dependency relationships.
9. Preserve legal-scope disclaimers and role boundaries.
10. Require accessibility and student-support review at publication gate.

## Module findings

### Module 01 — Trucking Industry Foundations
**Strengths:** Clear industry map; structured vocabulary; federal agency/registration distinctions; load lifecycle; explicit correction of beginner misconceptions; dispatcher-role scope.
**V2 need:** Convert the implicit instructional goals into measurable outcomes and require evidence that the learner can identify parties, classify authority/registration concepts, trace a load lifecycle, and distinguish interstate/intrastate and carrier/broker roles.

### Module 02 — Dispatcher Role & Legal Boundaries
**Strengths:** High-quality statutory/regulatory/guidance distinctions; bona fide agent and broker analysis; allocation-of-traffic framework; multiple-carrier nuance; current 2026 broker-authority/financial-security material; explicit legal-advice disclaimer.
**V2 need:** Require documented scenario analysis demonstrating that learners can identify facts that increase brokerage risk and explain when to stop/escalate rather than make unsupported legal conclusions. Current-law verification must be repeated at publication and periodically thereafter.

### Module 03 — Trucks, Trailers, Freight & Equipment
**Strengths:** Equipment/freight-fit reasoning; repeated anti-assumption controls; configuration-specific warnings; dimensional/weight distinctions; federal/state distinction; scenario-based application.
**V2 need:** Formal competency evidence showing the learner can screen equipment fit, identify missing facts, classify when specialized/oversize/hazmat/food issues require escalation, and avoid booking from weight or nominal dimensions alone.

### Module 04 — Carrier & Driver Onboarding
**Strengths:** Strong separation of carrier compliance responsibility from dispatcher operational readiness; current FMCSA-system flags; insurance distinctions; payment-change cybersecurity; dispatcher profile vs. DQ file; reverification tracker; structured onboarding workflow.
**V2 need:** Require completion of an onboarding case file/checklist as performance evidence. Time-sensitive Motus/medical-certificate material must be reverified before release. Student records should show successful completion of the onboarding exercise.

### Module 05 — Load Boards & Freight Sourcing
**Strengths:** Strong lead-versus-booked-load distinction; platform volatility warnings; missing-information workflow; fit screening; shipper-direct legal boundary; repeatable search workflow; extensive scenario lab.
**V2 need:** Require a documented load-candidate screening exercise in which the learner applies carrier profile, equipment, geography, missing-information, and next-stage routing criteria. Platform-specific claims require dated verification.

### Module 06 — Broker Verification & Fraud Prevention
**Strengths:** Layered verification model; entity/contact distinction; broker authority and financial security; BEC/account-takeover awareness; contact verification; document/payment-path controls; calibrated risk classifications; avoidance of unsupported fraud accusations.
**V2 need:** Require a scored verification case in which the learner documents sources checked, unresolved discrepancies, risk classification, and appropriate proceed/hold/escalate/decline decision. Current FMCSA-system information requires scheduled reverification.

## Publication gate for Modules 01–06 V2
Before any V2 module is labeled publication-ready:
- verify current regulatory and platform-specific statements;
- confirm outcomes align to assessments and instructor guides;
- confirm each required student activity produces retainable evidence;
- confirm accessibility of text, tables, links, and any multimedia;
- confirm no unsupported employment, earnings, licensing, accreditation, or certification claims;
- preserve version history and source-verification date;
- confirm curriculum changes are reflected in catalog/program documentation where required.

## Accreditation evidence to retain
For each module retain: approved module version, learning outcomes, assessment map, answer key/rubric, sample completed student work where permitted, completion records, revision history, source-verification record, instructor/subject-matter review record, accessibility review, and documented corrective changes arising from student outcomes or feedback.
