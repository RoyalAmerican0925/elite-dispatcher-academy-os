# Modules 01–06 Accreditation Gap Audit

## Scope
This audit reviews the existing Elite Dispatcher Academy Modules 01–06 against accreditation-readiness expectations relevant to a future distance-education institution. It is not a finding of accreditation and does not substitute for an accreditor's formal review.

## Executive Finding
Modules 01–06 are academically stronger than a typical first-draft vocational course. They already contain substantial evidence of instructional design quality: scoped module outcomes, applied dispatcher scenarios, knowledge checks, instructor answer guides, dependency maps, terminology controls, source classifications, current-date verification flags, and explicit boundaries between law, guidance, industry practice, carrier policy, and academy convention.

However, the modules are not yet accreditation-ready as a complete institutional submission. The largest remaining gaps are formal program-level outcome mapping, documented student workload/contact-hour equivalencies, standardized grading and mastery rules, documented student-instructor interaction expectations, accessibility/technology standards, evidence of student achievement and satisfaction, faculty qualification/supervision documentation, and a controlled evidence trail tying each module to institutional policies and continuous-improvement data.

## Accreditation Context
DEAC evaluates institutions across mission and objectives; educational program objectives, curricula and materials; educational services; student support; student achievement and satisfaction; administration and faculty qualifications; admissions; advertising/recruitment; financial responsibility; tuition policies; facilities/equipment; and research/self-improvement. DEAC also subjects educational offerings to subject-matter review for completeness, accuracy, currency, and alignment with stated educational outcomes.

ACCET similarly evaluates the institution, program design, training delivery, instructional personnel, student services, attendance/progress controls, outcomes, and continuous improvement. Current ACCET materials also distinguish and regulate distance-learning delivery methods and supervised practical experience.

Strengths:
- Clear instructional purpose and scope.
- Applied role-identification scenario.
- Explicit completion outcomes (students should be able to identify major parties, distinguish DOT/FMCSA, distinguish USDOT/authority, etc.).
- Instructor answer guide.
- Fact/source dependency controls.
- Defined program-scope language avoiding overgeneralization.

Accreditation status: STRONG CONTENT FOUNDATION / DOCUMENTATION GAPS.

### Module 02 — Dispatcher Role & Legal Boundaries
Strengths:
- High-stakes legal topic is expressly scoped as educational information, not individualized legal advice.
- Statute, regulation, and guidance are separately classified.

- Module summary states measurable competencies.
- Dependency map and instructor-safety notes are present.
- Current and potentially unstable legal points are flagged for verification.

Accreditation status: STRONG CONTENT FOUNDATION / REQUIRES FORMAL OUTCOME-TO-ASSESSMENT MAPPING.

Strengths:

## Gaps That Must Be Added Across Modules 01–06

### 1. Formal Learning-Outcome Mapping
Each module needs numbered module learning outcomes (MLOs). Every lesson, scenario, knowledge-check question, worksheet, simulation, and capstone element must map to one or more MLOs. MLOs must roll up to numbered program learning outcomes (PLOs).

Required artifact: `PLO-MLO-ASSESSMENT-MATRIX.md`.

### 2. Published Mastery and Grading Rules
The existing knowledge checks are good, but accreditation readiness requires a consistent policy defining:
- passing score;
- attempts allowed;
- remediation requirements;
- treatment of scenario/written-response items;
- progression rules;
- re-evaluation after remediation;
- capstone/final-exam thresholds.

Required artifact: `ACADEMIC-PROGRESS-AND-MASTERY-POLICY.md`.

### 3. Workload / Clock-Hour / Contact-Hour Documentation
Each module needs an evidence-based estimate of student effort, including reading, video/instruction if added, guided practice, independent practice, assessments, simulations, and instructor-supported activity. Any clock-hour/contact-hour representation must follow the applicable regulator/accreditor definition rather than being reverse-engineered for marketing.

Required artifact: `MODULE-WORKLOAD-AND-HOUR-MATRIX.md`.

### 4. Distance-Education Interaction Design
The modules currently function primarily as strong self-paced instructional content. The institution will need documented mechanisms for meaningful instructional interaction/support appropriate to the delivery model, including response standards, academic help, feedback, and escalation.

Required artifact: `DISTANCE-EDUCATION-INTERACTION-PLAN.md`.

### 5. Accessibility and Technology Requirements
Student-facing materials need accessibility standards and testing (document structure, keyboard access, captions/transcripts for media, contrast/readability, screen-reader considerations, mobile compatibility where claimed, and accessible assessment design).

Required artifact: `ACCESSIBILITY-AND-TECHNOLOGY-STANDARD.md`.

### 6. Student Achievement Evidence
The course currently contains assessments but no operating-history data. Accreditation requires institutional evidence showing whether students actually achieve the stated outcomes. Track:
- enrollment;
- completion;
- withdrawal;
- assessment mastery;
- capstone performance;
- satisfaction;
- complaints;
- job/business outcome measures if represented in marketing.

Required artifact: `STUDENT-OUTCOMES-DATA-DICTIONARY.md` and controlled reports after launch.

### 7. Faculty/Instructor Qualification and Oversight
Answer guides exist, but the academy still needs role requirements for instructors/reviewers, minimum subject-matter qualifications, onboarding, evaluation, supervision, and continuing development.

Required artifact: `FACULTY-QUALIFICATIONS-AND-EVALUATION-STANDARD.md`.

### 8. Source/Version Governance
Modules 02, 04, 05, and 06 already flag unstable regulations/platforms. Accreditation readiness requires a documented cadence, accountable owner, authoritative source record, revision date, change decision, and proof that outdated material was replaced before students received it.

Required artifact: `CURRICULUM-CURRENCY-AND-VERSION-CONTROL.md`.

### 9. Student-Facing Policies and Support
The module content alone cannot satisfy institutional accreditation. The academy will also need admissions/enrollment standards, cancellation/refund rules, attendance/progress rules as applicable, complaint/grievance process, academic integrity, identity verification where required, privacy, student records, disability/accessibility support, technical support, and disclosures.

Required location: Accreditation Readiness institutional-policy set, separate from student manuals.

### 10. Continuous Improvement Loop
Assessment results, complaints, student feedback, instructor observations, regulatory changes, and employer/carrier feedback should feed a documented curriculum-review process.

Required artifact: `CURRICULUM-REVIEW-AND-CONTINUOUS-IMPROVEMENT.md`.

- Module 02: 82% accreditation-ready at the course-content level.
- Module 03: 80% accreditation-ready at the course-content level.
- Module 04: 82% accreditation-ready at the course-content level, subject to current-date reverification.
- Module 05: 84% accreditation-ready at the course-content level.
- Module 06: 85% accreditation-ready at the course-content level.

Overall Modules 01–06 course-content readiness: approximately 81%.

This does NOT mean the institution is 81% ready for accreditation. Institutional readiness is much lower because operating history, state authorization, student outcomes, financial responsibility, faculty systems, student services, policies, technology controls, and other institutional evidence must exist and operate over time.

## Required Design Rule for Modules 07–15
Do not build Modules 07–15 using the old content-only template. From Module 07 forward, every module should be created with accreditation metadata from the beginning:
1. numbered MLOs;
2. PLO mapping;
3. estimated workload;
4. instructional activities;
5. graded assessment map;
6. mastery criteria;
7. remediation path;
8. source/provenance record;
9. version/current-date fields;
10. accessibility checklist;
11. instructor-support expectations;
12. evidence fields for later student-outcome reporting.

After Modules 07–15 use this structure, Modules 01–06 should be retrofitted to the same standard without weakening or unnecessarily rewriting their existing instructional content.
