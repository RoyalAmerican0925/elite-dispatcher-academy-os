## Carrier / Motor Carrier
**Plain-English meaning:** The transportation business that provides the motor-vehicle transportation of freight.  
**Industry use:** Commonly shortened to “carrier.”  
**Dispatcher significance:** A dispatcher commonly coordinates freight activity on the carrier side and must not confuse the carrier with the broker or shipper.  
**Common misuse:** Treating every transportation intermediary as a carrier.  
**Related terms:** driver, broker, shipper, operating authority.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Formal regulatory/legal wording must remain synchronized with the Academy source register.  
**Date-sensitive:** No for the basic concept; legal wording requires source control.

## Shipper
**Plain-English meaning:** The party that tenders or sends freight from the origin for transportation.  
**Dispatcher significance:** The shipper role is defined by the shipment function, not automatically by ownership of the goods.  
**Common misuse:** Saying the shipper always owns the freight.  
**Related terms:** origin, consignee/receiver, broker, carrier.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Consignee / Receiver
**Plain-English meaning:** The party or location receiving the freight at destination.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Delivery requirements, appointments, facility instructions, and proof-of-delivery activity may involve the consignee/receiver.  
**Related terms:** destination, shipper, POD.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Broker
**Plain-English meaning:** An intermediary that arranges transportation by an authorized motor carrier rather than physically moving the freight as the motor carrier in that transaction.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Dispatchers must understand who is offering/arranging the load, whose authority is involved, and the legal boundary between carrier-side dispatch activity and brokerage.  
**Common misuse:** Calling anyone who finds a load a broker, or assuming a broker is the motor carrier.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; legal-boundary depth in Module 02.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Formal definition and boundary claims require current primary-source verification.

## Deadhead
**Plain-English meaning:** Truck miles traveled without the revenue load aboard, commonly including positioning miles to reach pickup.  
**Industry use:** “What’s the deadhead to pickup?”  
**Dispatcher significance:** Deadhead affects time, fuel, positioning, total trip miles, and load economics.  
**Common misuse:** Comparing loads only by loaded miles while ignoring miles required to reach pickup.  
**Related terms:** loaded miles, total trip miles, RPM, positioning.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; advanced economic application in Module 07.

## Loaded Miles
**Plain-English meaning:** Miles traveled while the load is on the truck.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Useful operating measure but incomplete by itself for profitability analysis.  
**Common misuse:** Treating loaded miles as total miles required to execute the load.  
**Related terms:** deadhead, total trip miles, RPM.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; expanded Module 07.

## Appointment
**Plain-English meaning:** A scheduled pickup or delivery time/window established for a facility interaction.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Timing affects planning, communication, and feasibility.  
**Common misuse:** Treating every listed facility time as equivalent to FCFS access.  
**Related terms:** pickup, delivery, FCFS, live load/unload.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## First Come, First Served (FCFS)
**Plain-English meaning:** A facility process in which trucks are generally handled in arrival order rather than by a specific appointment slot, subject to the facility’s actual rules.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Changes how arrival planning and waiting risk are interpreted.  
**Common misuse:** Assuming FCFS means a truck may arrive at any hour or that service will be immediate.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Live Load / Live Unload
**Plain-English meaning:** Loading or unloading while the driver and equipment remain at the facility for the process.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Facility time can affect scheduling and downstream planning.  
**Related terms:** detention, drop-and-hook.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Drop-and-Hook
**Plain-English meaning:** An operation in which a driver drops one trailer and hooks to another rather than waiting for the same trailer to be live loaded or unloaded.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Changes equipment, timing, trailer-control, and facility planning considerations.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Bill of Lading (BOL)
**Plain-English meaning:** A core shipment document associated with the freight and transportation movement.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Students must learn what information it can carry, when it appears in the workflow, and why it should not be confused with a rate confirmation or POD.  
**Common misuse:** Treating every load-related document as interchangeable.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; deeper document handling follows curriculum scope.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Legal characterizations require source control.

## Proof of Delivery (POD)
**Plain-English meaning:** Delivery evidence/documentation showing that freight was delivered, commonly involving a signed or otherwise completed delivery record.  
**Dispatcher significance:** It supports post-delivery documentation and payment workflow.  
**Common misuse:** Treating POD, BOL, invoice, and rate confirmation as the same document.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Rate Confirmation
**Plain-English meaning:** A document used in broker/carrier freight transactions to record agreed load and rate information.  
**Dispatcher significance:** It is an important information source for the load file and should be checked for discrepancies and instructions.  
**Common misuse:** Teaching it categorically as the sole or automatically controlling contract in every transaction.  
**Introduced:** foundational recognition Module 01; deeper sourcing/booking application later.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Contractual claims require careful context and source control.

## Rate Per Mile (RPM)
**Plain-English meaning:** A rate expressed per mile. The denominator matters: loaded-mile RPM and effective/total-mile RPM are not automatically the same.  
**Dispatcher significance:** A high displayed RPM can be misleading if deadhead and total trip requirements are ignored.  
**Related terms:** loaded miles, deadhead, total trip miles, contribution margin.  
**Introduced:** foundational recognition before advanced application; expanded Module 07.

## Total Trip Miles
**Plain-English meaning:** The miles required for the relevant trip analysis, including applicable positioning/deadhead plus loaded movement according to the calculation being performed.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Supports more realistic load comparison than loaded miles alone.  
**Introduced:** Module 07 depth, with foundational deadhead recognition earlier.

## Contribution Margin
**Plain-English meaning:** For Academy load-analysis purposes, the amount remaining from load revenue after the defined trip-level variable costs included in the exercise.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Helps compare what a load contributes after relevant trip costs rather than confusing gross revenue with profit.  
**Common misuse:** Calling gross load revenue “profit.”  
**Introduced:** Module 07.  
**Source/regulatory note:** The Academy must state exactly which costs are included in each exercise.
