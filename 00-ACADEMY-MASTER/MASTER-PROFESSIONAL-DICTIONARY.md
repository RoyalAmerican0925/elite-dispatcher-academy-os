## FOUNDATIONAL ROLES AND FREIGHT FLOW

## Carrier / Motor Carrier
**Plain-English meaning:** The transportation business that provides the motor-vehicle transportation of freight.  
**Industry use:** Commonly shortened to “carrier.”  
**Dispatcher significance:** A dispatcher commonly coordinates freight activity on the carrier side and must not confuse the carrier with the broker or shipper.  
**Common misuse:** Treating every transportation intermediary as a carrier.  
**Related terms:** driver, broker, shipper, operating authority.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Formal regulatory/legal wording must remain synchronized with the Academy source register.  
**Date-sensitive:** No for the basic concept; legal wording requires source control.

## Shipper
**Plain-English meaning:** The party that tenders or sends freight from the origin for transportation.  
**Dispatcher significance:** The shipper role is defined by the shipment function, not automatically by ownership of the goods.  
**Common misuse:** Saying the shipper always owns the freight.  
**Related terms:** origin, consignee/receiver, broker, carrier.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Consignee / Receiver
**Plain-English meaning:** The party or location receiving the freight at destination.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Delivery requirements, appointments, facility instructions, and proof-of-delivery activity may involve the consignee/receiver.  
**Related terms:** destination, shipper, POD.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Broker
**Plain-English meaning:** An intermediary that arranges transportation by an authorized motor carrier rather than physically moving the freight as the motor carrier in that transaction.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Dispatchers must understand who is offering/arranging the load, whose authority is involved, and the legal boundary between carrier-side dispatch activity and brokerage.  
**Common misuse:** Calling anyone who finds a load a broker, or assuming a broker is the motor carrier.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; legal-boundary depth in Module 02.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Formal definition and boundary claims require current primary-source verification.

## Carrier-Side Dispatcher
**Plain-English meaning:** For Academy purposes, a person performing dispatch/coordination functions on behalf of a motor carrier within the carrier-side role and applicable legal/contractual boundaries.  
**Dispatcher significance:** The Academy trains carrier-side dispatch work; students must know when a requested action belongs to a broker, driver, carrier owner/manager, compliance function or other authorized party.  
**Common misuse:** Assuming a dispatcher automatically has authority to make every carrier, driver, compliance, contract or brokerage decision.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; legal-boundary depth in Module 02.

## Origin / Pickup
**Plain-English meaning:** The place/facility where the freight movement begins for the load segment being dispatched and where pickup activity occurs.  
**Dispatcher significance:** City-level origin information may be insufficient for execution; facility, timing, reference and instruction details can matter.  
**Common misuse:** Treating a city pair as a complete freight file.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Destination / Delivery
**Plain-English meaning:** The place/facility where the dispatched freight movement is scheduled to deliver.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Delivery appointment, facility requirements, references and receiving status affect execution and closeout.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Lane
**Plain-English meaning:** A described freight movement between geographic markets or origin/destination areas.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Used in sourcing, market comparison, positioning and operational planning.  
**Common misuse:** Treating a broad lane label as though it supplies the exact pickup/delivery facilities or conditions.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; market application Module 05.

## Capacity
**Plain-English meaning:** Available transportation capability; context may refer to trucks, trailers, drivers, equipment types or broader market supply.  
**Dispatcher significance:** The dispatcher must know what type of capacity is actually being discussed.  
**Common misuse:** Assuming the word always means the same resource.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## FACILITY, EQUIPMENT AND MOVEMENT LANGUAGE

## Appointment
**Plain-English meaning:** A scheduled pickup or delivery time/window established for a facility interaction.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Timing affects planning, communication, and feasibility.  
**Common misuse:** Treating every listed facility time as equivalent to FCFS access.  
**Related terms:** pickup, delivery, FCFS, live load/unload.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; trip-planning application Module 10.

## First Come, First Served (FCFS)
**Plain-English meaning:** A facility process in which trucks are generally handled in arrival order rather than by a specific appointment slot, subject to the facility’s actual rules.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Changes how arrival planning and waiting risk are interpreted.  
**Common misuse:** Assuming FCFS means a truck may arrive at any hour or that service will be immediate.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Live Load / Live Unload
**Plain-English meaning:** Loading or unloading while the driver and equipment remain at the facility for the process.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Facility time can affect scheduling and downstream planning.  
**Related terms:** detention, drop-and-hook.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Drop-and-Hook
**Plain-English meaning:** An operation in which a driver drops one trailer and hooks to another rather than waiting for the same trailer to be live loaded or unloaded.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Changes equipment, timing, trailer-control, and facility planning considerations.  
**Introduced:** Module 01.

## Deadhead
**Plain-English meaning:** Truck miles traveled without the revenue load aboard, commonly including positioning miles to reach pickup.  
**Industry use:** “What’s the deadhead to pickup?”  
**Dispatcher significance:** Deadhead affects time, fuel, positioning, total trip miles, and load economics.  
**Common misuse:** Comparing loads only by loaded miles while ignoring miles required to reach pickup.  
**Related terms:** loaded miles, total trip miles, RPM, positioning.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; advanced economic application in Module 07.

## Loaded Miles
**Plain-English meaning:** Miles traveled while the load is on the truck.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Useful operating measure but incomplete by itself for profitability analysis.  
**Common misuse:** Treating loaded miles as total miles required to execute the load.  
**Related terms:** deadhead, total trip miles, RPM.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; expanded Module 07.

## Total Trip Miles
**Plain-English meaning:** The miles required for the relevant trip analysis, including applicable positioning/deadhead plus loaded movement according to the calculation being performed.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Supports more realistic load comparison than loaded miles alone.  
**Introduced:** Module 07 depth, with foundational deadhead recognition earlier.

## DOCUMENT AND COMMERCIAL LANGUAGE

## Bill of Lading (BOL)
**Plain-English meaning:** A core shipment document associated with the freight and transportation movement.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Students must learn what information it can carry, when it appears in the workflow, and why it should not be confused with a rate confirmation or POD.  
**Common misuse:** Treating every load-related document as interchangeable.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; deeper document handling follows curriculum scope.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Legal characterizations require source control.

## Proof of Delivery (POD)
**Plain-English meaning:** Delivery evidence/documentation showing that freight was delivered, commonly involving a signed or otherwise completed delivery record.  
**Dispatcher significance:** It supports post-delivery documentation and payment workflow.  
**Common misuse:** Treating POD, BOL, invoice, and rate confirmation as the same document.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; closeout depth Module 14.

## Rate Confirmation
**Plain-English meaning:** A document used in broker/carrier freight transactions to record agreed load and rate information.  
**Dispatcher significance:** It is an important information source for the load file and should be checked for discrepancies and instructions.  
**Common misuse:** Teaching it categorically as the sole or automatically controlling contract in every transaction.  
**Introduced:** foundational recognition Module 01; sourcing/screening context Module 05; booking/document depth Module 09.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Contractual claims require careful context and source control.

## Invoice
**Plain-English meaning:** A billing document requesting payment for services under the applicable commercial arrangement.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Delivery does not by itself mean the billing/payment workflow is complete; required documents and billing conditions may remain.  
**Common misuse:** Treating an invoice as the same thing as POD or rate confirmation.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; closeout depth Module 14.

## Rate Per Mile (RPM)
**Plain-English meaning:** A rate expressed per mile. The denominator matters: loaded-mile RPM and effective/total-mile RPM are not automatically the same.  
**Dispatcher significance:** A high displayed RPM can be misleading if deadhead and total trip requirements are ignored.  
**Related terms:** loaded miles, deadhead, total trip miles, contribution margin.  
**Introduced:** foundational recognition before advanced application; expanded Module 07.

## Contribution Margin
**Plain-English meaning:** For Academy load-analysis purposes, the amount remaining from load revenue after the defined trip-level variable costs included in the exercise.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Helps compare what a load contributes after relevant trip costs rather than confusing gross revenue with profit.  
**Common misuse:** Calling gross load revenue “profit.”  
**Introduced:** Module 07.  
**Source/regulatory note:** The Academy must state exactly which costs are included in each exercise.

## Counteroffer
**Plain-English meaning:** A proposed alternative to an offered rate or other negotiable term.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Module 08 requires the student to connect a counteroffer to the carrier's verified operating/economic position and communicate it professionally.  
**Common misuse:** Treating an unsupported demand or fabricated competing offer as professional negotiation.  
**Introduced:** Module 08 planned scope.

## Booking
**Plain-English meaning:** The workflow by which a freight opportunity is accepted/confirmed for the carrier under the applicable agreement and operating process.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Booking requires confirmation of the actual load, terms, parties, instructions and documentation rather than merely saying “we'll take it.”  
**Common misuse:** Treating a verbal expression of interest as proof that every booking term/document is complete.  
**Introduced:** Module 09 planned scope.

## VERIFICATION, RISK AND DECISION LANGUAGE

## Known / Unknown / Verify
**Plain-English meaning:** A three-part discipline for separating information the file states, information that is absent, and information that exists but requires confirmation/reconciliation before reliance.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Prevents beginners from filling operational gaps with assumptions.  
**Common misuse:** Treating any populated field as automatically verified.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; transferred throughout the Academy.

## Hold
**Plain-English meaning:** Pause the next operational action because a material issue remains unresolved.  
**Dispatcher significance:** A hold is a control action, not automatically an accusation of wrongdoing.  
**Introduced:** foundational decision discipline; specialized use Module 06 and later workflows.

## Escalate / Hand Off
**Plain-English meaning:** Route an issue to the person, workflow or competency owner with the authority/expertise to resolve it.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Professional dispatching includes recognizing limits rather than guessing.  
**Common misuse:** Treating escalation as failure or trying to solve an out-of-scope issue without authority.  
**Introduced:** Module 01; expanded throughout the program.

## Entity Verification
**Plain-English meaning:** Confirming that the business/entity being represented is the entity it claims to be using appropriate evidence/sources.  
**Dispatcher significance:** A legitimate entity record does not by itself prove the contacting individual is authorized.  
**Related terms:** contact verification, authority, impersonation.  
**Introduced:** Module 06 depth.

## Contact Verification
**Plain-English meaning:** Independently confirming that the person/channel contacting the carrier is legitimately associated with the represented entity/transaction.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Helps detect impersonation even where the underlying company is real.  
**Common misuse:** Using the suspicious message's own contact information as the sole method to verify itself.  
**Introduced:** Module 06 depth.

## Red Flag
**Plain-English meaning:** A fact, mismatch or pattern that warrants additional attention or verification.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Red flags support a decision to verify/hold/escalate; they do not automatically prove fraud.  
**Common misuse:** Calling every unusual detail “confirmed fraud.”  
**Introduced:** Module 06.

## TRIP, STATUS AND EXCEPTION LANGUAGE — VERIFIED PROGRAM TARGETS

The following entries are architecture-level targets because Modules 08–15 are planned in the authoritative module map but not yet authored. Exact definitions, legal notes and examples must be finalized during production.

## ETA — Estimated Time of Arrival
**Plain-English meaning:** A current estimate of when a truck is expected to reach a location.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Must be communicated as an estimate based on current information, not a guarantee.  
**Introduced:** Module 10 planned scope; active monitoring Module 12.

## Check Call / Status Update
**Plain-English meaning:** A communication used to obtain or report current load/driver/trip status under the carrier's operating process.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Supports continuity, ETA management, exception detection and documented communication.  
**Introduced:** Module 12 planned scope.

## Detention
**Plain-English meaning:** In general industry usage, time a truck/driver is held at a facility beyond the relevant expected/free-time arrangement; exact eligibility, free time, documentation and payment depend on the applicable agreement/policy.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Requires accurate timestamps, communication and agreement-specific verification.  
**Common misuse:** Teaching a universal detention amount or universal free-time rule when none has been established.  
**Introduced:** Module 13 planned scope.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Agreement-specific unless a governing rule actually applies.

## TONU — Truck Ordered Not Used
**Plain-English meaning:** Industry term commonly used for a truck dispatched/ordered for a load that is cancelled or unavailable under circumstances where compensation may be requested/owed under the applicable agreement.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Requires factual documentation and agreement-specific handling.  
**Common misuse:** Assuming a universal TONU amount or automatic entitlement.  
**Introduced:** Module 13 planned scope.

## Exception
**Plain-English meaning:** A material deviation/problem requiring attention outside the expected load plan.  
**Dispatcher significance:** The dispatcher must identify, document, communicate and route the exception to the correct response path.  
**Introduced:** Module 13 planned scope; capstone transfer Module 15.

## Notice of Assignment (NOA)
**Plain-English meaning:** A notice associated with assignment of receivables/payment rights, commonly encountered in factoring contexts.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Payment-routing instructions must be handled through the applicable verified process rather than changed from an unverified message.  
**Introduced:** onboarding/payment awareness earlier; closeout depth Module 14.  
**Source/regulatory note:** Legal/payment effects depend on actual documents and applicable law/agreements.

## Load Closeout
**Plain-English meaning:** The post-delivery workflow that brings required operational documents/status and billing handoff to the carrier's defined completion state.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Physical delivery, document completion and payment completion are separate states.  
**Introduced:** Module 14 planned scope.

## Shift Handoff
**Plain-English meaning:** A structured transfer of active load/status/exception responsibilities and unresolved items from one responsible person/period to another.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Prevents operational information from disappearing between people or work periods.  
**Introduced:** Module 15 business-operations/capstone scope.

## SOP — Standard Operating Procedure
**Plain-English meaning:** A documented repeatable procedure for performing an operating task or workflow.  
**Dispatcher significance:** Supports consistent service, training, handoff, quality control and escalation.  
**Common misuse:** Creating a generic checklist that does not identify trigger, owner, steps, evidence and exception path.  
**Introduced:** Module 15 business-operations scope.
