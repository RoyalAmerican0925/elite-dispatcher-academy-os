# Module 15 — Architecture Resolution

**Status:** PROGRAM SCOPE RESOLVED / PRODUCTION NOT YET AUTHORIZED

## Controlling decision
Module 15 will intentionally combine the two repository control-map requirements rather than discard either one.

The authoritative near-term map requires **Dispatcher Business Operations, Client Service, Systems, and Capstone Practice**. The project-control map requires **Capstone and Integrated Dispatch Operations** using the full Modules 01–14 workflow. These requirements are complementary when the module is designed as a two-layer final module with one integrated performance standard.

## Module 15 working title
**Dispatcher Business Operations, Client Service, Systems & Integrated Capstone**

The title may receive copy-editing before publication, but production may not silently remove either the business-operations layer or the integrated dispatch capstone.

## Employment outcome
By the end of Module 15, the student must be able to organize a carrier-side dispatch operation, communicate professionally with a client/carrier, maintain operational records and service standards, and execute an integrated dispatcher shift that requires the student to apply prior Academy competencies under realistic time, information, documentation and exception pressure.

## Layer A — Dispatcher business operations and client service
This layer prepares the student to operate the dispatch function as a repeatable professional service rather than a collection of improvised load-by-load actions.

Required competency families:
- service scope and client expectations;
- professional onboarding/handoff boundaries without duplicating Module 04;
- carrier/client communication standards;
- workflow ownership and escalation paths;
- recordkeeping and file organization;
- dispatch desk SOP use;
- task/status tracking;
- service-quality measures and follow-up;
- confidentiality and appropriate information handling;
- business continuity and handoff discipline;
- basic client-service problem resolution;
- system-of-record discipline;
- operational reporting;
- business/dispatch separation of duties where applicable;
- professional language across the full freight lifecycle.

This is not a generic entrepreneurship module. Content must remain tied to the occupational function of providing and managing dispatcher services.

## Layer B — Integrated dispatch capstone
The capstone is the Academy's final transfer-of-learning environment. It must require the student to combine prior competencies rather than answer isolated questions by module.

The final scenario set must be derived from the completed Modules 01–14, but the architecture requires at minimum:
- carrier/driver/equipment context;
- freight opportunity intake;
- party/role identification;
- sourcing and screening;
- entity/contact/load verification;
- profitability analysis;
- negotiation;
- booking/document review;
- trip planning;
- HOS/ELD feasibility awareness;
- driver communication and load monitoring;
- changing information and progressive events;
- delay/exception/escalation decisions;
- POD/document closeout;
- billing/payment/factoring awareness;
- professional client communication;
- recordkeeping and shift handoff.

## Capstone operating design
The capstone must not be a long multiple-choice exam disguised as a simulation. It should function as a realistic dispatcher shift with incomplete information, documents, messages, calls/notes, competing priorities, progressive events and consequences.

Required decision types include:
1. PROCEED — enough verified information and authority exist to continue.
2. VERIFY — information exists but requires confirmation.
3. HOLD — a material unresolved issue prevents the next action.
4. DECLINE / DO NOT BOOK — verified facts make the transaction unsuitable or impermissible under the applicable workflow/policy.
5. ESCALATE / HAND OFF — the issue exceeds the student's role, authority or competency.
6. DOCUMENT / COMMUNICATE — the operational decision is incomplete until the correct party/file is updated.

## Business-operations practical evidence
Before or within the capstone, the student should produce usable artifacts such as:
- dispatcher desk workflow/SOP;
- client/carrier communication standard;
- shift/load status tracker;
- escalation matrix;
- file/document organization standard;
- client-service issue log or resolution record;
- shift handoff report;
- basic operating-performance report.

Artifacts should reuse Academy tools where appropriate rather than create duplicate forms solely for Module 15.

## Assessment architecture
Module 15 requires distinct evidence layers:
- knowledge/application check for business-operations concepts;
- business-operations practical;
- integrated capstone simulation;
- final competency record mapped to Modules 01–15;
- remediation path for failed critical competencies;
- completion determination that does not rely only on an averaged score.

Critical failures in the final capstone should include unsafe guessing, knowingly bypassing required verification, material role/legal-boundary errors, materially false operational communication, improper handling of a critical document/payment change, or proceeding when a prior-module stop condition requires hold/escalation. Exact critical items are defined only after Modules 08–14 are built and validated.

## Professional-language requirement
The final module is the transfer test for Academy language fluency. Students must use correct role, equipment, document, rate, verification, booking, trip, HOS, exception, closeout and client-service terminology in context. A technically correct decision communicated with materially misleading terminology is not full mastery.

## Boundary controls
Module 15 integrates; it does not reteach every earlier module. If a student cannot perform an underlying skill, remediation routes back to the competency's owning module.

Module 15 must not:
- replace current legal/regulatory verification with a summary;
- teach advanced accounting, tax or legal practice outside dispatcher occupational scope;
- turn into generic motivational business content;
- repeat Modules 01–14 merely to increase page count;
- use capstone performance to hide an unremediated critical prerequisite failure.

## Completion architecture
A student completes the Academy only when required knowledge, practical and simulation evidence demonstrates the defined competencies and all critical-failure rules are satisfied. Certificate eligibility is a release-system decision tied to the final assessment/completion policy, not merely attendance or opening every lesson.

## Production dependency
Do not author the final Module 15 capstone scenario package until Modules 08–14 have approved competency maps. The business-operations layer can be designed earlier, but final capstone dependencies and scoring must be generated from the verified full-program competency map.

## Resolution
The earlier scope conflict is **RESOLVED AT ARCHITECTURE LEVEL**:

**Module 15 = business operations/client service/systems + integrated dispatcher capstone.**

This resolution does not mark Module 15 built, commercially complete, or `PUBLICATION_READY`.
