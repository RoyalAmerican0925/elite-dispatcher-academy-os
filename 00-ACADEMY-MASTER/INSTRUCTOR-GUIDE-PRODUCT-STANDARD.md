# Elite Dispatcher Academy — Instructor Guide Product Standard

**Status:** ACADEMY-WIDE PRODUCT CONTROL

An answer key is not an instructor guide. A scoring guide is not an instructor guide. A source register is not an instructor guide. The commercial Academy requires an instructor-facing layer that explains how to teach, observe, diagnose and remediate the competency.

## 1. Purpose
Each module instructor guide must help a qualified instructor/facilitator deliver the module consistently without needing private production notes, chat history or unstated author knowledge.

The guide must answer:
1. What must the student be able to do?
2. What prerequisite knowledge is assumed?
3. Which concepts require emphasis or demonstration?
4. What beginner errors should the instructor expect?
5. Which claims are source-sensitive or date-sensitive?
6. How should tools and activities be facilitated?
7. What counts as acceptable evidence?
8. What is a critical failure?
9. How should weak performance be diagnosed and remediated?
10. What must be rechecked before publication/delivery?

## 2. Required sections per module

### A. Module control header
- module number/title;
- version/revision date;
- instructional status;
- source-sensitive status;
- prerequisites/imports;
- exports to later modules;
- related simulation;
- associated manual/tool/assessment paths.

### B. Employment outcome and competency map
List the observable occupational outcome and competency IDs. Avoid vague objectives such as “understand trucking.” Use observable actions such as identify, verify, calculate, compare, document, communicate, sequence, escalate, reject or hand off.

### C. Instructor preparation
Before teaching, identify:
- files to review;
- current sources that require re-verification;
- fictional artifacts to prepare;
- tools/worksheets students need;
- runtime/simulation requirements;
- common terminology that must remain consistent;
- any material that should not be shown to students because it contains keys/scoring logic.

### D. Lesson-by-lesson teaching notes
For every major manual section:
- instructional purpose;
- essential point;
- workplace application;
- misconception to watch for;
- demonstration/example suggestion;
- embedded practice connection;
- professional-language expectation;
- transition to the next concept.

The guide should not reproduce the entire student manual. It adds delivery intelligence.

### E. Professional-language coaching
Identify required terms and what correct workplace use sounds like. Include:
- correct term;
- common misuse;
- material consequence of misuse;
- acceptable student phrasing;
- language that overstates evidence or legal conclusions;
- later-module transfer.

### F. Activity facilitation
For each worksheet, drill, scenario or lab:
- purpose;
- setup;
- expected reasoning path;
- acceptable alternative reasoning where applicable;
- predictable errors;
- debrief questions;
- what evidence the instructor should observe;
- whether the activity is formative or scored.

### G. Assessment administration
The instructor guide must distinguish:
- knowledge check;
- module exam;
- practical assessment;
- simulation;
- remediation/reassessment.

For each scored component, state the governing scoring source rather than creating a conflicting local rule.

### H. Critical-failure controls
Critical failures must be explicit, occupationally justified and synchronized with the actual scoring policy. Examples may include proceeding through a known stop condition, unsafe/legal-boundary errors, unsupported accusations, falsifying a record, or materially incorrect handling of a payment/document instruction where the module owns that competency.

A critical failure is not simply a difficult question.

### I. Diagnostic remediation
Remediation must identify the failed mental model, not merely tell the student the right answer.

Recommended diagnostic structure:
- observed error;
- likely misconception;
- owning competency;
- manual section/tool to revisit;
- targeted practice;
- evidence required on reassessment.

### J. Source/currentness notes
For regulated, legal, state-specific, platform-specific or date-sensitive instruction:
- identify the controlling source register;
- flag what must be rechecked;
- prohibit the instructor from presenting stale screenshots, rates, policies or system names as permanent facts;
- distinguish federal law/regulation from carrier policy, platform rule, contract term and Academy teaching model.

### K. Simulation facilitation
For modules with a simulation:
- learning purpose;
- prerequisite competencies;
- runtime setup;
- whether attempts are independent or coached;
- what first-attempt evidence is preserved;
- critical decisions;
- remediation behavior;
- instructor debrief;
- what completion status does and does not mean.

`BUILD COMPLETE — RELEASE QA DEFERRED` is a software/content build state, not student mastery and not Academy publication readiness.

### L. Module closeout
Instructor confirms:
- required competencies assessed;
- critical failures cleared;
- required practical/simulation evidence recorded;
- professional-language standard met;
- remediation completed where needed;
- student is ready for the next module under the program progression rule.

## 3. Separation of instructor-facing assets

### Instructor Guide
How to teach, observe, diagnose, facilitate and close the module.

### Answer Key
Correct answers/rationales for knowledge checks/exams and, where appropriate, model responses.

### Practical Scoring Guide / Rubric
Observable criteria, point/level logic, critical failures and performance evidence for the practical.

### Simulation Scoring/Competency Record
Decision-level evidence, critical decisions, attempt state and competency result for the simulation.

### Source Register
Evidence supporting regulated/date-sensitive claims.

These may cross-reference one another but must not contradict one another.

## 4. Premium instructor-value test
An instructor guide fails if it merely says “review the lesson, give the quiz, discuss answers.” It must contain enough occupational and pedagogical intelligence to produce consistent delivery and meaningful remediation.

Ask:
- Could a competent new instructor tell why each activity exists?
- Could the instructor recognize a plausible but dangerous beginner misconception?
- Could the instructor distinguish a knowledge gap from a judgment gap?
- Could the instructor remediate without exposing the entire answer set?
- Could two instructors apply substantially the same completion standard?

If not, the guide is incomplete.

## 5. Contamination rule
Instructor materials must not contain production chat, AI instructions, internal email, unfinished TODO/TBD language, unsupported claims, private credentials or accidental student-answer exposure in student-facing paths.

## 6. Academy release requirement
Before commercial release, every module must either have a complete instructor guide conforming to this standard or a documented approved equivalent that provides the same functions. Existing files titled `Instructor-Guides/Module-XX.md` must be audited against this standard; filename presence is not evidence of product completeness.
