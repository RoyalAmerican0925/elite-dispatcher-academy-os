# Academy Master Dictionary — Student Fluency Development

**Status:** DRAFT_COMPLETE — QA required before PUBLICATION_READY
**Section:** Fourth and final top-level section of the Academy Master Dictionary framework.
**Purpose:** Maps the terminology built across the 13 Master Dictionary branches, the Acronym Dictionary, and the Professional Communication Guide back to where students actually encounter and practice it — by module, by scenario, by simulation — and defines what "fluency" actually means and how it's assessed. This is the piece that turns a reference dictionary into an instructional tool.

## Section 2: Terms Practiced in Scenarios

Each module's Scenario Lab exercises its vocabulary through applied fact patterns rather than definition recall. Representative mapping:

| Module Scenario Lab | Vocabulary exercised |
|---|---|
| Module 02 (12 scenarios, S-02-01–S-02-12) | Allocation of traffic, label vs. conduct, return/decline vs. reassignment |
| Module 03 (13 scenarios, S-03-01–S-03-13) | GOOD FIT/BAD FIT/NEEDS MORE INFORMATION/SPECIALIZED classification; H/N/X distinction; gross-vs-axle weight |
| Module 05 (sourcing scenarios) | FOUND vs. VERIFIED AVAILABLE; entity vs. contact verification; red flags |
| Module 06 (12 scenarios, S-06-01–S-06-12) | CLEAR/VERIFY/HOLD/ESCALATE/DO NOT USE/DO NOT BOOK risk classification |

The consistent pattern across every Scenario Lab: students apply a **classification framework** (a small, defined set of outcome labels) to a fact pattern, rather than recalling a definition in isolation. Fluency, in this academy's design, is demonstrated through correct classification under realistic conditions — not through reciting a glossary entry.

## Section 3: Terms Required in Simulations

### SIM-001 (Module 01 — Freight Movement Desk)
Requires fluent, applied use of: Shipper, Broker, Motor Carrier, Dispatcher, Driver, Receiver, Factoring Company (role identification); arranging vs. physically transporting (the core Module 01 distinction); USDOT number vs. operating authority; the four flow types (physical, communication/arrangement, document, payment/factoring).

### SIM-002 (Module 02 — Agency Boundary Desk)
Requires fluent, applied use of: Represented carrier, allocation of traffic, significant brokerage risk vs. carrier-side agency, label vs. conduct, return/decline vs. reassignment, STOP/IDENTIFY/CHECK/ESCALATE.

**Pattern:** Both simulations require students to select and apply the correct classification term to a *specific decision*, under time/decision pressure, with progressive information reveal — a materially higher fluency bar than a knowledge check's multiple-choice recall. This is intentional: SIM-001 and SIM-002 exist specifically to test whether vocabulary has become usable judgment, not just memorized definition.

## Section 5: Professional-Language Competency Framework

A four-level model for assessing a student's actual fluency, as distinct from their raw knowledge-check score:

### Level 1 — Recognition
The student can define a term correctly when asked directly (knowledge-check level). They may still confuse adjacent terms under any pressure (e.g., broker vs. dispatcher, USDOT vs. MC number) and cannot yet apply the term to classify a novel fact pattern.

### Level 2 — Application
The student can correctly apply a term's classification framework to a presented scenario when the scenario clearly matches a pattern they've seen before (e.g., correctly classifies a straightforward Scenario-Lab-style fact pattern). Confusion still increases on ambiguous or combined fact patterns.

### Level 3 — Judgment
The student can correctly classify an unfamiliar, combined, or partially ambiguous fact pattern — the level SIM-001 and SIM-002's later stages are specifically designed to test (e.g., Stage 7's "Final Boundary Decision" in SIM-002). They correctly distinguish commonly-confused pairs (H vs. X, Loaded RPM vs. Effective RPM) without hesitation and recognize when information is genuinely missing rather than guessing.

### Level 4 — Fluency
The student uses correct, precise terminology unprompted in open-ended communication — written notes, verbal scripts, carrier/broker/driver-facing communication — without needing the term supplied by a question format. They can explain *why* a common misuse is wrong to someone else, not just avoid the misuse themselves. This is the level the Professional Communication Guide's example scripts are written to model.

### Assessment Mapping

| Assessment type | Competency level primarily tested |
|---|---|
| Knowledge Check (diagnostic) | Level 1–2 |
| Graded Exam | Level 2–3 |
| Practical Assessment | Level 2–3, with some Level 4 elements (written recommendation tasks) |
| Simulation (SIM-001, SIM-002) | Level 3, reaching toward Level 4 in later stages |
| Real-world professional communication (post-graduation) | Level 4 |

**Design principle:** No single assessment type should be expected to demonstrate Level 4 fluency on its own — that level is reached through the cumulative effect of repeated, varied application across knowledge checks, exams, practicals, and simulations, not from any one component in isolation.
