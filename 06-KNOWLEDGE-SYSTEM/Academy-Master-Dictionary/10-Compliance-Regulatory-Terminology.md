## Section 1: Federal Agencies

### DOT (U.S. Department of Transportation)
*(Established in Module 01.)* The broad federal department responsible for transportation policy across all modes — the umbrella agency, not the specific agency trucking professionals deal with most often day to day.

### FMCSA (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration)
*(Established in Module 01.)* The administration within DOT that specifically regulates commercial-motor-vehicle safety, carrier and driver registration, and related oversight — FMCSA is part of DOT, not a separate synonym for it.

### PHMSA (Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration)
**What it means:** The DOT agency responsible for hazardous materials transportation regulation, including shipper classification responsibility (see Module 03's hazmat content).
**What it does NOT mean:** The same agency as FMCSA — PHMSA and FMCSA are separate agencies within DOT with different regulatory focuses, though hazmat transportation involves both.
**Common misuse:** Attributing a hazmat classification rule to FMCSA when it actually originates from PHMSA's regulations, or vice versa.
**Operational example:** "Shipper hazmat-classification responsibility traces to PHMSA guidance, distinct from FMCSA's carrier-safety regulations."

## Section 2: Registration & Identification

### USDOT Number
*(Established in Module 01.)* An identifier used by FMCSA to identify and track a regulated entity — identifies who the company is, not what it's authorized to do. Distinct from operating authority.

### Operating Authority
*(Established in Module 01, expanded in Module 04.)* The separate federal registration authorizing a company to conduct specified regulated transportation operations — having a USDOT number does not automatically mean a company holds the specific operating authority its intended operation requires.

### MC Number
*(Established in Carrier Terminology and Module 01.)* A specific type of operating-authority docket number historically associated with for-hire property carrier authority — not the same thing as a USDOT number.

### Motus (USDOT Registration System)
*(Established in Module 01 and 04.)* FMCSA's current, centralized registration platform — **CURRENT-DATE MATERIAL.** Do not treat any specific status claim about Motus as permanently fixed; check Module 01's and Module 04's own verification dates and date-sensitive registers before relying on current-system claims.

### Legacy L&I (Licensing & Insurance)
*(Established in Module 01 and 04.)* FMCSA's former registration system — **CURRENT-DATE MATERIAL.** As of the verification date recorded in Module 04, a historical-record system not reflecting current Motus filings. Do not use as a source for current filing status without checking whether this status has since changed.

### SAFER (Safety and Fitness Electronic Records)
*(Established in Module 01, 04, and 05.)* A public FMCSA snapshot tool providing supplemental safety/entity information — not a complete substitute for current registration, authority, or insurance verification through the appropriate current source.

### New Entrant
**What it means:** FMCSA's designation for a motor carrier newly registered and operating under its own authority, subject to a specific monitoring period.
**What it does NOT mean:** The same thing as the informal industry term "green authority" (see Carrier Terminology) in every respect — New Entrant is a specific FMCSA program status; "green authority" is broader informal industry shorthand. Confirm current program specifics with an appropriate source rather than assuming.
**Common misuse:** Assuming New Entrant program requirements from memory rather than verifying current FMCSA guidance, given this is an area subject to change.
**Operational example:** "Confirm this carrier's New Entrant status and any applicable monitoring requirements through a current FMCSA source."

## Section 3: Financial Responsibility (Cross-Reference Only — No Figures Restated)

**IMPORTANT:** This section deliberately does not restate any dollar figures, percentages, or day-counts. Modules 02, 04, and 06 carry the current, sourced figures along with their own date-sensitive claim registers. Check those directly.

### BIPD (Bodily Injury / Property Damage) Insurance
*(Established in Module 04.)* Federally required public-liability insurance for for-hire motor carriers, with minimums varying by commodity and vehicle weight. See Module 04's Section 4 for the current figure and its verification date.

### BMC-84 / BMC-85
*(Established in Module 02 and 06; cross-referenced in Broker Terminology.)* FMCSA forms evidencing a broker's or freight forwarder's required financial security — BMC-84 for a surety bond, BMC-85 for a trust fund. Current dollar minimums, trustee-eligibility rules, and timeline figures (cure windows, replenishment periods, asset-liquidity standards) are all date-sensitive; see Module 02's and Module 06's Date-Sensitive Claim Registers directly rather than relying on any figure repeated elsewhere.

### Financial Responsibility
**What it means:** The general regulatory category covering both carrier insurance requirements (BIPD) and broker financial security (BMC-84/85) — the umbrella concept, not a single specific dollar figure.
**What it does NOT mean:** A single unified requirement — carrier and broker financial responsibility are governed by related but distinct rules with their own separate figures.
**Common misuse:** Conflating the carrier's insurance requirement with the broker's financial security requirement as if they were the same figure or the same rule.
**Operational example:** "Carrier financial responsibility and broker financial security are related concepts but governed by distinct rules — verify each separately for its applicable party."

### Unauthorized Brokerage Penalty
*(Established in Module 02, Section 19.)* The civil-penalty framework under 49 U.S.C. § 14916 for conducting brokerage without required authority. The current inflation-adjusted maximum dollar figure has already been revised more than once within a single year in this academy's own production history — treat any specific number as requiring reverification at the point of use, per Module 02's own Date-Sensitive Claim Register.

## Section 4: Safety & Compliance Records (Introductory Only)

### Safety Rating
**What it means:** FMCSA's formal assessment of a motor carrier's safety compliance, resulting in a rating category.
**What it does NOT mean:** A figure this academy's curriculum currently teaches in specific category-name detail — flagging this as an area requiring independent verification before any specific rating-category names or criteria are taught, since this has not yet received the same module-level sourced treatment as USDOT/authority/financial-security content has.
**Common misuse:** Assuming or asserting specific current safety-rating category names or thresholds without independent verification.
**Operational example:** "Safety rating status should be checked through an appropriate current FMCSA source — specific category criteria are not something to assert from memory."

### CSA (Compliance, Safety, Accountability) / SMS (Safety Measurement System)
**What it means:** FMCSA's data-driven program and scoring system for monitoring and prioritizing carrier safety compliance.
**What it does NOT mean:** A settled, static scoring system — flagging this as another area requiring independent verification before specific scoring mechanics are taught, since it has not yet received sourced module-level treatment in this curriculum.
**Common misuse:** Teaching or asserting specific current CSA/SMS scoring thresholds or category weights without independent verification against a current source.
**Operational example:** "CSA/SMS data can inform carrier vetting, but specific current scoring mechanics should be verified against an authoritative current source before being taught as fact."

### Out-of-Service Order (Entity-Level)
**What it means:** An FMCSA determination that an entire motor carrier is prohibited from operating, distinct from the vehicle-level Out of Service designation already covered in Equipment Terminology.
**What it does NOT mean:** The same thing as a single vehicle or driver being placed out of service — an entity-level order affects the whole carrier's ability to operate, a materially more serious status.
**Common misuse:** Confusing a single out-of-service vehicle with an entity-level out-of-service order against the whole carrier; the two are very different in scope and seriousness.
**Operational example:** "Verify whether an out-of-service condition applies to a specific vehicle or to the carrier's entire operating authority — these are very different situations."

## Section 5: Registration & Tax Reciprocity (Introductory Only)

### UCR (Unified Carrier Registration)
*(Established in Module 04.)* A federally mandated, state-administered annual registration and fee program based on fleet size for interstate operations — the motor carrier's own legal responsibility to file and pay; not automatically the dispatcher's filing duty.

### IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement)
**What it means:** An agreement among U.S. states and Canadian provinces simplifying fuel-tax reporting for carriers operating across multiple jurisdictions.
**What it does NOT mean:** A federal FMCSA program — IFTA is a separate fuel-tax reciprocity agreement, administered differently from FMCSA safety/registration requirements.
**Common misuse:** Confusing IFTA fuel-tax reporting obligations with FMCSA operating-authority or safety-compliance requirements; they are governed by different frameworks.
**Operational example:** "IFTA fuel-tax reporting is a separate carrier obligation from FMCSA operating authority — don't conflate the two when discussing carrier compliance."

### IRP (International Registration Plan) / Base Plate
*(Introduced in Carrier Terminology.)* A reciprocal vehicle-registration agreement among U.S. states and Canadian provinces allowing apportioned registration fees based on distance traveled in each jurisdiction — "base plate" refers informally to the vehicle registration obtained under this plan.
**What it does NOT mean:** The same thing as USDOT registration or operating authority — IRP is a vehicle-registration/tax-reciprocity framework, separate from FMCSA's safety-registration system.
**Common misuse:** Conflating IRP vehicle registration with USDOT number or operating-authority registration.
**Operational example:** "IRP registration and USDOT/operating-authority registration are separate systems serving different regulatory purposes."
