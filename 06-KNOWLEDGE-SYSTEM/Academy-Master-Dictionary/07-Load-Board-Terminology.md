## Section 1: What a Load Board Is (and Isn't)

### Load Board
*(Established in Module 05.)* A marketplace/tool where freight opportunities may be posted for authorized users to search — a sourcing lead source, not a government authority database and not proof of a load's current availability.

### Public / Free Board
**What it means:** A load board accessible without a paid subscription, typically with more limited features than a paid platform.
**What it does NOT mean:** A board with inherently lower or higher fraud risk than a paid board — this academy does not teach that paid boards universally carry less fraud risk, since that hasn't been established as a reliable rule (see Module 05's caution against unsupported platform generalizations).
**Common misuse:** Assuming a free board is inherently less trustworthy, or that a paid board's cost alone establishes trustworthiness.
**Operational example:** "This load was found on a free board — apply the same verification discipline as any other sourcing lead."

### Paid / Subscription Board
**What it means:** A load board requiring a paid subscription, typically offering more features, filters, and data than free alternatives.
**What it does NOT mean:** A guarantee of superior freight quality or lower risk — subscription cost affects available features, not the underlying verification discipline still required for any load found there.
**Common misuse:** Skipping standard verification steps because a load was sourced from a paid, seemingly more "legitimate" platform.
**Operational example:** "The paid board's additional filters make searching faster, but every load found still requires the same fit check and verification."

### Broker Portal
*(Established in Module 05.)* A freight-access environment associated with a particular broker or brokerage network — distinct from a general load board, and not a substitute for independent regulatory verification of that broker.

### Private Freight Network
**What it means:** A closed freight-matching system limited to a specific group of participants (e.g., a shipper's own approved carrier network), rather than open to the general public or all subscribers.
**What it does NOT mean:** Automatic membership or access — private networks typically require specific approval or invitation to participate.
**Common misuse:** Assuming access to one private network implies access to, or legitimacy within, an unrelated private network.
**Operational example:** "This shipper only tenders freight through its private carrier network — confirm membership status before expecting to see their loads."

## Section 2: Posting Information

### Load Posting
*(Established in Module 05/Broker Terminology.)* A broker's advertisement of available freight, typically including origin, destination, equipment type, and sometimes rate — a sourcing lead requiring further verification, not proof the load is currently available.

### Posting Age
*(Established in Module 05.)* How long a posting has been visible on a board — a priority signal worth noting, not proof of current availability or unavailability. There is no universal age threshold that determines whether a load is worth pursuing.

### KNOWN / MISSING / UNCLEAR / VERIFY
*(Established in Module 05's posting-field classification framework.)* A working classification system for evaluating what a posting actually tells you: KNOWN facts are stated directly, MISSING facts are absent entirely, UNCLEAR facts are ambiguously stated, and VERIFY marks anything requiring confirmation before relying on it — the discipline that prevents a dispatcher from assuming missing information rather than checking it.

### FOUND vs. VERIFIED AVAILABLE
*(Established in Module 05; already cross-referenced in Dispatch Terminology.)* FOUND means a potentially relevant load opportunity has been located; VERIFIED AVAILABLE means its availability has actually been confirmed — not the same status, and treating them as equivalent risks pursuing freight that's already covered.

### Equipment Label (Posting)
**What it means:** The equipment type stated on a load posting (e.g., "dry van," "reefer," "flatbed").
**What it does NOT mean:** Proof of actual physical or regulatory compatibility — a load-board equipment label does not by itself confirm dimension, weight, temperature, or commodity fit (see Module 03's core multi-factor fit-check discipline, which applies regardless of how confidently a posting is labeled).
**Common misuse:** Booking a load based solely on its posted equipment label without running the full fit check.
**Operational example:** "The posting says 'dry van,' but the full fit check — commodity, weight, dimensions — still needs to be verified before booking."

### Rate (Posted)
**What it means:** The rate figure shown on a load posting, whether a firm offer or a starting point for negotiation.
**What it does NOT mean:** A confirmed, final, or non-negotiable figure — posted rates can be negotiated, and the actual rate isn't final until reflected in a reviewed, signed rate confirmation (see Module 05's rate-confirmation review discipline).
**Common misuse:** Treating a posted rate as automatically the final rate without any negotiation or confirmation step.
**Operational example:** "The posted rate is a starting point — confirm the actual agreed rate in the rate confirmation before treating it as final."

### Contact Information (Posting)
**What it means:** The phone number, email, or messaging contact provided on a load posting for reaching the posting party.
**What it does NOT mean:** Verified identity — contact information on a posting is a starting point for outreach, not proof that whoever responds is who they claim to be (see the Entity Verification/Contact Verification distinction in Broker Terminology).
**Common misuse:** Treating a posting's listed contact information as sufficient identity verification on its own.
**Operational example:** "Use the posting's contact information to reach out, but still apply entity and contact verification before proceeding with an unfamiliar broker."

## Section 3: Search & Filtering

### Search Filter
**What it means:** A tool on a load board allowing a user to narrow results by criteria such as origin, destination, equipment type, or date.
**What it does NOT mean:** A guarantee that filtered results are all genuinely compatible — filters narrow the search, but each result still requires its own fit check and verification.
**Common misuse:** Assuming every result returned by a filtered search is automatically appropriate without further screening.
**Operational example:** "Filtering by equipment type and origin radius narrows the search, but each candidate still needs individual screening."

### Search Radius
*(Established in Module 05.)* The geographic distance from a specified point within which a search returns results — set by carrier policy, market conditions, driver availability, and repositioning plan, not a fixed universal number.

### Load Availability Changes
**What it means:** The reality that a load's status on a board can change between when it's viewed and when it's acted on — it may be covered, modified, or removed.
**What it does NOT mean:** A rare occurrence — availability changes are a normal, expected part of using any load board, which is exactly why FOUND and VERIFIED AVAILABLE remain distinct statuses.
**Common misuse:** Assuming a load seen minutes ago is still available without reconfirming.
**Operational example:** "Reconfirm availability before finalizing — board listings can change between when you first see them and when you're ready to book."

### Book-It-Now
**What it means:** A load-board feature on some platforms allowing a carrier to commit to a posted load immediately, at the posted rate, without a separate negotiation step.
**What it does NOT mean:** A universal feature available on every platform or every posting — this is a platform-specific feature that must not be assumed available without confirming it on the specific board and posting in question.
**Common misuse:** Assuming every posting supports immediate booking without checking whether that specific feature is actually offered.
**Operational example:** "This posting supports book-it-now on this platform — confirm the terms before using that feature, since it typically commits you at the posted rate."

## Section 4: Platform Features & Business Model (Introductory, Deliberately Non-Specific)

**Note:** This section deliberately avoids naming or describing specific commercial platforms' exact current features, since those change faster than curriculum revision cycles (see Module 05's own caution on this point). Where Module 05 names specific current platforms for illustrative purposes, this dictionary points to that module rather than repeating platform names here.

### Load-Board Account
**What it means:** A user's registered access credentials for a specific load-board platform.
**What it does NOT mean:** Something to be casually shared across multiple users without checking that specific platform's actual terms — whether credential sharing is permitted depends on that platform's current Terms of Service, which varies by platform and is not something this academy states as a universal rule (see Module 05's explicit caution against overgeneralizing platform TOS).
**Common misuse:** Assuming credential-sharing rules from one platform apply identically to a different platform.
**Operational example:** "Check this specific platform's current Terms of Service before assuming account-sharing practices from another board apply here."

### Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
**What it means:** A login security method requiring more than just a password — typically a password plus a second verification step (e.g., a code sent to a phone).
**What it does NOT mean:** A feature offered identically on every platform — MFA availability and requirements vary by platform.
**Common misuse:** Assuming MFA protection exists on an account without confirming it's actually enabled.
**Operational example:** "Enable MFA on the load-board account where the platform offers it, as a basic account-security practice."

### Broker Credit Display
**What it means:** A feature some load-board platforms offer showing a broker's commercial credit information alongside their postings.
**What it does NOT mean:** A government safety rating or a payment guarantee — commercial credit information is a private company's payment-reliability assessment, not connected to FMCSA authority status (see Broker Terminology's Financial Security entry and Module 05's credit/factoring distinctions).
**Common misuse:** Treating a platform's displayed credit score as equivalent to regulatory compliance verification.
**Operational example:** "The platform's credit display is one data point about payment reliability — it doesn't substitute for entity/authority verification."

### Load-Board Mileage
**What it means:** The distance figure a load board displays for a given posting.
**What it does NOT mean:** Necessarily the same as practical, drivable miles (see Trucking Terminology's Practical Miles vs. Shortest-Route Miles entry) — this academy does not assume board-calculated mileage is always straight-line or always practical without evidence about how a specific platform calculates it.
**Common misuse:** Using a board's displayed mileage figure directly for RPM calculations without confirming it reflects a realistic, drivable route.
**Operational example:** "Cross-check the board's displayed mileage against a practical routing tool before using it in the RPM calculation."
