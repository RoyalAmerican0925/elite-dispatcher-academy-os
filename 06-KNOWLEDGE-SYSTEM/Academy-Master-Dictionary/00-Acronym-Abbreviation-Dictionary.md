# Academy Master Dictionary — Acronym & Abbreviation Dictionary

**Status:** DRAFT_COMPLETE — QA required before PUBLICATION_READY
**Section:** Second top-level section of the Academy Master Dictionary, alongside the 13-branch Master Dictionary itself.
**Purpose:** A single alphabetical quick-reference for every acronym/abbreviation actually used across this academy's curriculum and the 13 terminology branches — not a generic industry acronym list padded with terms this project doesn't use.
**Format:** Acronym → Full name → One-line meaning → Source (where the full entry/definition lives) → "Not to be confused with" note where a genuine mix-up risk exists.
**IMPORTANT:** This file is a lookup index, not a replacement for the fuller entries. For the complete definition (including what a term does NOT mean, common misuse, and operational examples), follow the Source reference to the originating branch or module.

---

## A

**ASN** — Advance Ship Notice. An electronic notification sent to a receiver in advance of a shipment's arrival. *Source: Shipper/Receiver Terminology, §6.*

**ATRI** — American Transportation Research Institute. An industry research organization publishing operating-cost data; general context only, not a substitute for carrier-specific figures. *Source: Rate/Pricing Terminology; Module 07.*

## B

**BIPD** — Bodily Injury / Property Damage (insurance). Federally required public-liability insurance for for-hire motor carriers. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §3; Module 04.*

**BMC-84** — FMCSA form evidencing a broker's/freight forwarder's surety-bond financial security. *Source: Broker Terminology, §3; Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §3.*
**Not to be confused with:** BMC-85 (the trust-fund alternative to the same requirement).

**BMC-85** — FMCSA form evidencing a broker's/freight forwarder's trust-fund financial security. *Source: Broker Terminology, §3; Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §3.*
**Not to be confused with:** BMC-84 (the surety-bond alternative to the same requirement).

**BOL** — Bill of Lading. The core shipping document recording shipper, consignee, carrier, freight description, and terms of carriage. *Source: Documentation Terminology, §1.*

## C

**CDL** — Commercial Driver's License. Required to operate specified categories of commercial motor vehicles; class and endorsements must match the specific vehicle and cargo. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §4.*

**CDLIS** — Commercial Driver's License Information System. The federal system tied to CDL record-keeping, referenced in the context of medical-certification data transmission. *Source: Module 04 (NRII/medical-certification content).*

**CMV** — Commercial Motor Vehicle. The broader regulatory term covering commercial vehicles subject to applicable federal/state rules — covers both combination vehicles and straight trucks. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §4.*

**COI** — Certificate of Insurance. A document summarizing represented insurance information — not proof by itself of current operating authority or unchanged coverage. *Source: Carrier Terminology, §4; Module 04.*

**CSA** — Compliance, Safety, Accountability. FMCSA's data-driven carrier safety compliance program. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §4.* **⚠️ Flagged: specific scoring mechanics not yet independently verified in this curriculum — do not teach specific current criteria without independent verification.**

## D

**DEF** — Diesel Exhaust Fluid. A fluid required for certain diesel engines; referenced as one of the carrier costs excluded from Module 07's simplified contribution-margin calculations. *Source: Module 07, §10.*

**DOT** — U.S. Department of Transportation. The broad federal department responsible for transportation policy across all modes. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §1; Module 01.*
**Not to be confused with:** FMCSA (the specific administration within DOT that regulates motor-carrier safety; DOT is the umbrella, not a synonym).

**DQ File** — Driver Qualification File. The motor carrier's required driver-qualification and compliance record set under applicable law — carrier-owned, distinct from the dispatcher's own operational profile on a driver. *Source: Module 04; Dispatch Terminology cross-reference.*

## E

**EIA** — U.S. Energy Information Administration. The appropriate source for current on-highway diesel fuel price data; never hard-code a fixed fuel price. *Source: Rate/Pricing Terminology, §2; Module 07.*

**ELD** — Electronic Logging Device. The device recording a driver's duty status and hours electronically — the authoritative current record for available hours. *Source: TERMINOLOGY-LEDGER.md (controlled term); HOS Terminology, §2.*

**ETA** — Estimated Time of Arrival. A projected arrival time based on the best current information — an estimate to be updated as conditions change, not a binding guarantee. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §6.*

## F

**FCFS** — First Come, First Served. A facility scheduling approach where arrival order determines service order, within the facility's actual operating hours — not 24-hour access. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §6.*

**FMCSA** — Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. The DOT administration specifically regulating commercial-motor-vehicle safety, carrier and driver registration, and related oversight. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §1; Module 01.*

**FTL** — Full Truckload. A shipment large enough to fill (or be billed as filling) an entire trailer, moving directly without being combined with other shippers' freight. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §1.*
**Not to be confused with:** LTL (Less-Than-Truckload) or Partial.

## G

**GCWR** — Gross Combination Weight Rating. The manufacturer-rated maximum safe total weight for a tractor and trailer operating together. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §4.*

**GVW** — Gross Vehicle Weight. The actual current total weight of a vehicle/combination including cargo. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §4.*

**GVWR** — Gross Vehicle Weight Rating. The manufacturer's rated maximum safe operating weight for a given unit. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §4.*
**Not to be confused with:** GVW (the actual current weight, vs. GVWR's manufacturer rating) or the separate federal 80,000 lb legal roadway baseline (Equipment Terminology, §2), which is a different figure from any manufacturer rating.

## H

**H Endorsement** — CDL endorsement for hazardous materials generally. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §6; Equipment Terminology, §3; Module 03.*
**Not to be confused with:** N or X endorsements — see those entries below.

**HOS** — Hours of Service. The federal framework governing how many hours a commercial driver may drive and must rest. *Source: TERMINOLOGY-LEDGER.md (controlled term); HOS Terminology (entire branch, deliberately conservative — see that branch's own status note).*

## I

**IFTA** — International Fuel Tax Agreement. An agreement simplifying fuel-tax reporting for carriers operating across multiple states/provinces — separate from FMCSA safety/registration requirements. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §5.*

**IRP** — International Registration Plan. A reciprocal vehicle-registration agreement allowing apportioned fees based on distance traveled in each jurisdiction — separate from USDOT/operating-authority registration. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §5; Carrier Terminology.*

## L

**LPOA** — Limited Power of Attorney. A document granting specifically defined agency authority per its own written terms — scope-limited, not blanket signing authority. *Source: Carrier Terminology, §5; Module 02.*

**LTL** — Less-Than-Truckload. A shipment too small to require a full trailer, typically combined with other shippers' freight through a terminal network. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §1.*

## M

**MC (Number)** — Motor Carrier number. A specific type of operating-authority docket number historically associated with for-hire property carrier authority. *Source: Carrier Terminology, §3; Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §2.*
**Not to be confused with:** USDOT number — an MC number identifies operating authority (what a company may do); a USDOT number identifies the entity (who the company is). See Module 01's foundational USDOT-vs-authority distinction.

**MEC** — Medical Examiner's Certificate. The document certifying a driver's medical qualification, subject to the NRII electronic-transmission framework and its temporary exemption provisions. *Source: Module 04.*

**MFA** — Multi-Factor Authentication. A login security method requiring more than a password — availability varies by platform. *Source: Load-Board Terminology, §4.*

**MPG** — Miles Per Gallon. A truck's fuel efficiency figure, used in estimated-fuel-cost calculations — varies by truck, load, and conditions; use the specific unit's actual figure, not a generic assumption. *Source: Rate/Pricing Terminology, §2; Module 07.*

## N

**N Endorsement** — CDL endorsement for operating a tank vehicle — about vehicle configuration, not about the material being hazardous. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §6; Equipment Terminology, §3; Module 03.*
**Not to be confused with:** H (hazmat generally) or X (the combined N+H requirement).

**NMFC** — National Motor Freight Classification. A standardized freight-classification system used primarily in LTL pricing — an industry pricing system, not a federal safety classification. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §1.*
**Not to be confused with:** Hazmat hazard class, a completely different, safety-driven classification system.

**NOA** — Notice of Assignment. A UCC-governed document legally transferring the right to payment from the carrier to a factoring company. *Source: Billing/Factoring Terminology, §2; Module 04.*

**NRII** — National Registry II. The FMCSA framework for secure electronic transmission of medical-examiner certification data — subject to a temporary paper-MEC exemption that is explicitly flagged as date-sensitive in Module 04's own register. *Source: Module 04.*

## O

**OOS** — Out of Service. A designation indicating a vehicle, driver, or entire carrier has been determined unfit to continue operating until a specific deficiency is corrected. *Source: Equipment Terminology, §5 (vehicle-level); Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §4 (entity-level — a materially more serious, distinct status).*

**OTR** — Over-the-Road. Long-haul trucking operations covering multiple states, requiring extended time away from home. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §3.*
**Not to be confused with:** Regional or Local/Short-Haul, which describe smaller operating scopes.

## P

**PHMSA** — Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration. The DOT agency responsible for hazardous-materials transportation regulation, including shipper classification responsibility. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §1; Module 03/06.*

**PM** — Preventive Maintenance. Scheduled maintenance performed regularly rather than only in response to breakdowns — not a substitute for a driver's own pre-trip inspection. *Source: Equipment Terminology, §5.*

**POD** — Proof of Delivery. Documentation confirming freight was delivered, typically including a signature. *Source: TERMINOLOGY-LEDGER.md (controlled term); Shipper/Receiver Terminology, §5.*

**PPE** — Personal Protective Equipment. Safety gear (e.g., hard hat, vest) some facilities require for dock/yard access. *Source: Shipper/Receiver Terminology, §2 (Facility Requirements).*

## R

**RPM** — Rate Per Mile. See Loaded RPM and Effective RPM — two distinct calculations that must not be conflated. *Source: TERMINOLOGY-LEDGER.md (controlled term); Rate/Pricing Terminology, §1.*
**Not to be confused with:** Loaded RPM (revenue ÷ loaded miles only) vs. Effective RPM (revenue ÷ total trip miles including deadhead) — the whole point of Module 07's "loaded mile blindness" lesson is that these two numbers can differ significantly.

## S

**SAFER** — Safety and Fitness Electronic Records. A public FMCSA snapshot tool for supplemental safety/entity information — not a complete substitute for current registration/authority/insurance verification. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §2; Module 01/04/05.*

**SDLA** — State Driver Licensing Agency. The state-level agency medical-certification data is electronically transmitted to under the NRII framework. *Source: Module 04.*

**SMS** — Safety Measurement System. FMCSA's scoring system underlying the CSA program. *Source: Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §4.* **⚠️ Flagged: specific scoring mechanics not yet independently verified in this curriculum.**

## T

**TONU** — Truck Ordered Not Used. See the controlled Ledger entry — amount and eligibility are agreement-specific, never assumed. *Source: TERMINOLOGY-LEDGER.md (controlled term).*

**TWIC** — Transportation Worker Identification Credential. A credential some port and secure-facility environments require for access — not a universal requirement at every facility. *Source: Shipper/Receiver Terminology, §4.*

## U

**UCC** — Uniform Commercial Code. The state-law framework governing NOAs and similar commercial assignment documents. *Source: Billing/Factoring Terminology, §2.*

**UCR** — Unified Carrier Registration. A federally mandated, state-administered annual registration/fee program based on fleet size — the carrier's own responsibility, not automatically the dispatcher's filing duty. *Source: Carrier Terminology, §3; Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §5; Module 04.*

**USDOT (Number)** — An identifier used by FMCSA to identify and track a regulated entity — identifies who the company is, not what it's authorized to do. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §4; Compliance/Regulatory Terminology, §2; Module 01 (foundational distinction).*
**Not to be confused with:** MC number (operating authority — what the company may do) or DOT (the broad federal department, not an entity identifier at all).

## W

**W-9** — IRS tax-identification/certification form used in business payment setup. A name difference from FMCSA-registered information is commonly explainable (disregarded-entity structures) and not, by itself, evidence of fraud. *Source: Carrier Terminology, §3; Module 04.*

## X

**X Endorsement** — The combined tank-vehicle-plus-hazardous-materials CDL qualification. *Source: Trucking Terminology, §6; Equipment Terminology, §3; Module 03.*
**Not to be confused with:** H alone (hazmat generally) — a driver holding only H does not satisfy a load requiring X, which needs both the N (tank) and H (hazmat) elements together.

## Numbers

**3PL** — Third-Party Logistics. Broad industry shorthand for outsourced logistics providers — covers companies with very different actual legal roles (broker, freight forwarder, warehouse operator); the label alone doesn't establish which. *Source: Broker Terminology, §1.*

## Index Notes

This dictionary intentionally excludes acronyms that are generic to business writing broadly (e.g., FAQ, N/A) or that don't actually appear anywhere in this academy's built curriculum or the 13 terminology branches — the goal is a working reference for this specific project, not an exhaustive general logistics-industry acronym list padded beyond what's actually used here.

Two acronyms in this file (**CSA**, **SMS**) carry an explicit verification flag inherited from Compliance/Regulatory Terminology — their specific current scoring mechanics have not yet received the sourced, verified treatment the rest of this academy's regulatory content has. Do not teach specific current criteria for either without independent verification first.
