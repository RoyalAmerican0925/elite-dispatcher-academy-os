## Section 1: Invoicing Basics

### Invoice
**What it means:** The carrier's formal request for payment on a completed load, typically submitted with supporting documentation (POD, rate confirmation).
**What it does NOT mean:** A request that's automatically paid immediately upon submission — payment timing depends on the applicable payment terms.
**Common misuse:** Submitting an invoice without the required supporting documentation, delaying payment processing.
**Operational example:** "Submit the invoice with the signed POD and rate confirmation attached — missing documentation is a common cause of payment delay."

### Payment Terms
**What it means:** The agreed timeframe within which a broker or shipper will pay a carrier's invoice (e.g., "net 30" meaning payment within 30 days of invoice submission).
**What it does NOT mean:** A universal standard across every broker or shipper — payment terms vary by relationship and agreement, and should be confirmed rather than assumed.
**Common misuse:** Assuming standard payment terms without confirming the actual terms for a specific broker relationship.
**Operational example:** "Confirm this broker's actual payment terms before assuming a standard net-30 timeline applies."

### Net Terms (e.g., Net 30)
**What it means:** A payment term specifying the number of days after invoice submission within which payment is due.
**What it does NOT mean:** A guarantee of payment exactly on that day — net terms establish an expected timeframe, not an absolute guarantee independent of the paying party's actual practices.
**Common misuse:** Treating a stated net-terms figure as a legal guarantee rather than the agreed expected timeframe it actually represents.
**Operational example:** "This broker's standard terms are net 30 — factor that into cash-flow planning for this load."

### Remittance
**What it means:** The actual payment sent in satisfaction of an invoice, along with the information identifying which invoice(s) it covers.
**What it does NOT mean:** The same thing as the invoice itself — remittance is the payment and its accompanying identifying information, occurring after the invoice has been submitted and processed.
**Common misuse:** Confusing remittance information with invoice submission; they occur at different points in the payment process.
**Operational example:** "Confirm the remittance details match the expected invoice amount before reconciling as paid."

## Section 2: Factoring

### Factoring / Factoring Company
*(Established in Module 01, 05, and 07.)* A company that purchases a carrier's unpaid invoices at a discount, paying the carrier faster than the carrier's customer would otherwise pay — addresses cash-flow needs since freight payment terms often extend well beyond the time it takes a carrier to run another load.

### NOA (Notice of Assignment)
*(Established in Module 04.)* A UCC-governed document legally transferring the right to payment from the carrier to the factoring company. If a carrier requests payment to a new account bypassing an active NOA, the correct dispatcher response is to STOP and resolve the conflict through an out-of-band callback — not to offer legal advice on receivable ownership.

### Recourse Factoring
**What it means:** A factoring arrangement where, if the broker/shipper ultimately fails to pay the invoice, the factoring company can require the carrier to buy back or repay the advanced amount.
**What it does NOT mean:** A guarantee-free arrangement for the carrier — under recourse factoring, the carrier retains some financial risk if the underlying invoice ultimately goes unpaid.
**Common misuse:** Assuming factoring approval eliminates all financial risk for the carrier without checking whether the specific arrangement is recourse or non-recourse (see Module 05's caution against this exact oversimplification).
**Operational example:** "Confirm whether this factoring arrangement is recourse or non-recourse — that materially affects who bears the risk if the broker doesn't pay."

### Non-Recourse Factoring
**What it means:** A factoring arrangement where the factoring company absorbs the loss if the broker/shipper ultimately fails to pay, without requiring the carrier to repay the advance.
**What it does NOT mean:** Protection against every possible loss scenario — non-recourse factoring typically still excludes certain circumstances (e.g., disputes over the underlying freight/service itself), and specific contract terms control what's actually covered.
**Common misuse:** Assuming non-recourse factoring provides unconditional protection in every circumstance without reading the specific contract's actual exclusions.
**Operational example:** "This is a non-recourse arrangement, but confirm the contract's specific exclusions before assuming complete protection from every risk."

### Factoring Approval
*(Established in Module 05 and 07.)* A factoring company's decision to purchase a specific carrier's invoice from a specific broker relationship — a private financial-risk decision, not a legal certification of the broker's legitimacy, and not proof the broker is free of any concern.

### Factoring Decline
*(Established in Module 05 and 06.)* A factor's decision not to purchase a specific invoice — reflects that factor's own financial-risk assessment; it is not proof of criminal conduct by the broker, and should not be treated as an automatic red flag rather than one data point among several.

### Quick-Pay
*(Established in Module 05 and 07.)* A fee some brokers or factors charge for expedited payment, distinct from standard factoring — a different tool solving the same cash-flow problem, with its own applicable percentage that must come from the actual terms in use, not an assumed figure.

## Section 3: Accessorial & Reimbursement Billing

### Accessorial Billing
**What it means:** The process of invoicing for accessorial charges (detention, layover, TONU, lumper reimbursement) in addition to the base linehaul revenue.
**What it does NOT mean:** An automatic add-on to every invoice — every accessorial charge requires its own documentation and verification against the applicable agreement before being billed (see Module 07's accessorial-verification discipline, which applies equally at the billing stage).
**Common misuse:** Billing an assumed accessorial amount without the documentation required to support the claim.
**Operational example:** "Attach the detention documentation before billing that accessorial charge — an undocumented claim is more likely to be disputed or delayed."

### Lumper Reimbursement (Billing)
*(Established in Shipper/Receiver Terminology.)* Repayment to the carrier for a lumper fee paid at loading/unloading — whether reimbursable, and whether excluded from dispatcher commission, depends on the specific carrier agreement; a receipt is needed to support the claim.

### Detention Pay
*(Cross-referencing the controlled Detention entry.)* Payment for time held at a facility beyond agreed free time — not an automatic entitlement; whether it applies, and at what rate, depends entirely on the specific applicable agreement and supporting documentation.

### Per Diem (Equipment)
**What it means:** A daily charge, commonly associated with trailer or chassis usage beyond an agreed free period (e.g., in intermodal/drayage contexts, or carrier-to-carrier trailer interchange).
**What it does NOT mean:** A universal daily rate applied identically across every equipment relationship — per diem terms and rates are specific to the applicable agreement (e.g., a trailer interchange agreement, or an intermodal carrier's specific terms).
**Common misuse:** Assuming a specific per diem rate or free-time period without confirming the actual applicable agreement.
**Operational example:** "Confirm the applicable per diem terms in the interchange agreement before assuming a specific daily rate for extended chassis use."

## Section 4: Payment Risk & Verification (Cross-Reference)

### Payment Redirection
*(Already established in Broker Terminology.)* A fraud attempt where a bad actor requests that payment or remittance instructions be changed — the correct response is always STOP and verify through a previously trusted, out-of-band channel before acting.

### Broker Financial Security
*(Already established in Compliance/Regulatory and Broker Terminology.)* A broker's required $75,000-baseline financial responsibility — one verification layer, not a payment guarantee for any specific transaction. See the citing modules' own Date-Sensitive Claim Registers for current figures rather than repeating them here.

### Commercial Credit Information
*(Established in Module 05.)* A private company's assessment of a broker's payment reliability/history — not a government safety rating and not connected to FMCSA authority status; a broker's good commercial credit reputation doesn't establish federal compliance, and federal compliance doesn't guarantee reliable payment.
