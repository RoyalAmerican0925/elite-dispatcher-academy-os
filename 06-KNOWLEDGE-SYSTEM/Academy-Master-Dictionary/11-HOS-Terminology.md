## Section 1: Core HOS Concepts (Framework Only)

### HOS (Hours of Service)
*(Already a controlled term — see `TERMINOLOGY-LEDGER.md`.)* The federal framework governing how many hours a commercial driver may drive and must rest, before and between duty periods.

### Duty Status
**What it means:** A driver's current classification at any given moment — broadly, categories distinguishing actively driving, on-duty but not driving, sleeper berth, and off-duty time.
**What it does NOT mean:** A status a dispatcher can assign or override — duty status reflects the driver's actual activity and is the driver's own record to maintain accurately, not something dispatch decides on the driver's behalf.
**Common misuse:** A dispatcher instructing or pressuring a driver to log a duty status that doesn't reflect their actual activity.
**Operational example:** "Verify the driver's current duty status and remaining available hours before committing to a delivery window — don't assume based on when the trip started."

### Available Hours
**What it means:** The amount of driving and/or on-duty time a driver has remaining before required rest, based on their actual current HOS record.
**What it does NOT mean:** A figure a dispatcher can estimate from mileage and elapsed clock time alone — Module 05 explicitly teaches that a statement like "600 miles in 12 hours is automatically a violation" is too simplistic; actual available hours must be verified from the driver's real current record, not calculated backward from distance.
**Common misuse:** Committing to a delivery schedule based on an assumed available-hours figure rather than the driver's actual current status.
**Operational example:** "Verify the driver's actual available hours from their current record before committing to this delivery window — don't back-calculate from mileage alone."

### Required Rest / Off-Duty Period
**What it means:** The rest time a driver must take before resuming driving, as required by applicable HOS rules.
**What it does NOT mean:** A fixed, universally identical duration in every circumstance — this academy has not yet independently verified and taught the specific current duration requirements and exceptions, and this file does not assert one.
**Common misuse:** Assuming a specific rest-period duration from general industry familiarity rather than the driver's own current, verified requirement.
**Operational example:** "Confirm the driver's actual required rest period from their own current record rather than assuming a standard figure."

### HOS Feasibility Verification
*(Established in Module 05, Section 29.)* The correct beginner-level dispatcher response to questionable trip timing: verify the driver's actual available hours, duty status, and route/appointment feasibility before committing to a schedule — rather than concluding automatic legality or automatic violation from mileage and elapsed time alone.

## Section 2: Related Equipment & Records (Cross-Reference)

### ELD (Electronic Logging Device)
*(Already a controlled term — see `TERMINOLOGY-LEDGER.md`.)* The device recording a driver's duty status and hours electronically — the authoritative current record a dispatcher should reference for a driver's actual available hours, rather than an estimate.

### ELD Check
**What it means:** The practice of confirming a driver's current available hours and duty status through their actual ELD record, rather than assumption.
**What it does NOT mean:** A one-time check that stays valid for the rest of a multi-day trip — available hours change continuously as a driver drives and rests, so an ELD check reflects a specific moment, not an ongoing guarantee.
**Common misuse:** Relying on an ELD check from earlier in a trip to make a scheduling decision later in that same trip, without a fresh check.
**Operational example:** "Run a fresh ELD check before committing to this delivery window, rather than relying on this morning's hours figure."

### Sleeper Berth
**What it means:** A truck's sleeping compartment, used by a driver for required rest, and referenced in certain HOS provisions allowing rest time to be split under specific conditions.
**What it does NOT mean:** A feature this academy currently teaches the specific split-rest provision rules for — flagging the specific sleeper-berth provision mechanics as not yet independently verified and taught in this curriculum, consistent with this file's overall conservative approach to HOS specifics.
**Common misuse:** Asserting specific sleeper-berth split-rest rules from general industry familiarity rather than a verified current source.
**Operational example:** "Confirm the driver's specific sleeper-berth usage and its effect on their available hours through their own current record, rather than assuming general split-rest rules apply without verification."

## Section 3: Operational Coordination (Dispatch-Side)

### Home Time
*(Already established in Dispatch Terminology.)* A driver's scheduled or expected time away from the road, spent at home — a key planning factor when sourcing and assigning loads, distinct from HOS legality itself.

### Relay
*(Already established in Dispatch Terminology.)* A method of moving a load using more than one driver in sequence — one operational way to keep a load moving within HOS constraints without requiring one driver to exceed their available hours.

### Team Driver / Solo Driver
*(Already established in Trucking Terminology.)* A team operation uses two drivers alternating so the truck can keep moving with minimal downtime; a solo operation's schedule is entirely governed by one driver's own available hours. Team driving does not eliminate HOS requirements — each driver individually remains subject to applicable rules; only the truck's overall downtime is reduced.

### Trip Planning (HOS Context)
*(Already established in Dispatch Terminology; expanded here.)* Mapping a load's route and timing with the driver's actual current and projected available hours as a hard input — not an afterthought checked only if timing looks obviously tight. The discipline Module 05 teaches (verify, don't calculate from mileage alone) applies at the planning stage, not just when a problem is already suspected.

## What's Deliberately NOT in This File

This is the shortest and most conservative branch in the entire dictionary, and that's a direct reflection of curriculum status rather than an oversight. The following are explicitly NOT covered here because this academy has not yet built and independently verified the underlying content:

- Specific current hour limits (the 11-hour driving limit, 14-hour on-duty window, or any other specific figure)
- The 30-minute break requirement and its specific triggering conditions
- Sleeper-berth split-rest provision specifics
- Adverse driving conditions exceptions
- Short-haul HOS exemption criteria
- 34-hour restart provisions
- ELD malfunction/exception procedures
- Any other specific current HOS figure or exception

**Recommendation:** Before this branch can be considered complete, the same kind of dedicated, sourced module-level treatment Module 03 gave equipment or Module 04 gave onboarding needs to happen for HOS specifically — likely as its own curriculum module (already anticipated in the original 15-module sequence) with its own regulatory verification pass and Date-Sensitive Claim Register, before this dictionary branch can responsibly expand beyond the conceptual framework covered above.
