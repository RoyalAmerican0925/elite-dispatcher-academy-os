## Core Principle

Polish must never hide weak evidence.

Use this order:

**verified fact → operational meaning → learning objective → instruction → practice → assessment → remediation → integration → approval gate**

If the evidence is weak, stop the claim from becoming stronger through wording.

## Student-Facing Cleanliness Standard

Student-facing files must not contain:

- chat transcripts;
- AI prompts;
- email-like handoffs between agents;
- internal author instructions;
- raw research notes;
- hidden reasoning;
- debugging residue;
- Zapier/Make instructions;
- TODO or TBD placeholders;
- unapproved metadata;
- duplicate draft sections;
- internal production status language.

Preserve valuable structured information outside the student edition rather than deleting it when it supports future software, analytics, simulations, or automation.

## Curriculum Review Questions

For every lesson/module ask:

1. What must the learner know?
2. What must the learner be able to do?
3. What prior knowledge does this depend on?
4. What mistakes are predictable?
5. What practice proves competency?
6. What assessment evidence is sufficient?
7. What happens if the student fails?
8. Is the language professional and beginner-usable?
9. Are facts actually verified?
10. Does this integrate with the rest of EAOS?
