## Executive Summary
This research package provides the foundational regulatory definitions and role distinctions required for Module 01 of the Elite Dispatcher Academy. The primary objective is to establish a clear legal boundary between a **Dispatcher** (an agent of the carrier) and a **Broker** (an intermediary requiring FMCSA authority). 

The most critical finding is that while "Dispatcher" is not a federally licensed role, the *actions* taken by an individual can inadvertently trigger "Broker" status under 49 CFR Part 371, which carries significant legal and financial requirements (e.g., BMC-84 bond). This document distinguishes between established federal definitions and common industry misconceptions that pose legal risks to students.

## Key Findings

### 1. Verified FMCSA/DOT Definitions & Terminology
*Based on 49 CFR and FMCSA official documentation.*

| Term | Verified Definition | Regulatory Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Motor Carrier** | A person or entity that provides transportation services for hire. | Requires Operating Authority (MC Number) and USDOT Number. |
| **Broker** | A person/entity that "arranges, or holds itself out as arranging, for the transportation of property by a motor carrier." | **Crucial:** Must have FMCSA Broker Authority and a surety bond (BMC-84/85). |
| **Freight Forwarder** | A person/entity that "arranges for the transportation of property... and takes possession of the property." | Often involves taking legal responsibility for the cargo during transit. |
| **Shipper** | The person or entity that initiates the shipment and relinquishes possession of the goods. | The origin point of the freight. |
| **Consignee** | The person or entity to whom the freight is being shipped (the receiver). | The destination point of the freight. |
| **Driver** | The individual operating the commercial motor vehicle (CMV). | Subject to Hours of Service (HOS) and ELD mandates. |
| **Dispatcher** | An individual or entity that coordinates the movement of freight on behalf of a motor carrier. | **Note:** Not a federally licensed role, but must act as an *agent* of the carrier to avoid broker status. |

### 2. Role Distinctions & The "Regulatory Line"
The distinction between a Dispatcher and a Broker is determined by **agency** and **control of funds**.

*   **The Dispatcher (Agent Model):** Acts on behalf of the Motor Carrier. They negotiate loads, communicate with brokers, and manage paperwork *for the carrier*. The carrier maintains control over the freight and the contract.
*   **The Broker (Intermediary Model):** Acts as a middleman between the Shipper and the Carrier. They "sell" the carrier's capacity to the shipper. If an individual negotiates a rate between a shipper and a carrier and takes a spread (margin) without being an agent of the carrier, they are legally acting as a broker.

### 3. VERIFIED FACTS vs. UNRESOLVED / DO NOT TEACH AS FACT

#### ✅ VERIFIED FACTS (Safe for Curriculum)
*   **Broker Authority Requirement:** Any entity acting as a broker must possess FMCSA Broker Authority and maintain a bond (typically $75,000).
*   **Dispatcher Agency:** A dispatcher is legally safe when they act as an authorized agent of the motor carrier, meaning the carrier is the one entering into the contract with the broker/shipper.
*   **Payment Flow:** In a standard dispatch model, the carrier is paid by the broker, and the dispatcher is paid a fee by the carrier.
*   **No Federal "Dispatcher License":** There is no federal requirement for a "Dispatcher License" from the DOT, provided the individual is acting as an agent of a carrier.

#### ❌ UNRESOLVED / DO NOT TEACH AS FACT (High Risk)
*   **"Dispatchers don't need any authority":** **DO NOT TEACH THIS AS A UNIVERSAL TRUTH.** While true for agents, if a student's business model involves negotiating directly with shippers to "find trucks," they are acting as a broker and *do* need authority.
*   **"You can take a percentage of the load from the broker":** **DO NOT TEACH THIS.** This is a major red flag. If a dispatcher receives money directly from a broker for a load, they are likely violating broker regulations or the carrier's contract. Payment should come from the carrier.
*   **"Dispatching is a legal loophole to avoid being a broker":** **DO NOT TEACH THIS.** This implies intent to circumvent the law. Instead, teach "The Legal Distinction of Agency."

## Gaps or Areas for Clarification

*   **State-Level Regulations:** While FMCSA governs interstate commerce, some states may have specific nuances regarding "Freight Brokerage" vs. "Transportation Management" services. Research into specific state-level "Transportation Agent" laws is recommended if the academy targets specific regional markets.
*   **Contractual Nuance:** The exact wording of a "Dispatcher Service Agreement" is what legally protects a dispatcher. While the DOT defines the roles, the *contract* defines the relationship. The academy should emphasize that a dispatcher's legal safety is tied to their contract with the carrier.
