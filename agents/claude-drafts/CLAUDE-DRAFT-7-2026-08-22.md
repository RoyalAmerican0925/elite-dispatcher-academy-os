# Dispatcher vs. Broker: Know the Regulatory Boundary

---

## Why This Distinction Matters

One of the most important things you will learn as a freight dispatcher is where your legal authority begins and ends. The federal government draws a clear line between carriers, dispatchers (also called bona fide agents), and brokers. Understanding that line is not just good business practice — it is a compliance requirement. Operating on the wrong side of that line without the proper authority can expose you and your clients to serious regulatory consequences.

This section walks you through the definitions, the key differences, and real-world scenarios so you can recognize where you stand.

---

## The Regulatory Foundation

The starting point for understanding these roles is **49 CFR § 371.2**, the federal regulation that contains the official definitions of a broker and a bona fide agent. The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) is the agency responsible for enforcing these rules.

In **June 2023**, FMCSA issued final guidance that clarified the definitions of broker and bona fide agent, specifically addressing how dispatch-service activities fit into these categories. This guidance was significant for the dispatching industry because it confirmed that the label someone uses to describe their work does not automatically determine their regulatory status. What matters is the nature of the activity being performed.

---

## Defining the Three Roles

**Carrier**
A motor carrier is a company or individual that physically transports freight using its own equipment and drivers. The carrier holds operating authority and takes on legal responsibility for the cargo during transit.

**Dispatcher / Bona Fide Agent**
A dispatcher who operates as a bona fide agent works on behalf of a carrier, not independently between shippers and carriers. A bona fide agent acts as a representative of the carrier and does not arrange transportation as an independent intermediary. The key factor is the agency relationship — the dispatcher is acting for the carrier, not standing between the shipper and carrier as a separate party arranging the deal.

**Broker**
A property broker arranges transportation of freight for compensation by connecting shippers with carriers. A broker operates as an independent intermediary. To operate legally as a broker, a person or company must obtain **FMCSA broker authority** and maintain **$75,000 in financial security** through either a **BMC-84** (surety bond) or a **BMC-85** (trust fund agreement).

---

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Role | Works On Behalf Of | Arranges Loads Independently | FMCSA Broker Authority Required | Financial Security Required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Carrier** | Itself | N/A — moves freight directly | No | No (for brokerage) |
| **Dispatcher / Bona Fide Agent** | The carrier | No — acts as carrier's agent | No, if operating as true agent | No, if operating as true agent |
| **Broker** | Neither party exclusively | Yes — independent intermediary | Yes | Yes — $75,000 via BMC-84 or BMC-85 |

---

## ⚠️ Caution

> **The title "dispatcher" does not automatically exempt anyone from broker registration requirements.**
>
> FMCSA's June 2023 guidance made clear that the nature of the activity — not the job title — determines whether broker authority is required. If you are independently arranging transportation between shippers and carriers for compensation, and you are not acting as a true agent of the carrier, you may be functioning as a broker under federal regulation regardless of what you call yourself. Dispatching is not a loophole around broker registration. If your business model raises questions about which category applies to you, consult a qualified transportation attorney before proceeding.

## Three Scenarios: Applying the Concepts

**Scenario 1 — The Clear Dispatcher**
Maria works exclusively with one owner-operator, James. She finds loads, communicates with shippers, handles paperwork, and negotiates rates — all on James's behalf and under his authority. She does not work with any other carriers and does not hold herself out to shippers as an independent service. Maria is functioning as a bona fide agent of James's carrier operation. She is not required to hold broker authority under this arrangement.
